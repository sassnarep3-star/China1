Jurisdiction: China

The framework, article by article

Seven instruments decide your question. Only one of them is recent, and it is the one everyone cites incorrectly.

1 · Classification: you are 境外个人, and that inverts everything

PBoC Order [2006] No.3, Art.37 defines the two personal categories:

  • 境内个人 — a Chinese citizen holding a PRC resident identity card, military ID or armed-police ID.
  • 境外个人 — a foreign national (including stateless persons) holding a passport, or a Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan resident holding the relevant travel permit.

Nationality decides, not residence. A US citizen living in Shanghai is still 境外个人. SAFE's Xiamen guide adds that holders of a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID or a Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan residence permit, and Chinese passport holders with foreign permanent residence, are each handled by reference to the overseas-individual rules.

The single most consequential consequence 汇发〔2020〕14号 Art.54: the USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota covers everyone's 结汇 (RMB out of FX) but covers 购汇 (FX out of RMB) for 境内个人 only.

Art.59: an overseas individual converts China-source current-account income on documents, and "overseas individuals holding a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID are subject to the annual purchase facilitation quota."

So: no quota for you on the purchase side — but also no ceiling. Confirmed independently by SAFE Tianjin (2025) and SAFE Xiamen.

2 · The USD 50,000 figure is not a ceiling — for anyone

This is the most widely repeated error in the field, and it is an error in two directions at once.

Direction 1 — it is a threshold, not a limit. SAFE's own regional Q&A states plainly that the annual facilitation quota "is not a limit on an individual's annual FX purchase or settlement; it only means that amounts above the equivalent of USD 50,000 cannot be handled on an identity document alone." Above it, you bring transaction evidence. The volume is not capped.

Direction 2 — for you it does not exist. Art.59 gives the purchase quota to overseas individuals only where they hold a permanent residence ID.

Reframe The problem was stated as "a cap I keep hitting". It is actually "no cap, but a proof requirement I must satisfy." That is a much better problem, and it is why the answer is a documentation exercise rather than a tax-avoidance trick.

4 · The emigrant-transfer limitation nobody mentions

Art.2 of PBoC Announcement [2004] No.16 defines 移民转移 as the remittance of "lawful property owned in China before obtaining the emigration status", realised into cash.

Three things follow, and all three are frequently omitted from summaries:

  1. Post-emigration savings do not qualify. Money earned in China after you became a foreign national goes through the ordinary current-account route, not 移民转移.
  2. The property must actually be sold. It is a realisation mechanism, not a transfer mechanism. You cannot move a flat you still own.
  3. The hukou must be cancelled, evidenced by a public-security certificate plus consular certification of your foreign settlement. That is a one-way door with consequences far beyond tax.
Timing If you still hold Chinese household registration, the cheapest moment to move a pre-emigration balance is before you give it up — and the 移民转移 window is only open if you do. Sequence this deliberately rather than discovering it afterwards. Get coordinated Chinese and US advice; this is the one genuinely one-way decision in the analysis.

6 · Three dead ends worth naming

Transferring to a Chinese relative

Art.67: domestic FX transfers are confined to your own accounts and close relatives — but where the two accounts belong to a 境内个人 and a 境外个人 respectively, the transfer is treated as a cross-border transaction. It does not escape the rules; it has to satisfy the same outward-payment test. And if the arrangement is designed to consume their quota, it becomes 分拆 under Art.62.

Re-converting an earlier exchange

Art.60: unused RMB from a prior exchange can be converted back within 24 months against the original exchange slip. This reverses your own purchase. It creates no new capacity and does nothing for RMB you earned or saved.

Investing offshore to move money out

QDII-type products, Stock Connect and Bond Connect are investment channels: redemptions normally return to your onshore RMB account. They do not repatriate anything. Cross-boundary Wealth Management Connect is restricted to Greater Bay Area residents. No operative rule for an individual outbound direct investment scheme was located at all — see the Flagged page rather than assuming one exists.

8 · What changed in 2026

Crypto: the rule the internet cites is repealed

银发〔2021〕237号 — the "924 notice" — was repealed on 2026-02-06 by 银发〔2026〕42号. The replacement keeps virtual-currency business activity an illegal financial activity, bars institutions and payment providers from servicing it, voids offending individual civil acts, and adds RWA-tokenisation and RMB-stablecoin prohibitions.

Remittances: a much lower identity threshold

PBoC Order [2025] No.11, effective 2026-01-01: for any outbound remittance of RMB 5,000 or USD 1,000 equivalent, the institution must verify the remitter's identity accurately; remitter name, account, address and payee details must travel with the payment; records kept 10 years.

This is a data-quality gate, not a cap. It does make name/address mismatches between your Chinese account and your US bank a leading cause of rejection.
