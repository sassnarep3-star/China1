Getting money out of China as a US citizen

What it costs to turn yuan in a Chinese bank account into dollars in a US account, which routes are lawful, and where tax really bites.

The short answer

  1. Converting and wiring the money is not what gets taxed. China has no tax item for exchanging currency. The US taxes only a currency gain above $200 if the yuan rose in dollar terms while you held it. What the Chinese bank asks for is proof that tax on the underlying income was already dealt with — that is the "tax" people run into. S14 S03 S04 S27
  2. If the money is documented and already taxed, the cheapest lawful route costs about $68.49 on $10,000 and $915.90 on $400,000 (0.685% falling to 0.229%): buy dollars at your Chinese bank and wire them to your own US account. S09 S10 S31
  3. If Chinese tax was never paid on the income, that tax is the real cost: 20% for dividends, property gains and similar; 3%–45% for wages; plus 0.05% per day late, and fines of 50% to 5 times the tax where it counts as evasion. S14 S16
  4. Shortcuts cost more than the tax. Underground banks, borrowed quotas, false papers and crypto carry fines up to 30% of the amount (up to 100% if serious) and criminal exposure. S01 S21 S20
  5. On the US side, report rather than pay: FBAR above $10,000 in foreign accounts, Form 8938 above its thresholds, Form 3520 if the money is a gift or inheritance above $100,000. S23 S24

Why the money looks stuck

For a foreign passport holder the bank does not apply the USD 50,000 yearly quota that Chinese citizens have. Instead it must see where each yuan came from and that tax was handled. With papers, there is no fixed ceiling. Without papers, the bank must refuse — and the missing paper is usually the tax certificate. S03 S04 S01
