Executive summary

Best available answer: if the balance is lawful, documented, after-tax current-account RMB income of a U.S. passport holder, use SAFE's overseas-individual current-income purchase/remittance process (P01), then compare executable BOC, HSBC and eligible Wise quotes. If it is not current income, do not relabel it: use the matching property, inheritance, insurance or business route.

Recommended sequence

  1. Resolve bank FX classification and PRC tax residence separately.
  2. Identify the real source of every part of the balance (salary, property sale, inheritance, dividends, already-taxed principal).
  3. Match each part to its legal entitlement: documented current income, emigrant property, inheritance, property sale, insurance, or business distribution.
  4. Complete tax certificates/filings for the underlying income before remitting.
  5. Only then compare execution rails (BOC/HSBC TT, HSBC Global Transfer, eligible Wise, WU cash pickup) using live executable quotes.
  6. Keep receipts for U.S. FBAR/FATCA/3520 and personal FX-gain analysis.

Cost answer in plain English

  • BOC standard TT scenario: CNY216.92 (USD32.42) at USD10,000 and CNY410.00 (USD61.27) at USD400,000. Published 0.1% commission is capped, so the known sender fee stops rising at USD40,000 in this scenario.
  • HSBC Premier/Advance TT: CNY220.00 (USD32.88) at USD10,000 and CNY620.00 (USD92.65) at USD400,000, with the USD figures converted at BOC's ask only for comparability.
  • HSBC Private Banking: published individual TT commission/cable is zero, but eligibility, FX and third-party charges are not zero.
  • Wise: four observed public bank-fee examples range from CNY436.18 to CNY1,574.72. They are point-in-time source-amount quotes, not a formula or a guaranteed 40-row schedule.
  • Not included: live FX difference, intermediary/receiving-bank charges, notarization/translation, underlying income tax and U.S. tax. Therefore no honest all-in total is available.

Provider rails do not create legal entitlement
Wise from-CNY is limited to eligible non-mainland-Chinese individuals with China work/tax records and own-account destinations. Tax follows the income, not the wire
Moving after-tax principal is not itself a PRC IIT category. Housing gains, dividends and other income have their own rules. From 2026-09-01 foreign individuals' FIE dividends face 20% IIT; the old exemption is repealed. U.S. citizens report worldwide income and may face FBAR/FATCA duties independent of the transfer.		S31 · State Taxation Administration / Guangdong S32 · State Taxation Administration S33 · STA Shanghai S37 · IRS S40 · IRS
U.S. reporting penalties are ceilings, not transfer fees
FBAR/FATCA thresholds and penalties are separate from conversion cost. Bittner counts nonwillful FBAR penalties per report; willful 50% is a statutory ceiling in specified cases, not a predicted annual charge. The U.S. 1% remittance tax applies to specified U.S.-outbound physical-instrument transfers, not China-to-U.S. moves.		S40 · IRS S41 · eCFR / FinCEN S42 · U.S. Supreme Court S44 · IRS
Prohibited schemes are rejected, not priced
Quota splitting, underground banking, crypto bridges and fabricated trade are rejected. PBOC Yinfa [2026] No. 42 is the current virtual-currency authority and expressly repealed the 2021 notice. Prohibited schemes receive no execution steps or legitimising price.		S01 · State Council / gov.cn S03 · SAFE / State Council Gazette S45 · People's Bank of China
