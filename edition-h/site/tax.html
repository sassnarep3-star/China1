Tax on both sides

Two countries, two different questions. China taxes the income that created the money. The United States taxes your worldwide income and requires you to report the account independently of any transfer. Neither taxes the act of changing currency.

How the two interact, mechanically

1. PRC tax is computed on the income under PRC law and paid in China. The transfer is a separate administrative step that requires proof the tax was paid.

2. The US taxes the same income under its own rules, in dollars, at the §988 rate for any exchange-rate gain.

3. The US grants a credit for the PRC tax paid on that same income, limited to the US tax attributable to it. Where the PRC rate is higher than the US rate on that slice, the excess generally cannot be refunded — that difference is the only sense in which double taxation bites, and it is a rate difference, not a penalty.

What is deliberately not filled in. Your cost basis, holding period, whether the money is salary or capital, whether you were tax-resident in China in the year, and whether you have filed before. All of those change the answer, none of them are known, and inventing them would produce a confident wrong number. See Flagged for review.
