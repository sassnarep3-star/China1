Breaking down "the conversion penalty"

The premise says converting triggers a considerable tax. Checked provision by provision, that premise is four unrelated things stacked into one word. This page separates them and puts a verified number on each, and leaves blank what cannot be known without your own facts.

At USD 400,000 the statutory exposure on these is between $120,000 and $400,000. That is not a fee you negotiate; it is the range the regulation sets, before confiscation of illegal gains and before any criminal referral.

Layer 4 · US tax and filing penalties — usually the largest number

The largest exposure in this whole analysis is not on the Chinese side. It is a filing failure on the US side, and it is triggered by not filing, not by transferring.

The willful FBAR penalty is the greater of the inflation-adjusted statutory figure or 50% of the account balance. At a USD 400,000 balance that second limb is $200,000 — 213 times the total cost of moving the money lawfully.

What this means in one paragraph

The conversion itself is not taxed. The friction is under one per cent at every size. The tax that does arise is on the income behind the money, at 20% of the gain where the transfer-of-assets rate applies, and it is owed whether or not you move anything. The penalties that reach 30% to 100% are for using a prohibited channel, and the penalties that reach 50% of the balance are for not filing in the United States. Every one of those is avoidable by doing the documented thing in the right order.
