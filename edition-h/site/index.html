What it actually costs to get money out of China

The premise is the expensive part. Converting your own money is not a taxable event in either country, and doing it lawfully costs between 0.23% and 0.88% of the amount. What produces the "considerable tax" people describe is either a PRC tax on the income behind the money, a fine for using a prohibited channel, or a US filing failure. This site prices the first, separates the other three, and shows its work for every figure.

The short answer

Use the documented route that matches the money's real source, settle the PRC tax on the income first, and fix your US reporting before you move a single dollar.

At USD 400,000 that is $937.34 — 0.234% of the amount. The same amount moved through an underground bank carries a statutory exposure of 30% to 100%, which is between $120,000 and $400,000. There is no arrangement in which the prohibited route is cheaper.

Where the money actually goes

Four different things get called "the conversion penalty". Only the first is a cost of converting.

LayerWhat it isSizeSource
1 · Friction FX margin, commission, cable, correspondent, receiving bank, documents. Published, unavoidable, charged once. 0.23%–0.88% BK-01 BK-04 BK-08
2 · PRC income tax IIT on the income or gain behind the money. Due whether or not you ever move it. The principal is never taxed. 20% of the gain CN-18 CN-19
3 · PRC conduct fine The sanction for using a prohibited channel — splitting, underground dealing, crypto, false invoices, illegal cash carriage. 20%–100% CN-04 CN-05 CN-06 CN-20
4 · US filing penalty FBAR, 8938 and 3520 failures, plus failure-to-file and accuracy additions. Triggered by not filing, not by transferring. 20%–50% US-09 US-10 US-11
