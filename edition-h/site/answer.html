The least-tax-penalty way out

Ranked by what actually reduces the penalty, not by what looks cheapest on a fee page. The first step is free and removes most of the risk.

The one-sentence answer. There is no tax on converting your own CNY into USD in either country — so the least-penalty strategy is not a clever conversion trick, it is to use the documented route that matches how the money was earned, pay the PRC tax that was already due on that income, and be current on your US filings before the money moves.

Why the premise is wrong, specifically

PRC tax law lists nine categories of income. Currency exchange is not one of them. Individual income tax attaches to salary, service remuneration, author's remuneration, royalties, business operation, interest/dividends/bonuses, leasing of assets, transfer of assets and incidental income. Moving your own after-tax principal between currencies falls outside all nine. That is the whole of the reason there is no conversion tax.

Quote: "The following categories of individual income shall be subject to individual income tax: (1) Income from salary and wages; … (9) Incidental income." — CN-18

On the US side, a personal conversion recognises no FX gain up to USD 200. §988(e)(2) disapplies the recognition rule for an individual's personal transaction, unless the gain that would otherwise be recognised exceeds USD 200. Above that threshold the exclusion simply does not apply and ordinary-income treatment follows.

Quote: "no gain shall be recognized … The preceding sentence shall not apply if the gain which would otherwise be recognized on the transaction exceeds $200." — US-01

The 1% remittance transfer tax does not apply to this direction. It is imposed on remittances sent FROM the United States TO a recipient in a foreign country, when the sender hands over cash, a money order, a cashier's cheque or a similar physical instrument. A China-to-United-States transfer is the opposite direction.

Quote: "a 1% remittance transfer tax applies to remittances sent from the United States to recipients in foreign countries when the sender provides cash, a money order, a cashier's check, or other similar physical instrument" — US-06

What you should not do

Splitting the amount across family and friends. The prohibition is in the administrative measure itself, and the watchlist lasts the year of listing plus two more, during which every FX transaction you make is pushed to the counter under strict documentary review. It applies to the person who lends a quota as well as the one who borrows.

CN-03 CN-11

Underground banking, crypto, false invoices, undeclared cash. The statutory range for the FX-control offences is a fine of up to 30% of the amount, and 30% to 100% where the circumstances are serious, plus confiscation. At USD 400,000 that is between $120,000 and $400,000 — larger than every lawful fee in this entire analysis, added together, many times over.

The realistic path if your balance is not salary

If the money is not documented current-account income, the honest position is that no clean route matches it. The three real options are: give it a genuine current-account character by earning it via a real activity (a registered trade or e-commerce business); use the capital-account route that fits the underlying event (property sale, emigration, inheritance); or leave some of it in RMB for future RMB needs and convert only the rest. Inventing a character for it is the offence described above.
