Step by step

The sequence, in the order that works.

Route pages carry their own procedure. This page is the master sequence: what to establish first, what to do at the bank, and what to file on the US side. Nothing here needs your input to read; the branches are chosen by facts only you can check.

Step 1 — establish your classification in writing

  1. Ask the bank, in writing, whether it treats you as a domestic individual or a foreign individual, and on what basis. The dividing line is one year of continuous residence (CN-01 Art. 52(2)).
  2. Ask whether it will accept an Art. 59 documented purchase from you. The article is permissive, so the answer is the bank's.
  3. Keep the answer. It decides whether your cap is USD 50,000 or none.

Step 3 — execute at the bank

  1. Present the file and request the purchase under the article that fits, not under "the quota" by default.
  2. Confirm the rate at the counter: the board is a reference, the counter rate governs (CN-07).
  3. Decide on full-amount arrival. It costs USD 15 if the receiving bank holds its account with BOC New York, otherwise USD 25, and it stops the principal being shaved by correspondent charges (CN-08).
  4. Expect identity verification above RMB 5,000 / USD 1,000 under the 2026 AML order; it is a check, not a cap (CN-05).
  5. Keep the purchase slip, the remittance advice and the bank's confirmation of the rate applied.

Step 4 — the US side, which is where the real money is

  1. File the FBAR for every year the aggregate foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any time. Late is better than never, and voluntary correction is cheaper than an examination (US-04).
  2. Check Form 8938 against the threshold for your filing status and residence (US-03).
  3. If the money is an inheritance or gift above USD 100,000, file Form 3520, Part IV (US-05).
  4. If the RMB was acquired at a materially different rate, compute whether the §988 gain on the disposition exceeds USD 200; if it does, the whole gain is ordinary income (US-01).
  5. Keep records for at least five years from the FBAR due date (US-04).

What not to do

  • Do not use a relative's quota. Art. 62 watch-lists both sides for three years, and the FX Regulations expose the conduct to a fine of up to 30%, or 30%-100% in a serious case (CN-01, CN-02).
  • Do not use a dealer who offers a better rate. Art. 45 adds confiscation of the money to the fine (CN-01).
  • Do not describe a capital transfer as a current-account purchase. The bank's Art. 68 power to refuse is the mild consequence; Art. 40 is the severe one (CN-02, CN-01).
