M01 · current

Bank purchase and wire as a foreign individual against documented China-source income

境外个人凭境内经常项目合法人民币收入购汇汇出

lawful conditional F-01 F-02 F-03

The widest lawful door in the rule text. If you are a foreign individual (a US passport ordinarily makes you one) and the RMB in the account is lawful current-account income earned in China, Art. 59 lets the bank sell you FX against your ID and the source documents, including tax vouchers, and Art. 65 lets you remit it. The text sets no annual cap on this route; the constraint is documentary, not numeric.

The rule

  • CN-02 Art. 59: a foreign individual's lawful current-account RMB income in China may be purchased into FX against ID and source documents including tax vouchers
  • CN-02 Art. 64: remitting FX from an account, up to USD 50,000 a day on ID alone, above that with transaction documents
  • CN-01 Art. 5: the State does not restrict current international payments and transfers
  • CN-01 Art. 12: current-account business must have a real, lawful transaction basis

CN-01 ↗ CN-02 ↗ CN-05 ↗ US-04 ↗

Cap or limit in the rule text

No annual cap stated in Art. 59 for a foreign individual who is not a permanent-residence holder. Art. 64 allows remittance of up to USD 50,000 a day from an FX account on ID alone, and above that with supporting documents.

Who this is for

A foreign individual with lawful current-account RMB income in China: salary, personal services, or other current-account receipts, with the tax position documented.

Documents the bank or SAFE will want

  • Passport and the bank's customer file
  • Evidence the RMB is current-account income: employment contract, payroll records, service contract and invoices, or dividend resolution
  • China individual income tax payment records or the tax certificate for the income
  • The individual FX purchase application (CN-02 Art. 57 applies to domestic individuals; banks apply the same form in practice)
  • Destination account details in your own name

Pros and cons

Pros

  • No annual cap in the rule text, which is the difference between this route and the USD 50,000 quota
  • Expressly contemplates tax vouchers, so a compliant tax history is an asset rather than an obstacle
  • One bank relationship, one currency conversion, published tariff

Cons

  • The bank decides whether your source qualifies as current-account income; a capital gain or a property sale does not
  • Requires a paper trail that many balances do not have
  • The route does not exist for a PRC citizen or for a permanent-residence holder, who fall under the quota instead

Step by step

  1. Confirm how the bank classifies you: foreign individual, or domestic individual because you have lived in China continuously for a year or more CN-01 ↗
  2. Assemble the income chain: contract, payroll or invoices, and the China tax record for the same money CN-02 ↗
  3. Ask the bank to purchase FX against those documents under Art. 59 rather than against the annual quota CN-02 ↗
  4. Remit in your own name; above USD 50,000 in one day the bank will ask for the transaction documents again CN-02 ↗
  5. Expect the 2026 identity-verification step on any remittance at or above RMB 5,000 / USD 1,000 CN-05 ↗
  6. File the US side: FBAR if the aggregate exceeded USD 10,000 at any time, and Form 8938 if the threshold for your filing status is met US-04 ↗
