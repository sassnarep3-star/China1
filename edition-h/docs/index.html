What it actually costs to get money out of China.

There is no tax on converting your own money in either country, so the premise that conversion is expensive is wrong. What is real is the capital-account barrier, a published bank tariff of well under 1%, and a US reporting exposure that can reach half the balance if a form is missed. This dashboard prices every route at every USD 10,000 step from USD 10,000 to USD 400,000 and shows its work.

The answer in one sentence. If the RMB came from salary or other current-account income in China, the cheapest lawful route is a documented bank purchase under Art. 59 of the 2020 Guidelines, wired straight to your US account: about $100.01 on USD 25,000 and 0.226% of the amount at USD 400,000. If it came from property or an inheritance, the quota and the 2004 personal-property transfer rules decide, and the answer changes.

Three facts decide everything

1. How did the RMB arise?

Salary, services and other current-account income route through a bank's documentary review. Property sale proceeds, an inheritance and an emigrant's capital route through a capital-account approval file. The same balance treated as the wrong kind of money is refused.

2. Are you a domestic or a foreign individual?

A US citizen is a foreign individual unless he has lived in China continuously for a year or more, which makes him domestic for FX purposes. Domestic means the USD 50,000 annual quota. Foreign, with documented China income, means no annual cap in the rule text. This single classification is the difference between eight years and one application (F-01).

3. What is your US filing history?

The bank's price is a rounding error next to a willful FBAR penalty, which is the greater of USD 165,353 or half the balance, per violation. If the account was never reported, fix that before worrying about the spread (F-05).

What is not the answer

Splitting the money across relatives' quotas and underground dealers are named offences with fines of up to 100% of the amount in a serious case, plus confiscation. They are listed here with the statute, and with no procedure.

* The $0 is about the character of the conversion, not about your whole tax position. Under 26 U.S.C. §988(e)(2) the foreign-currency gain on an individual's personal transaction is not recognised unless it exceeds USD 200 in that transaction, in which case the whole gain is recognised. Receiving an inheritance above USD 100,000 still needs Form 3520, and the account still needs an FBAR.
