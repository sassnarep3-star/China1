Tax and reporting

What is taxed, and what is merely reported.

Two propositions carry the whole answer. Conversion is not income in either country. Reporting is mandatory, and its penalties are the only numbers here that can exceed the money.

The §988 cliff, precisely

Section 988 makes a currency disposition an ordinary-income event. The relief for individuals is narrow: on a personal transaction, gain from exchange-rate movement after acquisition is not recognised — but the relief "shall not apply if the gain which would otherwise be recognized on the transaction exceeds $200". It is a cliff, not an allowance, and it is per transaction. The consequence for a large conversion is concrete: if you acquired the RMB years ago at a materially better rate, the gain can exceed USD 200 and the whole gain becomes ordinary income. The amount cannot be computed here because the acquisition rate and the acquisition dates of your balance are not known (F-13).

What this page does not do

  • It does not compute your US tax. The brackets, the filing status, the foreign tax credit and the source of the money are all unknown.
  • It does not compute a §988 gain. That needs the acquisition rate of the RMB and its acquisition date.
  • It does not state the 2026 US excise tax position on remittances, because the statute was not retrieved (F-22). The comment is on the flagged page and carries no figure.
