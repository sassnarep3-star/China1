The conversion penalty, broken down

A deep separation of FX margin, issuer fees, access costs, underlying income tax, US currency-gain tax, reporting penalties and unlawful-route fines.

The phrase “conversion penalty” mixes at least four different things. A commercial FX margin, a transfer fee, tax on the income that created the money, and penalties for unlawful conduct or non-reporting. Only the first two are prices of a lawful transfer; the others are not avoided by choosing a cheaper app.

What this means for least tax penalty

  • Do not create a taxable event just to move money: a sham service/dividend or fabricated sale can add real tax and penalties.
  • Use the route that matches the true source: documented salary (Art.59), property sale, inheritance/emigration, or genuine corporate proceeds.
  • Keep basis records: US personal FX gain above USD 200 can be recognised; “principal” is not a blanket exemption from currency-gain tax.
  • File FBAR/8938/3520/105 where triggered; reporting penalties can exceed the FX margin by orders of magnitude.
