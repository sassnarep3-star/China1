US reporting duties on money sitting in China

This is where a US citizen's real exposure lives. Note the distinction the IRS itself draws: whether the account produced taxable income has no effect on whether it is reportable. Reporting and taxation are separate questions, and the penalties attach to the reporting.

FormWho must file / thresholdWhenPenaltyNote
FBAR — FinCEN Form 114 A US person with a financial interest in, or signature authority over, foreign financial accounts whose aggregate value exceeded USD 10,000 at any time in the calendar year. Due 15 April, automatic extension to 15 October. Filed with FinCEN, not with the tax return. Keep records 5 years. Non-willful: USD 16,536 maximum per report (penalties assessed on or after 2025-01-17). Willful: USD 165,353 maximum, or 50% of the account balance, whichever is greater. A single CN¥ account in a Chinese bank is a foreign financial account. Whether it produced taxable income is irrelevant.
Source: S17 S19 S27
Form 8938 — Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets A specified individual holding specified foreign financial assets above the applicable threshold. US resident unmarried: above USD 50,000 at year end or USD 75,000 at any time. US resident MFJ: above USD 100,000 / USD 150,000. Living abroad unmarried: above USD 200,000 / USD 300,000. Living abroad MFJ: above USD 400,000 / USD 600,000. Attached to the annual income tax return. Not required if no income tax return is required for the year. USD 10,000, plus USD 10,000 per 30-day period after a 90-day IRS notice, capped at USD 50,000 additional. Overlaps FBAR but is not the same test — different statute (Title 26 vs Title 31), different thresholds, different scope.
Source: S20
Form 3520 — Part IV — large foreign gifts and bequests Aggregate gifts or bequests from a nonresident alien or foreign estate exceeding USD 100,000 in the tax year. Each gift over USD 5,000 must be separately identified. For purported gifts from foreign corporations or partnerships the threshold is USD 20,116 for 2025 and USD 20,573 for 2026. Due the 15th day of the 4th month after year end (6th month if living and working abroad); extendable to the 10th month. Filed separately from the income tax return. 5% of the amount of the foreign gifts per month the failure continues, capped at 25% in total. Receiving a gift or bequest is not taxable income. The penalty is for not reporting it.
Source: S21 S22
§988 — foreign currency gain on disposing of the CNY A disposition of nonfunctional currency is a §988 transaction. For an individual's personal transaction, no gain is recognised by reason of exchange-rate movement unless the gain would exceed USD 200. Reported on the return for the year of disposition where the threshold is exceeded. Not a penalty — the consequence is that the gain is ordinary income, not capital. The USD 200 test is a cliff applied per transaction, not an annual allowance. Just over USD 200 and the whole gain is in.
Source: S24

Penalty relief: the streamlined procedures

The IRS offers two tracks to a taxpayer who certifies that the failure to report foreign financial assets and pay all tax was not willful. Non-willful conduct means negligence, inadvertence, mistake, or a good-faith misunderstanding of the law.

TrackWhoOffshore penalty
Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures (SDOP) US residents — taxpayers who do not meet the non-residency test A single 5% Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty (percentage flagged — see F-note on the Flagged page)
Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures (SFOP) Taxpayers meeting the non-residency test (e.g. 330 full days abroad in one of the covered years) No miscellaneous offshore penalty

Source: S23. Two hard limits: the procedures are unavailable if the IRS has begun a civil examination of your returns for any taxable year, or if you are under IRS Criminal Investigation. And they are not amnesty — returns may still be audited, and additional civil penalties and even criminal liability remain possible if the conduct was in fact willful.

The one line that removes an excuse people rely on. The Form 3520 instructions state expressly that "the fact that a foreign country would impose penalties for disclosing the required information is not reasonable cause." You cannot defend a missing FBAR or Form 3520 by pointing at PRC secrecy law.
Source: S21

The treaty does not help here

The 1984 US–PRC Income Tax Convention (in force 1 January 1987) primarily allocates taxing rights and provides a foreign tax credit in Article 22 and information exchange in Article 25. Its Saving Clause — Protocol 1, paragraph 2 — preserves the US right to tax its citizens and residents as if the treaty had not come into effect. A treaty does not create a currency-mobility right, and it does not displace FBAR.

Source: S25 S26
