Who you are and what the money is

These four decide which article applies. Getting them wrong is the difference between a documented purchase and a refusal at the counter.

  3. If any part is a house sale or an inheritance, read the emigrant/inheritance route before anything else, because approval replaces the counter process.

Q04 Will one wire of the whole amount be accepted?openFor documented current-account income the rules read for this guide impose no ceiling, and the bank's commission is capped, so a single wire is the cheapest shape per dollar moved. The United States side

The transfer itself is not what the US taxes. What you owe depends on whether the income was reported in the year it was earned.

Q10 Was the China income reported on your US returns in the year it was earned?settledA US citizen is taxed on worldwide income from all sources. Foreign income is translated to dollars at the rate when it was received, paid or accrued. Tax paid to China on the same income can be taken as a credit on Form 1116, or as a deduction. IRS citizens abroadIRS foreign currencyIRS foreign tax creditYour filing history. If earlier years were missed, that exposure is older and larger than anything the transfer costs, and it is a question for a US preparer before money moves.
  1. Collect the US returns covering the years the money was earned.
  2. Match each year's China salary to the amount reported as foreign earned income.
  3. If any year is missing, get advice before the wire, because the arrival of the money does not create the liability but does attract attention.
Q11 Do the FBAR and Form 8938 thresholds apply to this account?settledFBAR: file when the aggregate value of foreign financial accounts exceeds USD 10,000 at any time in the calendar year, due 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October, filed with FinCEN and not with the return. Form 8938: for an unmarried person living in the US, more than USD 50,000 on the last day of the year or USD 75,000 at any time; the thresholds are higher for joint filers and for those living abroad. IRS 8938 vs FBAR31 CFR 1010.821The maximum non-willful penalty is published as USD 16,536 and the willful maximum as USD 165,353, but the statute's alternative measure for a willful violation was not modelled. F14
  1. Add up every foreign account you had during the year, including this one, at its highest point.
  2. Check the filing history for both forms separately; Form 8938 does not replace the FBAR.
  3. If either is missing for a past year, raise it with a preparer rather than filing only the current year.
Q12 Will the exchange itself create a taxable gain in the US?settledFor an individual disposing of foreign currency in a personal transaction, no gain is recognised from exchange-rate movement between acquisition and disposition. The statute withholds that treatment where the gain exceeds USD 200. 26 USC 988IRS foreign currencyThe character of a gain above USD 200, and whether a loss on a personal conversion can be deducted, were not verified. F13
  1. Compare the rate at which the renminbi was acquired with the rate at conversion; the difference is small at these amounts unless the money has sat for years.
  2. Keep the acquisition rate and date, because the USD 200 test is measured on the gain, not on the amount.
  3. Ask a preparer before reporting or deducting anything beyond that.
Q13 Is any part of this money a gift, a bequest or an inheritance?settledA gift or bequest from a nonresident alien individual or a foreign estate is reported on Form 3520 when the aggregate exceeds USD 100,000 in the taxable year, due the 15th day of the fourth month after the year end. IRS Form 3520PBOC Ann. 2004-16Whether a particular transfer is a gift, a bequest or your own property depends on the facts, and no page read for this guide settles it.
  1. Establish whose money it is before it moves: your own savings, a gift, or an estate distribution.
  2. If it is not yours, keep the will, the estate documents or the gift records, because the reporting is by the recipient.
  3. Read the China side of the same question: inherited money leaves China by a different route with SAFE approval.
Q14 Does the 1% remittance transfer tax apply to money coming in?settledNo. The IRS describes the tax as applying to remittances sent from the United States to recipients in foreign countries when the sender pays with cash, a money order, a cashier's check or a similar physical instrument. The sender is liable and the provider collects it. IRS IR-2026-48Nothing about inbound transfers; the definition is directional. The point is recorded here because the tax is easily misapplied to the wrong direction.
  1. Do not budget for this tax on an incoming wire.
  2. If you later send money out of the US by a covered method, expect the 1% to be collected at the counter.
  3. Read the IRS page before relying on a third-party summary, since the regulations were still proposed when it was published.

Before you commit: price, timing and the line you must not cross

Two questions left, and the second is the one that turns a cheap conversion into a fine.

QuestionWhat the rules already answerStill missingHow to find it out
Q15 What does the exchange rate itself cost, separately from the fees?settledOn the board reading used here the bank buys USD at 666.3 and sells at 669.11 per 100. Half of that gap, measured against the selling rate, is 0.2100% one way. That margin is charged on the whole amount and dwarfs the wire fee at large amounts: it is {fx10} on USD 10,000 and {fx400} on USD 400,000, before any fixed fee. BOC rate boardBOC tariffThe board states that the transaction rate is the one that applies, so the reading here is a reference point, not a quote. The rate moves daily. F20 F19
  1. Read the bank's selling rate on the day of the transfer, not the rate in the middle of the board.
  2. Compare the same one-way margin at another bank before committing; a fraction of a percent is worth more than the fee difference.
  3. Do the conversion once rather than twice: a round trip pays the margin twice.
Q16 Is splitting the money across people, years or accounts a way to reduce the cost?settledNo. Evading the limits by splitting is expressly prohibited, and so is using false documents. The penalties run to 30% of the amount, and to between 30% and 100% where the case is serious, with criminal liability where a crime is constituted. Private or disguised dealing in foreign exchange is prohibited on the same scale. Individual FX MeasuresFX RegulationNothing: the prohibition is explicit. What is unresolved is whether a factual pattern is treated as splitting, which is a question for the bank and, if it arises, a lawyer.
  1. Move the money once, in one documented wire, if the bank accepts it.
  2. Never use another person's quota or ID, and never accept funds from a third party to make up an amount.
  3. If the bank says no, ask for the reason in writing and escalate to the branch's compliance desk instead of changing the structure.
