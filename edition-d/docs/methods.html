Current-account routes (document-gated, quota-limited)

Current-account routes (document-gated, quota-limited)L1境内个人 annual facilitation quota: buy USD up to USD 50,000/person/year with ID only, then remit

Eligibility. 境内个人 — PRC residents with Chinese ID (includes Chinese nationals; a Chinese citizen who voluntarily naturalizes abroad automatically loses Chinese nationality, §9 Nationality Law — see S01/S17-related note in methodology; status fork matters).

Cap / limit. USD 50,000 per person per calendar year (S02 第二条； S03 第五十五/五十六条）. Remittances abroad on ID alone: same-day cumulative USD 50,000 (S03 第六十四条）.

Documents. Chinese ID (身份证). For amounts ≤ USD 50,000 cumulative you are doing a 'facilitation' (便利化) purchase: bank does the real-name registration and deducts from the annual quota. For a remittance ≤ USD 50,000 equivalent on the same day, ID alone; above, ID + documents showing the transaction amount (S03 Art. 64, S06).

Step by step.

  1. Confirm your status: holding a PRC resident ID card makes you a 境内个人 subject to the USD 50,000 facilitation quota (S01/S02).
  2. Open or use your own RMB settlement account at your bank and a personal FX account, or bring cash RMB for the conversion.
  3. Buy USD at the bank's 现汇卖出价 (spot sell) — you are the bank's counterparty; the 卖出价 of 669.29 CNY per 100 USD is the rate that applies to you on the captured board (B01).
  4. The bank records the purchase against your real-name annual quota; you receive USD in your FX account (or as cash, if the bank allows cash sales).
  5. Wire the USD to your US account: fill the outbound-remittance form (汇出汇款申请书), declare the purpose ('境外投资/留学/购汇汇出' etc. truthfully), attach beneficiary name/address/account/SWIFT.
  6. If the same-day remittance exceeds USD 50,000 equivalent, add documents showing the transaction amount (S03 Art. 64; S06).
  7. Keep the FX purchase voucher and remittance voucher; file FBAR if aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any time (S13) and Form 8938/5471 etc. as applicable.
Pros
  • No SAFE pre-approval for ≤ USD 50,000/yr; ID only in the common case.
  • Lawful, routine, well-trodden; bank executes in one visit or online for many banks.
  • Predictable published fee cap: 1‰ with ¥50 min / ¥260 max + ¥150 telegraph fee, counter rate (B02); online may be discounted (B03 — 2026 status unconfirmed, Flag F-D02).
Cons
  • Hard annual ceiling: USD 50,000 per person per year. To move USD 400,000 takes 8 calendar years of quota.
  • Cannot lawfully be stretched by splitting one year's need across banks or by using relatives' quotas ('ant moving' is prohibited — see P1).
  • Duty to use FX truthfully: the bank must match purchase purpose to remittance purpose and documents (S03 Art. 64).
US side. Transferring your own money is not a taxable US event. The CNY deposit sits on Form 8938/FBAR once over thresholds. §988 governs FX gain/loss if you have a USD functional basis vs CNY accounting.
China side. Chinese IIT: none on the remittance itself. Savings interest in the account: exempt since 2008-10-09 (S10). Dividend/interest income generally 20% if applicable — not part of this route (S09).
Sources: [S01] [S02] [S03] [S06] [S08] [B01] [B02]

Current-account routes (document-gated, quota-limited)L2境外个人 (non-resident individual — the usual US citizen) buy FX against lawful China-source RMB income; NO annual cap in the rule text

Eligibility. 境外个人 — foreign citizens (incl. naturalized US citizens; Chinese who naturalized abroad lost Chinese nationality automatically — Nationality Law §9, restated by NIA in registry context CN-17/S05 notes) and HK/MO/TW residents, who hold lawful China-source RMB income and whose identity documents the bank can verify.

Cap / limit. No annual cap appears in the rule text (S02 Art. 13; S03 Art. 59). Practically bounded by (a) the lawful, documented RMB income you actually have, (b) the bank's document review, (c) US reporting.

Documents. Passport (or HK/MO/TW ID) + documents proving lawful RMB income with the transaction amount — including tax vouchers (含税务凭证） (S02 第十三条； S03 Art. 59). Holders of a Foreign Permanent Resident ID card (持有外国人永久居留身份证的境外个人） instead use the USD 50,000 facilitation quota (S03 Art. 59).

Step by step.

  1. Confirm you are a 境外个人: foreign passport / HK-MO-TW residence status, and that you are NOT relying on a PRC ID for this purchase (S01 Art. 37; S03).
  2. Assemble income-source documents for the RMB amount you intend to convert: employment contract, payroll records, tax vouchers (完税证明/代扣代缴证明）, or transaction documents for the specific income — the rule requires '有交易额的购汇资金来源材料（含税务凭证）' (S03 Art. 59; S02 Art. 13).
  3. Walk into the bank (or use its approved online channel) and apply to purchase USD as a 境外个人 with those documents; the bank reviews the source materials and completes the FX sale.
  4. Remit the USD to your US bank account (汇出汇款）: same mechanics as L1 — purpose declaration, beneficiary SWIFT details; outbound remittance of FX bought under this route is not subject to the 境内个人 quota cap.
  5. Alternatively, remit FX already sitting in your China FX account: for 境外个人， FX held in their account remits out on ID alone, no amount limit stated in the rule text (S03 Art. 65(一）).
  6. Keep every document. File FBAR/Form 8938 for US reporting as applicable (S13, S16).
Pros
  • The only individual route with NO annual frequency or amount ceiling in the rule text — the full balance of lawfully earned China-source income can, in principle, move.
  • No SAFE pre-approval for the purchase itself; bank-level review.
  • Same published wire fee cap as L1 (B02); same measured board spread (B01).
Cons
  • Requires lawful China-source income and document-grade evidence incl. tax vouchers — unsupported cash balances cannot come out this way.
  • Capital-account savings accumulated before you became a foreign person are NOT covered by this route (no source document) — see L3/L4 routes.
  • Bank discretion at the document review stage; expect questions on amounts above typical monthly pay.
  • US reporting still applies in full (FBAR/8938) — the route is frictionless on the SAFE side but not on the IRS side.
US side. Not a taxable disposal. Pay US tax on the China income (it is reportable as earned; foreign tax credit available for Chinese IIT paid (S19); salary interest on the deposit afterward is US-taxable).
China side. Chinese IIT applies to the underlying income (S09); savings interest exempt (S10). The FX purchase itself: no Chinese tax.
Sources: [S01] [S02] [S03] [S08] [S09] [S10] [S13] [S16] [S19] [B01] [B02]

Capital-account exits (the whole-balance routes)

Capital-account exits (the whole-balance routes)L3Emigration asset transfer (移民转移): the only general-purpose exit for a whole capital balance

Eligibility. Chinese citizens (or lawful PRC residents) who have obtained the right to settle abroad (国外定居） — after acquiring foreign nationality (Nationality Law §9: voluntary naturalization abroad automatically lapses Chinese nationality) or foreign permanent residence — and who can produce (a) the public-security hukou-cancellation certificate, (b) the certified/consular proof of foreign settlement, (c) a tax certificate/完税凭证 from the tax authority where the property is located or the income arose (S04 Art. 8; S05).

Cap / limit. Whole declared balance, declared once, released in stages: first tranche ≤ 50% of declared total; after 1 year from first release, ≤ 50% of the remainder; after 2 years, the remainder. Declared total ≤ 等值人民币20万元 (≈ USD 30,000) may be released in one go after approval (S04 Art. 6). Approval: < ¥500,000 → provincial SAFE branch; ≥ ¥500,000 → SAFE approval after provincial preliminary review (S05).

Documents. 申请人身份证件； 户籍注销证明 (public security bureau); 中国驻外使领馆出具或认证的国外定居证明； 拟转移财产权利证明及合法来源证明； 税务机关开具的税收证明或完税凭证； SAFE application forms (S04 Art. 8).

Step by step.

  1. Become a foreign domiciliary first: naturalize (or complete foreign permanent-residence settlement) as legally required — this route exists for people moving abroad, not for undocumented outflows (S04 Art. 1–2; S05).
  2. Cancel your PRC household registration (户籍注销） with the public-security bureau — required for 移居外国 applicants (S04 Art. 8(四）).
  3. Obtain (a) certification/authentication of your foreign settlement proof from the Chinese embassy/consulate in the country of residence, and (b) a tax certificate or 完税凭证 from the tax authority where the property is located or income arose (S04 Art. 8(六）).
  4. Assemble proof of lawful ownership and lawful source for the whole balance (property deeds, deposits evidence, income records) — SAFE will not approve goods without source proof; disputed or unlawful assets are refused (S04 Art. 3; S05).
  5. File ONE application declaring the entire amount to be transferred, with your own account details abroad; SAFE reviews (provincial branch if < ¥500k; SAFE if ≥ ¥500k) (S05).
  6. After approval: convert and remit tranche 1 (≤ half); after one full year, tranche 2 (half of the remainder); after two years, the remainder — all direct to your own foreign account, never in cash (S04 Art. 6, Art. 11).
  7. If the declared total ≤ 等值人民币20万元， request one-shot release (S04 Art. 6 second para).
  8. Keep the SAFE approval; US side: report the account on FBAR/8938 when aggregate foreign financial assets exceed the thresholds (S13, S16).
Pros
  • The only individual route that lawfully moves an ENTIRE capital balance, including accumulated savings not traceable to a single documented income event — the textbook 'money stuck in China' exit.
  • Predictable staged legality: approvals are published, criteria are written, and the sequence is fixed by regulation rather than discretion.
  • One application covers the whole amount; no quota, no annual cycling.
Cons
  • Gated on emigration — you cannot use it while still a settled PRC resident; sequence is forced (foreign status → hukou cancellation → approval → staged release).
  • Time cost: up to ~2 years from first release for the full amount (50% → 75% → 100%) for balances > 等值人民币20万元.
  • Paperwork-heavy: tax clearance, hukou cancellation, consular authentication, notarization; SAFE approval two-tiered at ¥500k.
  • Only for amounts traceable in the legal-source sense; SAFE may refuse or curtail (S04 Art. 3; S05).
  • 2004 Announcement governs; SAFE practice may have evolved (heritage rules were revised 2014) — re-confirm locally before relying (Flag F-D04).
US side. A transfer of your own assets is not US-taxable. Interest remains §61 income; an FBAR will be due once the foreign account exceeds $10,000 aggregate; Form 8938 may also apply (S13, S16).
China side. No Chinese tax on the remittance itself. A tax clearance certificate is required and may involve settling outstanding taxes on any untaxed income; savings interest is exempt (S10); salary/dividends/property transfer income may attract 20% IIT (S09) — the 完税凭证 step forces full accounting.
Sources: [S01] [S04] [S05] [S09] [S10] [S13] [S16] [B01] [B02]

Capital-account exits (the whole-balance routes)L4Inheritance transfer (继承转移): whole inherited estate, one application, remit in one or instalments

Eligibility. Foreign citizens and HK/MO/TW residents who lawfully inherited assets in China (S05). Applies also after the 2014 SAFE simplification (see Flag F-D04 re current practice).

Cap / limit. Whole estate from one deceased may be declared once and remitted in one or several instalments; separate applications per deceased (S04 Art. 2, Art. 6; S05). Approval tiering same as emigrant: < ¥500k provincial, ≥ ¥500k SAFE (S05).

Documents. Identity documents of the foreign heir; inheritance certificate / notarized will (公证书） or People's Court judgment; deceased's property ownership proof; 完税凭证 / evidence taxes were handled; SAFE application materials (S04 Art. 8 applies by analogy to inheritance; S05).

Step by step.

  1. Establish the inheritance: notarized inheritance certificate (公证书） or a court judgment; for a will-less estate, the statutory-heirs determination.
  2. Collect the deceased's property proof (real-estate certificates, deposit books/bank statements, company equity), cancelled household registration of the deceased, and your own identity documents (S04 Art. 8 by analogy; S05).
  3. Obtain the tax certificate/完税凭证 from the competent tax authority evidencing that taxes relating to the inherited property/estate have been paid or cleared (S04 Art. 8(六）).
  4. Apply ONCE to the SAFE branch where the deceased was domiciled (原户籍所在地） — or where the property is located per current practice — declaring the full estate (S05).
  5. After approval, purchase FX and remit to your own foreign account in one or several instalments — instalment count is yours to choose; never cash (S04 Art. 11 by analogy).
  6. US side: a foreign inheritance is not income; report the foreign account on FBAR/8938; if someone else's estate (not yours as heir of own assets transferral) — Form 3520 may apply to you as US beneficiary of a foreign estate/trust distribution (S13, S16, S17, S18).
Pros
  • Moves a whole inherited balance — no annual quota, no staging wait (instalments allowed with a single application).
  • Not tied to your emigration status — a US citizen inheriting from China can use it today.
  • Simple fee profile: one approval, one-or-more transfers at the standard wire fee.
Cons
  • Requires a genuine inheritance from a China-domiciled decedent — not a route for your own accumulated savings.
  • Notarization + tax clearance pipeline; SAFE approval at ¥500k+ tier.
  • 2014 SAFE changes may have altered operational sequence; verify locally (Flag F-D04).
US side. Inheritance itself: not taxable to the US beneficiary, but Form 3520 may be required for large foreign gifts/bequests (aggregate >$10,000 statutory base, adjusted — see S17/S18), and FBAR/8938 for the account (S13, S16).
China side. No Chinese IIT on the transfer itself per the transfer rules; inheritance of certain property may still involve deemed disposed-property tax events on the decedent's side — obtain the 完税凭证 to prove clearance (S04 Art. 8; S09).
Sources: [S01] [S04] [S05] [S09] [S13] [S16] [S17] [S18] [B01] [B02]

Capital-account exits (the whole-balance routes)L5Property-sale route: sell the China property, then SAFE-approve and remit the proceeds

Eligibility. 境外个人 (incl. foreign-citizen US persons) who lawfully own and sell residential/commercial property in China (S01 Art. 22; S03 context). For 境内个人 the same outcome uses the quota/other routes; this route is explicitly for foreign sellers.

Cap / limit. Amount of the proceeds of the specific sale; requires SAFE approval (核准）; not part of the USD 50,000 annual quota (S01 Art. 22).

Documents. Foreign passport + property ownership certificate + sale contract + tax vouchers from the local tax bureau evidencing taxes paid on the sale (S01 Art. 22; S03 context; S09).

Step by step.

  1. Sign the sale; collect ownership certificates, the contract, and the full tax set-up (VAT, deed tax, IIT on transfer income — 20% on 财产转让所得 after costs (S09); note: rates and local practice vary — out of scope of the conversion cost).
  2. Pay all China-side taxes at the land/tax bureau; obtain the 完税凭证 (tax payment vouchers) — SAFE will not process without them (S01 Art. 22).
  3. File the SAFE approval application for the FX purchase/remittance of the proceeds, attaching the deed, contract and tax vouchers (S01 Art. 22).
  4. After approval, purchase USD at your bank and remit to your own foreign account in one or instalments; cash not permitted under capital-account rules (S01 Art. 26; S04 Art. 11 by analogy).
  5. US side: report the account (FBAR/8938). §988: gain/loss between your CNY proceeds and USD remittance is ordinary FX gain/loss on the difference between basis and redemption (S20).
Pros
  • Releases capital value trapped in an illiquid asset to a US account — not subject to the USD 50,000/yr quota.
  • A standing statutory route with express SAFE-approval text.
Cons
  • You must actually SELL the property — irreversible and transaction costs (agent fees, taxes) are large relative to the conversion penalty.
  • Sale-side Chinese taxes dwarf the conversion cost: property-transfer IIT 20% on transfer income (S09), plus other local taxes — these are disposition costs, not conversion costs (Flag F-D07).
  • SAFE approval is a real gate; timing weeks-to-months and officer discretion on document sufficiency.
  • If the owner remains a 境内个人 resident using the quota instead, 5万/mo cadence applies — this route is for 境外个人 use.
US side. Same as L3: not a taxable disposal merely because cash crosses borders; §988 ordinary FX gain/loss may apply (S20); FBAR/8938 apply to the receiving foreign account (S13, S16).
China side. 20% IIT on 财产转让所得 (transfer income after deducting costs) + property transaction taxes — outside this ladder (Flag F-D07). The FX purchase/remittance itself: no additional tax.
Sources: [S01] [S03] [S09] [S20] [S13] [S16] [B01] [B02]

Cash & physical cross-border

Cash & physical cross-borderL6Physical cash carry-out (three-tier permit ladder)

Eligibility. Any traveler, but the amounts are capped per trip and SAFE/customs control tightens stepwise (S11, S12).

Cap / limit. ≤ USD 5,000 no permit; USD 5,000–10,000 bank permit; > USD 10,000 SAFE permit; multiple permits summing > USD 10,000 will not be released by customs; RMB CNY 20,000/trip (S11, S12).

Documents. None ≤ USD 5,000 equivalent. USD 5,000–10,000: bank-issued 《携带外汇出境许可证》; > USD 10,000: SAFE-branch-issued 《携带证》 (S12). RMB > CNY 20,000 must be declared on red channel (S11).

Step by step.

  1. Buy travel FX as cash at the bank (現鈔賣出 = 669.29 on the captured board — no cash premium on that date, B01) or withdraw cash from the FX account if allowed.
  2. ≤ USD 5,000 equivalent: walk through without a permit (unless a same-day/short-period repeat traveler — declare anyway to be safe) (S12).
  3. USD 5,000–10,000: obtain the bank 《携带外汇出境许可证》 before departure (S12).
  4. > USD 10,000: obtain the 《携带证》 from the SAFE branch where the deposit/purchase bank is located before departure (S12).
  5. Declare at customs if required; upon arrival in the US, file FinCEN Form 105 if carrying > $10,000 (S21).
Pros
  • Zero FX fee at the counter beyond the board spread; no SWIFT chain.
  • Immediate possession — no bank wire delay.
Cons
  • Tiny scale: USD 5,000 free (or $10,000 with papers) per person per trip — USD 400,000 would need dozens of lawful trips, and multi-trip structuring to defeat the limits is unlawful conduct (S03 Art. 62; S07 Art. 39/45) — do not aggregate trips for this purpose.
  • Physical risk of loss/theft; customs seizure risk above limits; US: >$10,000 must also be declared on entry (S21).
  • Bank/SAFE permit fees not published in the sources used → blank (Flag F-D08 family).
US side. Not a taxable event; Form 105 declaration duty above $10,000 (S21); no FBAR on currency carried personally (it is not an account).
China side. No tax. Compliance is the entire story: over-limit carriage without permit = 携带证 violation + possible penalty and customs action (S11, S12).
Sources: [S11] [S12] [S21] [B01]

Spending & cards (never a conversion)

Spending & cards (never a conversion)N1Spend directly abroad on a mainland-issued UnionPay/debit card — never a conversion

Eligibility. Anyone with a mainland bank card enabled for overseas use (CUP/Visa/Mastercard).

Cap / limit. No published spending cap and NO consumption of the USD 50,000 quota (B04).

Documents. The domestic bank card; no FX purchase occurs.

Step by step.

  1. Use the mainland card to pay merchants abroad; settlement converts CNY→foreign currency at the acquiring-bank/network rate, not at your bank's board (B04).
  2. Annual overseas cash withdrawals per person across all cards: ≤ 等值人民币10万元 (B04).
  3. Per card per day cash withdrawal: ≤ 等值人民币1万元 (B04).
  4. Over-annual-limit withdrawals suspend both overseas cash withdrawal for the rest of the year and the next (B04).
Pros
  • Zero conversion paperwork; zero quota consumption; works at acceptance points globally.
  • Useful for expenses — not a repatriation mechanism.
Cons
  • Cannot pay a US landlord's mortgage into a US account, tuition by international wire, or invest in the US by card — it is not a transfer channel.
  • Network FX markups are not on any official schedule → unverifiable, blank (Flag F-D08).
  • Annual cash cap still applies if you want the money as USD cash.
US side. n/a — it is spending, not transfer. FBAR no; Schedule B no; keep ordinary records.
China side. n/a — no FX sale occurs.
Sources: [B04] [S03]

Spending & cards (never a conversion)N2ATM cash withdrawals abroad on a mainland-issued card

Eligibility. Mainland-issued card(s) with overseas ATM enabled.

Cap / limit. Per-card per-day ≤ 等值人民币1万元； per-person per-calendar-year across all cards incl. supplementary ≤ 等值人民币10万元； over-limit → suspension this year + next year (B04).

Documents. The card; bank PIN.

Step by step.

  1. Withdraw at compliant overseas ATMs up to the daily per-card cap.
  2. Track the calendar-year per-person cap across ALL your cards (incl. supplementary cards, i.e., your spouse's/children's supplementary cards count against you) — exceed it and overseas cash withdrawal is suspended for that year AND the next (B04).
Pros
  • Legal, card-simple way to hold USD cash abroad within limits.
  • No quota consumption (B04).
Cons
  • Scale ceiling: ¥100,000/yr ≈ USD 14,939/yr at the captured board — to extract USD 400,000 this way needs ~27 years; obviously not a plan.
  • Per-transaction ATM fees: not on any official CN schedule → unverifiable, blank (Flag F-D08).
US side. Currency carried out after withdrawal: >$10,000 must be declared on US entry (S21). FBAR no (cash in hand is not an account).
China side. n/a.
Sources: [B04] [S11] [S21]

Investment wrappers (not conversions)

Investment wrappers (not conversions)N3QDII funds / closed-loop connectors — not a CNY→USD conversion at all

Eligibility. Chinese investors (incl. some foreign residents per fund articles) buying RMB-denominated units of licensed managers with overseas exposure; money never actually becomes USD you can hold.

Cap / limit. Manager-level QDII quota and fund subscription limits; no personal quota; not disclosed per investor. Specific limits: NOT in this edition's registry (CSRC copy not retrieved this session) — see Flag F-D15. The FX legs are executed wholesale by the manager under SAFE pilot rules; no retail meter applies.

Documents. Fund account at the domestic bank/broker; subscription documents.

Step by step.

  1. Open a fund account with a domestic manager offering QDII share classes.
  2. Subscribe in RMB; the manager converts CNY→FX wholesale and deploys offshore.
  3. Redeem the units in RMB. You never receive USD wire proceeds in the US.
  4. Southbound Stock Connect / mutually-recognised funds: analogous — RMB settlement only.
Pros
  • Legitimate offshore market exposure legally from inside China; no remittance needed.
  • No quota consumption; fully legal; deep liquidity for the large managers.
Cons
  • Not repatriation: you hold RMB-denominated fund units, not USD; redemption is in RMB.
  • All costs embedded in NAV; no official per-transaction CN/USD fee schedule exists for the underlying FX legs → unverifiable, blank (Flag F-D08).
  • Traps: You can end up 'invested abroad' yet unable to spend in the US without a separate, lawful repatriation (L1–L5).
US side. PFIC risk on the US side (foreign fund units are almost certainly passive foreign investment companies; §1291 treatment) — see edition-c for detail; consult US tax counsel. FBAR/8938 yes.
China side. No conversion, no CNY→USD leg; gains taxed per fund rules; FX leg executed wholesale by manager.
Sources: [S03]

Keep-in-place / sequencing strategies

Keep-in-place / sequencing strategiesK1Keep it in China; consume there and on China-side trips

Eligibility. Anyone.

Cap / limit. n/a

Documents. None.

Step by step.

  1. Use the RMB balance directly in China: daily spending, paying China obligations, vacations, tuition for China-based programs (免征额 applies in facilitation contexts).
  2. On US trips: pay with the mainland card or withdraw cash within the ¥100,000/yr cap (N2).
  3. Gift or family support within China: fine; moving family funds out must still respect capital-account rules (L3–L5).
Pros
  • Zero cost, zero quota, zero paperwork beyond the cards.
  • Safest option when the money is genuinely for keeping.
Cons
  • Does not get money INTO the US — if the goal is US spending/investment, this is a non-answer; it is listed for completeness (and is often the correct retention decision).
US side. Nothing to report on the remittance side; the foreign account itself is reportable (FBAR/8938) if it is an account you hold (S13, S16).
China side. Savings interest exempt (S10); dividends 20% (S09).
Sources: [S03] [S09] [S10] [B04]

Keep-in-place / sequencing strategiesK2Hold offshore RMB (CNH) instead of onshore CNY; convert offshore later

Eligibility. Only someone who can lawfully hold CNH offshore — an offshore entity/account; NOT available at a Chinese branch counter for moving your own onshore balance.

Cap / limit. n/a — the onshore→offshore leg is precisely the controlled leg; this method is usually unreachable for the stuck balance in question.

Documents. Offshore account opening documentation; an existing offshore base of operations or an entity.

Step by step.

  1. There is no lawful instruction a mainland branch can execute to convert your onshore CNY into offshore CNH — CNH pools arise offshore.
  2. If you already hold CNH offshore (e.g., pre-existing offshore proceeds), you can sell CNH→USD offshore without touching SAFE.
Pros
  • For balances already offshore, CNH→USD conversion offshore is a market transaction without onshore FX controls.
Cons
  • Does nothing for your ACTUAL onshore balance — acquiring CNH requires the very outbound transfer that is controlled.
  • Mis-sold in practice: beware of anyone selling 'CNH placement' to move onshore balances — that is an underground-bank pattern (P2).
US side. If held offshore, FBAR/8938 and §988/§1256 analysis applies (S13, S16, S20).
China side. n/a.
Sources: [S03]

Keep-in-place / sequencing strategiesK3Pay US obligations from the China side (tuition, property management, insurance, vendors)

Eligibility. Anyone with genuine, documentable payees in the US.

Cap / limit. Each single payment ≥ USD 50,000 equivalent requires a tax filing (备案） with the competent tax authority; 境内个人境外留学、旅游、探亲等因私用汇 exempt (S08).

Documents. Invoices/contracts proving the debt/obligation; tax filing for single ≥ USD 50,000 payments (or applicable exemption — 因私用汇 category exempt, S08).

Step by step.

  1. Identify the genuine US payee and obtain the invoice/contract/tuition bill.
  2. If the payment is single ≥ USD 50,000 equivalent: file the tax filing (税务备案） with the competent tax bureau with the contract — unless exempt (因私用汇： tuition is on the exempt list, S08).
  3. Instruct your bank to remit directly to the US payee; the bank matches the purpose, the documents, and (for >USD 50k same-day) the amount to the transaction (S03 Art. 64).
Pros
  • Eliminates the intermediary step of repatriating then paying — one lawful leg.
  • Documented, tax-sanctioned; single large payments perfectly legal above $50k with the filing.
Cons
  • Only for genuine US payees; no retain/leave-change — the money leaves China but lands WHEREVER the payee is, not in your pocket.
  • Single ≥USD 50k filings are real paperwork; wrongful purpose declarations = fraud exposure.
US side. Not income to you; FBAR n/a for the recipient (an obligation you pay is not an account); keep proof for both tax authorities.
China side. No tax on the payment route; the underlying obligation may itself have tax consequences on the counterparty side. The 备案 is a process requirement, not a tax (S08).
Sources: [S03] [S08] [S06]

Keep-in-place / sequencing strategiesK4Stage the annual quota across multiple years (accumulation plan)

Eligibility. 境内个人 (PRC residents) willing to hold the balance in China while it migrates over years at USD 50,000/yr.

Cap / limit. USD 50,000/yr; USD 400,000 needs 8 years (40 transfers over 8 years using 汇出， or 8 once-per-year purchases).

Documents. Same as L1, repeated each year.

Step by step.

  1. Execute L1 each calendar year up to the quota.
  2. Track your quota year-to-date via the SAFE e-system/your bank; the quota is calendar-year, person-specific.
  3. Combine with L2 if you have documented China-source income (its route has no cap) to accelerate.
Pros
  • Perfectly lawful; no special approval; automatic discipline.
  • Predictable: USD 50,000 per year, forever.
Cons
  • Decade-scale time for large balances — 8 years for $400,000.
  • Regulatory change risk over the staging horizon.
US side. FBAR/8938 each year the balance sits in China (S13, S16) — unreported drag on this plan is where most expats fail.
China side. Interest continues to accrue tax-free in China (S10).
Sources: [S02] [S03] [S06] [B02] [S13]
