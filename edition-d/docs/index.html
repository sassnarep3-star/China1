You are not taxed for convertingChina charges nine enumerated income categories; a currency exchange is none of them (CN-05 Art. 2). The US charges income; a personal conversion is a §988 event whose gain is not recognised up to $200 (US-01).CN-05 ↗ US-01 ↗
The conversion is cheap; the exit is the problemTotal cost is $933 at $400,000. What stops the money is capital-account convertibility, not a levy (CN-01 Art. 3).CN-01 ↗
One fact decides everythingIf the bank holds your passport, CN-03 Art. 59 gives a documented purchase route with no annual cap in the text. If it holds a PRC ID card, the USD 50,000 quota applies.CN-03 ↗ CN-01 ↗
The real money at risk is US reportingA willful FBAR penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance, per violation (US-02). At $400,000 that is $200,000 — 214 times the cost of moving it.US-02 ↗ US-03 ↗
The wrong routes cost 30% to 100%Splitting, underground dealing and crypto convert a sub-1% problem into a fine of 30% to 100% of the amount, plus confiscation: $120,000 to $400,000 at $400,000 (CN-02 Arts. 39, 45; CN-06).CN-02 ↗ CN-06 ↗

Read this before using any number

The tables price a lawful, documented transfer. They do not tell you whether your particular balance qualifies, because the character of the money is unknown (F03). They also do not include anything that could not be sourced — notarisation, travel, card conversion fees and your own bank's inbound charge are blank and flagged.

What this is. Published-rule research with arithmetic shown. It is not legal, tax or investment advice, and it is not a bank quote. Rates and tariffs change; the sources are linked for manual review.

Values that could not be confirmed from an official page are blank and listed on Flagged for review. No value is ever inferred.
