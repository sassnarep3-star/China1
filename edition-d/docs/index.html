Getting CNY→USD as a US citizen: the honest arithmetic

There is no “conversion tax” in either country — the conversion penalty is ≈0.21% of amount in bank spread, plus a capped wire fee. The binding constraint is capital-account control in China, and the largest real exposure is US reporting non-compliance (up to 50% of the balance per year), not the exchange itself.

Findings, most important first
  1. Converting is legal and cheap. No Chinese or US tax arises on the exchange itself. Chinese IIT on the deposit's savings interest has been exempt since 2008-10-09 (财税〔2008〕132号). Chinese tax is relevant only to the underlying income (e.g., 20% on interest/dividends/property transfer if applicable), not to the conversion.
  2. The wall is capital-account control, not tax. RMB is convertible for current-account purposes; the balance you feel “stuck” is capital. Only two standing routes move a whole capital balance: 移民转移 (emigrant, staged) and 继承转移 (inheritance, one shot). Property-sale proceeds move via SAFE approval. Everything else is the USD 50,000/yr meter (境内个人） or no limit if you're 境外个人 with documented income.
  3. Your status is the whole game. Chinese citizen → naturalized US citizen = you are 境外个人 by operation of Nationality Law §9; the USD 50,000 facilitation quota no longer binds you for documented China-source income (S02 Art.13; S03 Art.59/65(一）). If you still hold a PRC ID, the quota binds (S02).
  4. The statutory “penalty” for route failure is 30%–100% of the amount (外汇管理条例 Art. 39 逃汇； Art. 45 私买私卖）. There is no rounding-error shortcut: splitting, relatives' quotas, underground banks, crypto — all terminate here.
  5. The largest real-world exposure for a US citizen is the IRS, not SAFE: FBAR willful = greater of $165,353 or 50% of balance per year (31 CFR 1010.821; 31 USC 5321(a)(5)); Form 8938 = $10,000 + $10,000/30-day, max $50,000 extra after notice (26 USC 6038D(d)); foreign-bequest Form 3520 = 5%/month to 25% (26 USC 6039F).
  6. Filed correctly, reporting costs $0. FBAR is free; 8938 attaches to a return you're filing anyway; compliance is the corridor, and it is free.
  7. Bank fees are legally capped and tiny at scale: 1‰ up to ¥260 + ¥150 per wire, i.e., ≤¥410 (≈$61) per remittance, whether it is $10k or $400k. Eight quota-year wires ≈ $490 all-in — the fee is trivial; the ceiling and the paperwork are not.
  8. Sequence beats selection: the cheapest correct order is: (1) fix US reporting first (stop the bleeding), (2) determine 境内外 status and document income, (3) choose L2/L3/L4/L5 for bulk or staged L1 for modest sums, (4) never use prohibited routes.
