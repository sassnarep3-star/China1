The obligations that run alongside the transfer

None of these is a tax on converting. All of them are triggered by holding or moving the account, and their penalties dwarf the cost of the transfer.

The one thing to fix first

An account that has existed for years and was never reported is a bigger problem than the transfer. The willful penalty is 50% of the balance per violation, and the assessment window runs six years from the transaction. Whether a violation was willful is a facts-and-circumstances question, not a box on a form. The 40-row tables on this site assume the reporting position is clean; if it is not, the transfer is the second problem.

How the reporting interacts with the route

  • The Chinese account stays reportable for every year it exists, including the year it is emptied.
  • FBAR values are converted at the end-of-year rate; Form 8938 uses the year-end rate as well.
  • A gift from a foreign relative is not a way around this: above USD 100,000 it is reportable on Form 3520, and the funds remain the donor's, not yours.
  • If the balance sits in a Chinese fund or insurance product rather than a deposit, the product may be a PFIC, which is a separate and potentially larger US tax problem that this edition did not model.
