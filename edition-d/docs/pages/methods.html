M01 · Documented current-account purchase and remittance as an overseas individual

lawful

The bank sells you USD against your lawful China-source current-account RMB income, and you wire it out.

Rule
A 境外个人 may purchase FX against lawful current-account RMB income earned in China, on ID plus source-of-funds documents including tax vouchers (CN-03 Art. 59; CN-01 Art. 13).
Limit in the text
No annual amount cap appears in the text of Art. 59 for a 境外个人 who does not hold a Foreigner Permanent Residence ID card; the USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota is expressed to apply only to such card holders. The bank still reviews authenticity case by case.
Documents
  • Passport
  • Source-of-funds documents for each tranche
  • Tax payment vouchers or exemption evidence for the income
  • Bank statements showing how the RMB arose

Pros

  • The only route in this inventory that is both express in the rule and usable for a normal, documented balance
  • No annual cap in the rule text for a passport-holder, unlike the 境内个人 quota
  • One conversion and one wire keeps third-party fees at their minimum
  • The USD 50,000 annual quota does not have to be stretched over eight years

Cons

  • The bank decides whether your documents are sufficient; a refusal is discretionary under CN-03 Art. 68
  • Costs are not proportional: the commission is capped, so large transfers are far cheaper per dollar than small ones
  • Requires the underlying income to be identifiable tranche by tranche
  • Tax vouchers may need to be obtained from the Chinese tax authority first

How it would work, step by step

  1. Ask the bank, in writing, for the account's subject category (境内个人 or 境外个人) and which identity document governs it. CN-01 Art. 28 makes this a bank determination on the identity document produced at account opening.
  2. Assemble the source-of-funds file for the specific tranche: employment contract or service agreement, payroll records, individual income tax payment certificates, and the bank statements showing the RMB arriving.
  3. Attend the branch with the passport and the file, and request a current-account FX purchase. CN-01 Art. 13 and CN-03 Art. 59 are the provisions to cite at the counter.
  4. Confirm the executed rate against the published board on the same day. The board is only a reference; CN-08 states the counter rate governs.
  5. Instruct the outward remittance in the same visit. Commission is 1 per mille with a CNY 260 cap, plus a CNY 150 cable fee to a non-Hong Kong destination (CN-07).
  6. If the payment is characterised as a service-trade payment above USD 50,000, expect the bank to require the tax filing verification (CN-03 Art. 49) before it releases the wire.
  7. Ask whether the full-amount-arrival option is available: USD 15 if the receiving bank holds its account with BOC New York, otherwise USD 25 (CN-09). Decline it if your US bank does not charge an inbound fee.
  8. Keep every document and the bank's receipt for five years (CN-03 Art. 12).
  9. Report the account on the US side for the year in which it existed, including the year it was emptied (US-03).

M02 · Current-account purchase paced to the USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota

lawful

If the bank classifies you as a 境内个人, the facilitation quota is USD 50,000 a year unless you produce purpose documents.

Rule
Individual settlement, and FX purchase by a 境内个人, are subject to an annual facilitation quota of USD 50,000 equivalent (CN-03 Art. 54); within it, ID alone suffices (Art. 55); outside it, ID plus source or purpose documents (Art. 56).
Limit in the text
USD 50,000 equivalent per person per year for the facilitation tier. Above the tier a documented current-account purchase remains available under Art. 56, so the quota is a convenience tier rather than an absolute ceiling.
Documents
  • PRC resident identity card if that is the classification on file
  • Purpose or source documents for anything above the quota

Pros

  • Simplest documentary burden at or below the quota
  • Available at any bank branch and through e-banking
  • Fee caps mean the extra cost of several wires is small in absolute terms

Cons

  • At USD 50,000 a year, USD 400,000 needs eight years — the binding cost is time and exposure, not fees
  • Splitting a larger amount across relatives' quotas to accelerate this is expressly prohibited (CN-03 Art. 62) and is listed as M14
  • The classification itself is the risk: a US citizen should not be using a PRC resident identity card if Chinese nationality has been lost
  • Each additional year keeps the account reportable in the US and exposed to exchange-rate movement

How it would work, step by step

  1. Establish which identity document the bank has on file (CN-01 Art. 28). If it is a PRC resident identity card, the USD 50,000 quota is the default tier.
  2. For a transfer at or below the quota, present the identity document and complete the individual FX purchase application. CN-03 Art. 55 requires ID alone at this tier.
  3. For a transfer above the quota, present source or purpose documents instead of reducing the amount — CN-03 Art. 56 permits a documented current-account purchase outside the quota.
  4. Do not divide the amount across family members or friends to stay inside several quotas. CN-03 Art. 62 prohibits it and imposes a watch list for the year of listing plus two further years.
  5. Process the wire in the same visit; the fee structure is identical to M01.
  6. File the US reports for every year the account exists (US-03).

Sources: CN-03 ↗ CN-01 ↗ F01 F03 F16

M03 · Emigration property transfer (移民财产转移)

lawful, needs approval

A person who has emigrated may apply once to transfer the whole of the property they owned before leaving, released in three stages.

Rule
Natural persons who have moved from mainland China to a foreign country or to Hong Kong/Macau may apply to convert and remit property lawfully owned before acquiring emigrant status (CN-04). Applications go to the SAFE branch at the original household-registration location.
Limit in the text
The whole declared balance, but at or above CNY 200,000 the release is staged: half first, half of the remainder after one year, the rest after two years. Below CNY 500,000 the provincial SAFE branch decides; at or above CNY 500,000 SAFE head office approves.
Documents
  • Proof of emigration and loss or cancellation of PRC household registration
  • Identity documents
  • Title and valuation documents for every asset declared
  • Notarised documents where not already notarised
  • Application form to the SAFE branch at the original household-registration jurisdiction

Pros

  • Expressly designed for exactly this problem: a whole balance, not a quota-sized slice
  • One application covers everything owned before emigration
  • Staged release is a schedule, not a refusal — the money is committed to move

Cons

  • Requires genuine emigrant status and documentary proof of it; a naturalised citizen who kept a live hukou has a problem to fix first
  • Notarisation, translation and valuation costs are real and were not verified here
  • Two years to full release, with exchange-rate exposure on the remainder
  • SAFE may refuse where the source of the property cannot be proven

How it would work, step by step

  1. Confirm emigrant status and obtain the household-registration cancellation record. CN-04 describes applicants as persons who moved abroad and owned the property before acquiring emigrant status.
  2. Inventory every asset owned before emigration, with title documents and a valuation.
  3. Have notarised every document that is not already notarised, including any power of attorney (CN-04).
  4. File one application at the SAFE branch for the jurisdiction of your original household registration. Below CNY 500,000 the provincial branch decides; at or above it, SAFE head office approves (CN-04).
  5. Expect the approval to schedule the release: half first, half of the remainder after one year, the balance after two years, for transfers at or above CNY 200,000 (CN-04).
  6. At each release, the bank purchases FX and remits. Commission and cable fees are charged per release (CN-07), which is why the staged route costs more in fees than a single wire.
  7. Keep the approval and every release receipt; the US reporting obligation runs in parallel (US-03).

Sources: CN-04 ↗ CN-01 ↗ F04 F06 F09 F14

M04 · Inheritance property transfer (继承财产转移)

lawful, needs approval

A foreign citizen who has lawfully inherited property in China may convert and remit the estate.

Rule
Foreign citizens and Hong Kong/Macau residents may apply to convert and remit lawfully inherited property in China (CN-04). All property inherited from the same decedent may be applied for once and remitted in one or several instalments.
Limit in the text
The declared estate. One application per decedent; property from different decedents requires separate applications (CN-04).
Documents
  • Death certificate of the decedent
  • Notarised inheritance certificate or probate document
  • Identity documents of the heir
  • Title and valuation documents for the inherited assets

Pros

  • Covers the whole estate in one application
  • No staged-release rule in the official description, unlike emigration transfer
  • Explicitly available to foreign citizens

Cons

  • Inheritance must be established under Chinese law first, which is a separate legal process
  • Notarisation of foreign documents and consular legalisation add cost that was not verified here
  • Not available for a living person's own savings

How it would work, step by step

  1. Obtain the death certificate and establish the inheritance position under Chinese law, with a notarised inheritance certificate.
  2. Assemble title and valuation documents for the inherited assets.
  3. File the application at the SAFE branch for the decedent's household-registration jurisdiction (CN-04).
  4. On approval, the bank converts and remits; one or several instalments are permitted for one decedent (CN-04).
  5. Retain the approval and receipts; report on the US side as required (US-03).

Sources: CN-04 ↗ CN-01 ↗ F06 F09 F14

M05 · Repatriation of proceeds from the sale of domestic commercial property

lawful, needs approval

An overseas individual who sold commercial property in China may remit the proceeds with SAFE approval.

Rule
An overseas individual's RMB proceeds from selling domestic commercial property may be purchased and remitted with SAFE approval (CN-01 Art. 22).
Limit in the text
The verified sale proceeds. Approval is required; the amount is not quota-limited in the rule text.
Documents
  • Property title and sale contract
  • Proof of the 20% individual income tax paid on the transfer gain
  • SAFE approval
  • Identity documents

Pros

  • Converts a property-funded balance that no current-account route can describe
  • The underlying tax position is settled at the point of sale, so the remittance is not a second taxable event

Cons

  • SAFE approval is a precondition, not a formality
  • The 20% individual income tax on the transfer gain is unavoidable and is charged on the gain, not on the balance
  • The gain itself is unknown in this case, so no tax figure is computed here

How it would work, step by step

  1. Complete the property transfer and pay the 20% individual income tax on the gain under CN-05 Art. 3(3).
  2. Obtain the sale contract, title documents and the tax payment certificate.
  3. Apply to SAFE for approval to purchase FX and remit the proceeds (CN-01 Art. 22).
  4. On approval, the bank purchases and remits; the fee structure is the same as M01.
  5. Report the sale and the account on the US side (US-03).

Sources: CN-01 ↗ F11 F13

M06 · Convert to USD now and hold it onshore

lawful

Buy the USD inside China and leave it in a USD account, remitting later under M01 or M02.

Rule
The purchase itself is the same documented current-account purchase as M01 (CN-03 Art. 59). What changes is that no wire is sent yet, so the cable and correspondent fees are not incurred.
Limit in the text
Same as the purchase route used.
Documents
  • As for M01
  • Instruction to credit the bank's USD account rather than remit

Pros

  • Removes exchange-rate exposure from today without paying wire fees yet
  • Cheaper in fees than converting and remitting in the same visit
  • The USD stays inside the regulated banking system

Cons

  • It does not solve the exit problem; it defers it
  • A USD deposit in China is still a foreign account for US reporting purposes
  • No separate purchase fee appears in BOC's published personal tariff, but a branch may charge; unverified

How it would work, step by step

  1. Complete the documented current-account purchase described in M01.
  2. Instruct the bank to credit your USD account instead of remitting.
  3. When you are ready to move it, use the outward remittance tier that matches your classification: CN-03 Art. 64 for a 境内个人 (USD 50,000 a day on ID alone) or Art. 65 for a 境外个人 (ID alone, no amount threshold in the text).
  4. Report the USD account in the US as a foreign financial account (US-03).

Sources: CN-01 ↗ CN-03 ↗ F02 F12 F17

M07 · Spend directly from the CNY balance abroad on a UnionPay card

lawful

Pay US expenses with a mainland-issued card so the money leaves as consumption rather than as a remittance.

Rule
Card spending is a payment, not an individual FX purchase, and no provision retrieved here subjects overseas card spending to the annual facilitation quota. That is an inference from the structure of CN-03 Arts. 54-59, not a quoted exemption, and it is flagged.
Limit in the text
No quota appears in the retrieved text; the card issuer's own limits apply.
Documents
  • Cardholder agreement with the issuing bank

Pros

  • Consumes no documented purchase quota in the retrieved rules
  • No wire, cable or correspondent fee at all
  • Useful for recurring USD expenses such as tuition, rent or medical bills

Cons

  • Only works for spending, never for moving a balance into your own US account
  • The card's own currency-conversion markup is the real cost and no official tariff was retrieved, so the cost cells are blank
  • Large or repeated overseas card use may be questioned by the issuer

How it would work, step by step

  1. Confirm with the issuing bank the card's overseas transaction limit and its currency-conversion fee; no official figure is published in the sources retrieved here.
  2. Use the card for genuine expenses, retaining receipts.
  3. Do not use the card to manufacture cash or to pay a counterparty who returns funds to you; that is a disguised transfer and falls under the prohibited routes.

Sources: CN-03 ↗ F18

M09 · Carry cash

lawful

Withdraw USD cash in China and carry it out within the published limits.

Rule
Outbound cash of USD 5,000 to 10,000 requires a foreign-exchange carriage permit from the bank; above USD 10,000 a SAFE permit is required. Inbound cash above USD 5,000 must be declared to customs (CN-03 Art. 89). More than USD 10,000 entering the United States must be reported (US-06).
Limit in the text
USD 10,000 per person per trip with the SAFE permit; USD 5,000 without any permit.
Documents
  • Passport
  • Bank cash-withdrawal record
  • Carriage permit where required
  • US currency report where required

Pros

  • No wire, cable or correspondent fee
  • The published cash and spot USD rates at BOC were identical on the board retrieved, so the exchange margin was not worse

Cons

  • At USD 5,000 a trip, USD 400,000 is 80 trips; at USD 10,000 it is 40
  • Physical loss and seizure risk on every trip
  • Travel cost is real and was not verified, so it is blank in the table
  • Repeated cash carriage invites scrutiny; deliberately structuring to stay under reportable amounts is a separate offence

How it would work, step by step

  1. Withdraw or purchase USD cash at the bank within the permit thresholds in CN-03 Art. 89.
  2. Obtain the carriage permit from the bank for USD 5,000-10,000, or from SAFE above USD 10,000.
  3. Declare the currency to US Customs and Border Protection on arrival if it exceeds USD 10,000 at one time (US-06).
  4. Keep the bank withdrawal slips; they are the evidence of lawful origin if questioned.

Sources: CN-03 ↗ US-06 ↗ F20

M12 · Individual trade or cross-border e-commerce settlement

lawful, needs approval

If the balance is genuine trading or e-commerce revenue, it can be settled outside the quota.

Rule
An individual engaged in cross-border e-commerce or market-procurement trade may settle FX through a personal FX account against transaction or electronic-trade information, and that settlement does not consume the annual facilitation quota (CN-03 Art. 66).
Limit in the text
Bounded by the genuine transaction value, evidenced by trade data.
Documents
  • Trade or platform records
  • Contracts, logistics documents or electronic trade information
  • Registration where the activity requires it

Pros

  • Does not consume the annual quota
  • Fee structure is the ordinary bank wire
  • Well defined for genuine e-commerce sellers

Cons

  • Only available if the money really is trade revenue; anything else is a fabricated trade basis, which is prohibited
  • Requires platform or customs data that matches the funds

How it would work, step by step

  1. Establish that the balance is settlement proceeds from genuine cross-border e-commerce or market-procurement trade.
  2. Assemble the platform statements, orders, logistics records or customs declarations that evidence the trade.
  3. Present the file at the bank for settlement under CN-03 Art. 66, noting that it does not consume the facilitation quota.
  4. Remit under the ordinary wire fee structure (CN-07).
  5. Keep the trade records for five years (CN-03 Art. 12).

Sources: CN-03 ↗ F03

M13 · Liquidate a foreign-invested enterprise and remit the foreign investor's RMB

lawful, needs approval

A statutory entitlement, but it requires a company and a liquidation.

Rule
A lawfully terminated foreign-invested enterprise may, after liquidation and tax payment under State rules, purchase FX and remit the RMB belonging to the foreign investor (CN-02 Art. 22 para. 3).
Limit in the text
The investor's share of the liquidation proceeds.
Documents
  • Liquidation approval and tax clearance
  • Capital verification and ownership documents
  • Audited liquidation accounts

Pros

  • An express entitlement in a State Council regulation, not a discretionary policy
  • Can move a whole balance at once

Cons

  • Only applies to money that is genuinely in a company, not to personal savings
  • Liquidation, audit, tax clearance and legal costs were not verified and are likely to exceed the FX cost by a wide margin
  • Takes months

How it would work, step by step

  1. Only reachable if the funds are company funds. Incorporate or identify the existing foreign-invested enterprise.
  2. Resolve liquidation, tax clearance and the audit under State rules.
  3. On completion, instruct the bank to purchase FX and remit the foreign investor's RMB under CN-02 Art. 22 para. 3.
  4. Report the company interest and the accounts on the US side; a foreign corporation may also bring Form 5471 into play, which was not analysed here.

Sources: CN-02 ↗ F23

M15 · Underground bank, mirror trade or private FX dealer

prohibited

Criminal exposure, and the counterparty risk is total.

Rule
Private, disguised or brokered buying and selling of FX in relatively large amounts carries a warning, confiscation of illegal gains and a fine of up to 30% of the amount, or 30% to 100% where serious (CN-02 Art. 45). Evading FX control to move capital abroad carries the same range under Art. 39, with criminal referral where the threshold is met.
Limit in the text
None — prohibited.
Documents
  • None: prohibited.

Pros

  • None: this route is prohibited.

Cons

  • Fine of 30% to 100% of the amount, plus confiscation
  • Criminal liability where the threshold is met
  • The counterparty holds your RMB before you are paid; there is no recourse

How it would work, step by step
