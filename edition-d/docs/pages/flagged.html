Twenty-five things this edition refused to guess

Each item is either unconfirmed, or a place where two official sources point differently. None of them blocks publication; all of them change the answer if they resolve one way rather than the other.

Nothing on this page is a finding about your account. These are gaps in the public record, or facts that only you or your bank can supply. They are listed here rather than filled in with a plausible number.

F01 · Two official instruments classify the same person differently

high

CN-02 Art. 52(2) defines 境内个人 as PRC citizens and foreigners who have resided continuously in China for a full year. CN-01 Art. 37 defines 境内个人 as holders of a PRC resident identity card and 境外个人 as passport holders. A US citizen who has lived in China for years is a 境内个人 under the Regulation and a 境外个人 under the Measures. Banks operate the Measures, because CN-01 Art. 28 makes the account category a determination from the identity document on file.

Why it matters. The annual USD 50,000 quota is written to apply to the 境内个人. Whether it applies to you turns on which document the bank holds, which is fact F02.

What would resolve it. A written statement from the bank of the account's subject category and the identity document on file.

Sources: CN-02 ↗ CN-01 ↗

F02 · Whether the bank will in practice treat a passport-holder's purchase as uncapped

high

CN-03 Art. 59 contains no annual amount limit for a 境外个人 purchasing FX against lawful current-account RMB income, and states that only a holder of a Foreigner Permanent Residence ID card uses the annual facilitation quota. CN-03 Art. 68 also gives the bank an express discretion to refuse a transaction where authenticity is in doubt.

Why it matters. This single point decides whether the balance moves in one operation or over eight years.

What would resolve it. A written pre-clearance from the branch's FX desk, naming Art. 59.

Sources: CN-03 ↗

F03 · The source of the funds is unknown

high

Every lawful route is defined by the character of the money: current-account income (M01), pre-emigration property (M03), inherited property (M04), property-sale proceeds (M05), trade revenue (M12). No fact about the actual balance was supplied.

Why it matters. Choosing the cheapest route before establishing the character of the funds is how people end up describing savings as something they are not.

What would resolve it. A tranche-by-tranche provenance file: payslips, tax certificates, sale contracts, probate documents, bank statements.

Sources: CN-01 ↗ CN-03 ↗ CN-04 ↗

F04 · Whether genuine emigrant status can be documented

high

The emigration transfer route is described for persons who moved abroad and owned the property before acquiring emigrant status, applying at the jurisdiction of their original household registration. Whether the household registration was cancelled, and when, is unknown.

Why it matters. It is the route built for exactly this problem, and it is unavailable without proof of emigration.

What would resolve it. The 户口注销证明 and the date of foreign permanent residence or naturalisation.

Sources: CN-04 ↗

F05 · Prior US filings are unknown

high

No information was supplied about past FBAR, Form 8938 or Schedule B filings for the Chinese account.

Why it matters. The largest quantified exposure in the whole picture is not the conversion at all: it is a willful FBAR penalty of the greater of USD 100,000 or 50% of the balance, per violation (US-02).

What would resolve it. A filing history check before any large inbound transfer.

Sources: US-02 ↗ US-03 ↗

F11 · The USD 200 threshold in §988(e)(2) applies to a balance this size

high

§988(e)(2) disapplies recognition of exchange-rate gain on a personal transaction only where the gain that would otherwise be recognised does not exceed USD 200. On a CNY balance of any size, the gain measured from the acquisition rate can exceed USD 200.

Why it matters. It means the common statement 'the US does not tax the FX gain on conversion' is true only up to USD 200 of gain. Above that, §988 gain is ordinary income.

What would resolve it. The acquisition dates and rates for each tranche of RMB, which is a records question, not a legal one.

Sources: US-01 ↗

F24 · The prohibited-route figures are statutory ranges, not assessments

high

The 30% and 100% figures are the maximum fine ranges in CN-02 Arts. 39 and 45. The actual fine depends on whether the case is 'serious', on confiscation, and on criminal referral. Splitting carries watch-list treatment under CN-03 Art. 62 rather than a percentage fine.

Why it matters. A range is the honest way to show exposure; a single number would be invented.

What would resolve it. SAFE enforcement decisions in comparable cases, which are not a prediction for any individual.

Sources: CN-02 ↗ CN-03 ↗

F25 · Crypto has no fixed penalty, only prohibition

high

CN-06 declares the activity illegal and to be banned, without specifying a civil fine for a retail participant. Related conduct can engage CN-02 penalties or criminal provisions, but no specific figure was verified.

Why it matters. The table for prohibited routes shows the CN-02 range as the exposure benchmark; it is not a crypto-specific penalty.

What would resolve it. Enforcement decisions, not text.

Sources: CN-06 ↗ CN-02 ↗

F06 · The full-amount-arrival fee is published in a corporate tariff, not a personal one

medium

The USD 15 / USD 25 full-amount figures appear in BOC's 2018 trade-finance price announcement. The personal e-banking tariff (CN-07) states the commission and cable fee but not the full-amount surcharge.

Why it matters. It is the second-largest wire cost line after the FX margin, and it may not apply to a personal remittance at all.

What would resolve it. A personal-channel quote at the counter, or BOC's personal service price list for the branch.

Sources: CN-09 ↗ CN-07 ↗

F07 · The US receiving-bank fee is bank-specific

medium

The model uses USD 15, taken from a Chase published fee schedule. Several US account tiers charge nothing for an incoming wire, and others charge more.

Why it matters. At USD 10,000 the difference between USD 0 and USD 25 is 0.25% of the transfer — larger than the bank's own commission.

What would resolve it. Your bank's current fee schedule.

Sources: BK-01 ↗ BK-02 ↗

F08 · Only one bank's tariff was verified, so this is not a bank comparison

medium

Bank of China's personal e-banking tariff was retrieved and verified in full. Comparable published personal tariffs for ICBC, CCB, ABC and CMB were not retrieved, so no cross-bank comparison is made and no figure is attributed to them.

Why it matters. A different bank may be cheaper; the reader should not read this as a recommendation of one institution.

What would resolve it. Each bank's published personal service price list.

Sources: CN-07 ↗

F09 · The verified tariff is old and explicitly subject to local variation

medium

The BOC personal e-banking fee table states rates effective 1 November 2014, and its note 1 says that where a separate agreement, a promotional price or a local fee difference applies, the agreed or locally published price governs.

Why it matters. The absolute fees in the tables may be out of date even though the published rule is unchanged.

What would resolve it. The branch's current displayed price list.

Sources: CN-07 ↗

F10 · The bank has a discretion to refuse

medium

CN-03 Art. 68 provides that the bank may refuse a transaction that violates FX rules or whose authenticity is in doubt, and must explain the reason and the appeal channel.

Why it matters. No document list in this edition guarantees that a particular branch will process a particular tranche.

What would resolve it. A written refusal or approval, which is itself useful evidence.

Sources: CN-03 ↗

F13 · The property gain is unknown, so the 20% is not computed

medium

The 20% rate applies to the transfer gain, not to the sale price or the balance. No purchase price, sale price or cost basis was supplied.

Why it matters. Presenting 20% of the balance would overstate the tax by a large multiple.

What would resolve it. The sale contract and the original acquisition cost.

Sources: CN-05 ↗

F14 · Notarisation, translation and valuation costs are blank

medium

CN-04 requires documents that are not notarised to be notarised, and applications may be made through an agent with a notarised power of attorney. No official price schedule for notarisation, translation or valuation was retrieved.

Why it matters. On the emigration and inheritance routes these fees can exceed the FX cost.

What would resolve it. A quote from a Chinese notary public and a certified translation provider.

Sources: CN-04 ↗

F15 · When the service-trade tax filing step is triggered

medium

CN-03 Art. 49 requires tax filing verification before a service-trade payment above USD 50,000 equivalent. Whether a particular remittance of a person's own previously taxed income is characterised as a service-trade payment is a branch-level determination.

Why it matters. It can add a step and a delay to the cheapest route.

What would resolve it. The branch's classification of the payment at the counter.

Sources: CN-03 ↗

F16 · No threshold in the text does not mean no documents at the counter

medium

CN-03 Art. 65 states that a 境外个人 remitting FX from an FX account does so on valid ID, without an amount threshold. CN-03 Art. 68 still allows the bank to demand evidence where authenticity is in doubt.

Why it matters. The difference between the rule and the counter is where delays happen.

What would resolve it. A pre-clearance call to the branch.

Sources: CN-03 ↗

F18 · Card currency-conversion fees were not sourced

medium

No official cardholder tariff stating the overseas transaction conversion rate was retrieved, so method M07 has blank cost cells.

Why it matters. Card spending may be the cheapest practical way to consume the balance, and its cost is entirely unknown here.

What would resolve it. The issuing bank's cardholder agreement and fee schedule.

Sources: none — that is the point

F19 · The rule for an individual's outbound RMB remittance was not retrieved

medium

Method M08 depends on cross-border RMB rules for individuals. The governing instrument was not retrieved and is not cited; the method carries no numbers and no steps.

Why it matters. If it is available, the offshore spread could be better at large size.

What would resolve it. The PBOC cross-border RMB settlement rules for individuals.

Sources: none — that is the point

F20 · Cash travel cost is blank and the structuring line is close

medium

The model counts only the FX margin and the number of trips; airfare, time and insurance are not priced. Repeated carriage just under reporting thresholds is itself a compliance risk distinct from the carriage rule.

Why it matters. The cash table looks cheap because its largest costs are not monetary or not priceable here.

What would resolve it. Actual travel costs, and legal advice before any repeated pattern.

Sources: CN-03 ↗ US-06 ↗

F21 · No official source confirms a fintech can execute this transfer

medium

CN-03 Chapter 6 licenses payment institutions for small-value current-account electronic payment connected to goods and services. It is not a framework for moving a personal balance offshore.

Why it matters. A provider's marketing page is not an authority to move capital.

What would resolve it. A named provider's SAFE payment-institution registration and its written confirmation that it will process the transaction.

Sources: CN-03 ↗

F12 · Whether China taxes interest on an ordinary deposit was not verified

low

CN-05 Art. 3(3) sets a flat 20% rate on interest, dividends and bonuses, while Art. 4 exempts interest on State treasury bonds and State-issued financial bonds. Whether a specific deposit-interest exemption applies was not verified.

Why it matters. It affects the tax on accrued interest, not the tax on the principal.

What would resolve it. The State Council's current deposit-interest rule.

Sources: CN-05 ↗

F17 · No purchase-only fee appears in the published tariff

low

The verified BOC personal tariff lists commissions for cross-border remittance but not for a purchase of FX that is not remitted. That is an absence of a line item, not proof that no fee exists.

Why it matters. It affects only method M06, where the whole claimed saving is the avoided wire fee.

What would resolve it. A branch quote for a purchase settled into a USD account.

Sources: CN-07 ↗

F22 · Outbound investment product rules were not retrieved

low

CN-01 Art. 17 states that a 境内个人 invests abroad through qualified domestic institutions, but no QDII product rule or eligibility test was retrieved.

Why it matters. It is an investment channel, not a way to get cash into your US account.

What would resolve it. CSRC and SAFE rules for the specific product.

Sources: CN-01 ↗

F23 · Corporate route costs and US corporate reporting were not analysed

low

CN-02 Art. 22 para. 3 gives the entitlement, but liquidation, audit and tax-clearance costs were not priced, and the US consequences of an interest in a foreign corporation (including Form 5471) were not analysed.

Why it matters. The route can be right where the money genuinely sits in a company, and is irrelevant otherwise.

What would resolve it. A China corporate lawyer and a US international tax adviser.
