Tax and penalties

What is taxed, what is not, and what the penalties are

Two questions are separated: whether the transfer is taxed (it is not), and whether the money was already taxable income (a different question, answered by the underlying facts).

China: the conversion is not in the tax base

The Individual Income Tax Law charges nine categories of income. Currency exchange is not one of them, and neither is the remittance of a person's own funds. The rate that people quote — 20% — is the flat rate on transfer of assets, interest, dividends and bonuses, leasing and incidental income. It falls on the gain or the income, never on the principal that is being moved.

United States: three separate questions

QuestionAnswerSource
Is moving my own money income?No provision treats a transfer between accounts you own as income. The only recognition rule that touches the conversion is §988.US-01 ↗
Is the FX gain on conversion taxable?Not if the transaction is personal and the gain that would otherwise be recognised does not exceed USD 200. Above USD 200 the exclusion does not apply and the gain is ordinary income.US-01 ↗
Do I have to report the account?Yes. An aggregate balance above USD 10,000 at any point in the calendar year requires an FBAR, and Form 8938 may also apply.US-03 ↗

The second row is the correction that matters: the earlier editions in this repository state the $200 rule accurately in one place and omit it in another. See R02 and F11. Any income the balance itself earns (interest, for example) is a separate worldwide-income question that this edition did not model.

The comparison that matters

At USD 400,000, the lawful route costs $933. A single willful FBAR violation can be $200,000. A finding of evading FX control can be $120,000 to $400,000. The fee is not the risk.
