Sale proceeds of your own Chinese property (overseas individual)

Route M05 · Property

If you are an overseas individual selling a Chinese home you own, the proceeds can be purchased and remitted with SAFE approval.

Status

Who can use it
A foreign citizen who owns and sells domestic commercial property.
Legal basis
S01 · PBOC Measures on Individual FX (2006) ↗ S02 · Detailed Rules on Individual FX (2007, as amended) ↗ S07 · Interim Measures on Outward Transfer of Personal Property (2004) ↗
Ceiling or gate
SAFE approval required (S01 Art.22). No amount ceiling stated in the instrument.
Tax treatment
Chinese individual income tax applies to the *gain* on the transfer of assets at a flat 20% (S08 Arts.2(8), 3(3)). That tax is owed because the property was sold, not because the money moves. The tax certificate is a precondition to the transfer (S07 Art.8(6)).

Step by step

  1. Sell the property and settle Chinese tax on the gain

    Transfer of assets is taxed at a flat 20% on the gain (S08 Art.3(3)).

    S08 · Individual Income Tax Law (official English text) ↗

  2. Obtain the tax certificate

    The tax authority at the property's location issues the certificate or paid-voucher (S07 Art.8(6)).

    S07 · Interim Measures on Outward Transfer of Personal Property (2004) ↗

  3. Apply to SAFE for approval to purchase FX and remit

    An overseas individual's sale proceeds may be purchased and remitted with SAFE approval (S01 Art.22; S02 Art.21).

    S01 · PBOC Measures on Individual FX (2006) ↗ S02 · Detailed Rules on Individual FX (2007, as amended) ↗

  4. Complete the bank purchase and wire with the approval

    The bank acts on the approval document and remits to your own account.

    S01 · PBOC Measures on Individual FX (2006) ↗

Advantages

  • A defined statutory route for exactly this asset.
  • The 20% is a tax on the gain, so the effective rate on the whole balance is much lower than 20%.

Disadvantages

  • SAFE approval plus a tax clearance certificate before the bank will act.
  • Other Chinese taxes on a property transfer (for example VAT and deed tax) were not verified in this session and are therefore not quantified - flag U06.
  • Gain must be computed from the original cost basis, which requires the original purchase documents.

Documents and evidence

  • Property title certificate and sale contract
  • Tax certificate or tax-paid voucher for the transfer
  • Passport and proof of the property being self-use as required
  • SAFE approval document
