RMB to Hong Kong, convert offshore (CNH), then wire to the US

Route M07 · Offshore

Move RMB to your own Hong Kong account, convert it there at offshore rates, and wire USD onward.

CostUnverifiedno official price located — see U08
Status
CONDITIONAL
Who can use it
Someone who already holds, or can open, a Hong Kong account in the same name and can satisfy the mainland bank that the RMB remittance is a permitted current-account payment.
Legal basis
S01 · PBOC Measures on Individual FX (2006) ↗ S03 · Regulation on Foreign Exchange Administration ↗ S02 · Detailed Rules on Individual FX (2007, as amended) ↗
Ceiling or gate
Not established by any instrument reviewed. Whether a mainland bank will remit RMB to a same-name Hong Kong account for an individual, and on what documents, is a bank and branch question - flag U02.
Tax treatment
No Chinese tax on the conversion; the same tax-on-the-event analysis applies. The offshore spread is a commercial price, not a rule.

No cost table is published for this route because no official price could be verified. Publishing a number here would be an estimate presented as a fact.

Step by step

  1. Open or confirm the Hong Kong account in the identical name

    An individual's cross-border RMB payment rests on authenticity review, and a same-name account is the usual basis a bank will accept (S03 Art.12).

    S03 · Regulation on Foreign Exchange Administration ↗

  2. Ask the mainland bank in advance whether it will send RMB to Hong Kong for you

    The instrument does not create an individual entitlement to send RMB offshore; the branch decides on the documents (flag U02).

    S01 · PBOC Measures on Individual FX (2006) ↗

  3. Convert in Hong Kong

    The offshore rate is a commercial quote; no official retail CNH spread was located (flag U03).

    no official source located — see flagged items

  4. Wire USD from Hong Kong to your US account

    A second wire means a second set of charges (flag U03).

    no official source located — see flagged items

Advantages

  • Offshore CNH spreads can be narrower than onshore retail spreads.
  • Once the money is offshore, later movement is outside Chinese FX control.

Disadvantages

  • Adds an intermediary bank, an intermediary jurisdiction and a second set of charges.
  • Requires a Hong Kong account and, in practice, proof of the same-name link.
  • The onshore leg is still a cross-border RMB payment subject to the same authenticity rules (S03 Art.12).
  • Two conversions or one conversion plus a wire means more spread, not less, unless the offshore spread is materially tighter - which is unverified here.

Documents and evidence

  • Passport and Hong Kong account proof
  • Same-name evidence
  • Source-of-funds documents for the mainland bank
