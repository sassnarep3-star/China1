Executive summary

The answer, the number and the documents — in one page.

This is the one-page version: the answer, the number, and the paperwork that decides whether you get the cheap route or the slow one.

The answer

Get the money characterised correctly, then buy USD at a Chinese bank and wire it. The cheapest lawful way to move a balance out of mainland China is a bank FX purchase followed by a SWIFT remittance, executed as an overseas individual against documented, lawful, current-account income earned in China. On a $400,000 balance the total cost of that route in this model is $940.95 — 0.235% of the money.

There is no Chinese tax on converting CNY into USD, and no US tax on moving your own after-tax principal. The tax you have been quoted is almost certainly one of three different things: (a) Chinese tax on the event that created the money, which was owed whether or not you moved it; (b) the commercial spread and wire fees; or (c) the penalty for a route that is not lawful.

What to do, in order

  1. Write down what created the RMB. Salary, fees, rent, interest, pension, property sale, inheritance, gift. Everything downstream depends on this one line.
  2. Collect the tax evidence. Chinese withholding records or tax-paid certificates for that income S02 · Detailed Rules on Individual FX (2007, as amended) ↗.
  3. Ask the branch, in writing, to process it as an overseas individual under the current-account guide S04 · Current-Account FX Business Guide 2020 ↗, quoting SAFE's own 2024 answer S05 · SAFE Tianjin Q&A (2024) on Art.59 ↗.
  4. Get the all-in quote: executed rate, commission, cable charge, and who pays the correspondent fee S09 · Bank of China FX rate board ↗.
  5. Wire to your own US account and keep the exchange memo — it is valid for 24 months for reconversion S02 · Detailed Rules on Individual FX (2007, as amended) ↗.
  6. File on the US side. FBAR above USD 10,000 in aggregate S10 · IRS - Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) ↗; Form 3520 Part IV for a foreign gift or bequest above USD 100,000 S11 · IRS - Gifts from a foreign person ↗.

For scale: the 20% cash fine on $10,000 would be $2,000 — about 21 times the cost of wiring the same money lawfully ($93.40).

Two honest caveats. First, the bank's commission and the correspondent deduction are not published by any regulator; they are modelling assumptions A2 and A3, disclosed on Cost tables and stress-tested there. Second, there is a genuine definitional conflict about whether a foreigner resident in China for a year is a domestic individual — answered differently by the 2008 Regulation and the 2006 Measures. It is published as U09.
