Cross-boundary Wealth Management Connect, southbound R14

粤港澳大湾区跨境理财通（南向通）

verified Low if used as designed; it does not solve the stated problem Not a repatriation route; listed for completeness and rejected
FieldValue
Legal basisRevised Implementation Rules for the Greater Bay Area Cross-boundary Wealth Management Connect pilot, issued January 2024 by the PBOC Guangdong Provincial Branch with the HKMA and the AMCM
How it worksAn eligible Greater Bay Area investor opens a remittance account locally and a paired investment account in Hong Kong or Macao. All cross-border remittances between the paired accounts are made in RMB through CIPS and are quota-managed in a closed loop. Principal and investment returns flow back through the same paired accounts.
Stated limitIndividual investor quota RMB 3,000,000, raised from RMB 1,000,000; RMB 1,500,000 each where the investor participates through both a bank and a securities company. The aggregate southbound quota is RMB 150 billion, shared with the northbound scheme. S33
Chinese taxNot applicable to the remittance; investment returns are taxed under normal rules.
US taxInvestment income is US taxable; the foreign accounts are reportable on FinCEN Form 114 and Form 8938.
DocumentationHousehold registration in the nine mainland GBA cities, or two years of continuous social insurance or individual income tax paid there; two years of investment experience; and either family financial net assets of RMB 1,000,000 at month end over the last three months, or family financial assets of RMB 2,000,000, or personal average annual income of RMB 400,000 over the last three years.

Pros

  • Pro. Moves RMB to Hong Kong through a fully sanctioned channel where offshore conversion to USD is possible
  • Pro. Quota was tripled in 2024 and securities companies were admitted to the pilot

Cons

  • Con. Not a repatriation channel. The paired-account design is closed loop and principal and returns are expected to flow back
  • Con. Cross-border remittances must be in RMB; foreign currency may not be remitted and conversion must occur in the offshore market
  • Con. Eligibility requires GBA household registration or two years of GBA social insurance or individual income tax, which a US citizen resident elsewhere cannot meet
  • Con. Only nine mainland cities participate

Sources: S33

Qualified domestic institutional investor products R26

合格境内机构投资者（QDII）产品

verified Low as designed; US PFIC reporting risk for the holder Not a repatriation route; listed for completeness and rejected
FieldValue
Legal basisMeasures Art.17; Detailed Rules Art.17
How it worksA domestic individual may invest in overseas fixed-income and equity financial products using FX or RMB through a bank, fund management company or other qualified domestic institutional investor holding a SAFE quota.
Stated limitBounded by the institution's SAFE-granted quota. The individual's own USD 50,000 facilitation quota is not consumed by a QDII purchase. S01 S02
Chinese taxInvestment returns are taxed under normal rules.
US taxReturns are US taxable. A foreign pooled fund may be a passive foreign investment company, bringing IRC s.1291 default treatment, excess distribution rules and Form 8621 filing, unless a qualified electing fund or mark-to-market election is available.
DocumentationSubscription through the licensed institution; risk disclosure and suitability assessment.

Pros

  • Pro. Exposure to offshore assets without using the personal facilitation quota
  • Pro. Fully sanctioned and simple to subscribe

Cons

  • Con. Not a repatriation channel: redemption proceeds return to the domestic account, so it moves value into offshore assets and back but does not deliver USD to the United States
  • Con. Quota availability is controlled by the institution and is frequently suspended when exhausted
  • Con. PFIC treatment can be punitive for a US holder and Form 8621 is required
  • Con. Product range is limited to what the institution is licensed to sell

Sources: S01 S02
