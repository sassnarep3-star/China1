The US-China income tax treaty

Signed at Beijing on 30 April 1984, effective 1 January 1987. Article numbering differs from the OECD model, so the citations below matter.

The treaty gives almost nothing on rates and everything on relief. Its 10% caps on dividends, interest and royalties are identical to the domestic reduced rate already provided by Art.91 of the Chinese EIT Implementation Regulations, so there is no withholding reduction to claim. Its value to a US person is Art.22, the foreign tax credit. S30 S14

Two points that matter for a US citizen

  • The saving clause. Protocol para.2 preserves the right of the United States to tax its citizens and residents under domestic law. The Technical Explanation confirms the United States may in any event tax dividends, interest, royalties and gains derived by US citizens. The treaty cannot be used to escape US tax. S30
  • Stale tax names. Art.2 names the individual income tax, the income tax concerning joint ventures with Chinese and foreign investment, the income tax concerning foreign enterprises and the local income tax. Those predate the 2008 Enterprise Income Tax Law and the 2018 Individual Income Tax Law amendment. Coverage of the current taxes relies on Art.2(2), which extends the Agreement to substantially similar taxes imposed after signature. S30 F11
