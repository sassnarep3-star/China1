Getting yuan out of China into US dollars

For a US citizen with yuan in a mainland bank account: what each lawful route costs at every USD 10,000 step up to USD 400,000, what it needs, and where the tax risk really sits.

The short answer

  1. Converting is not what gets taxed. No Chinese tax on conversion or remittance appears in the foreign-exchange rules. What the bank asks for is proof that the yuan is lawful and that Chinese tax on it was paid S01 S04.
  2. A documented bank wire is the cheapest verified route from USD 20,000 up. At Bank of China's tariff the all-in cost is $124.50 at USD 20,000, $311.32 at USD 100,000 and $940.90 at USD 400,000 S32 S30 S40.
  3. At USD 10,000, Wise is cheaper ($65.18 against $93.49), but only for salary backed by Chinese tax records S39 S37.
  4. The real tax exposure is elsewhere: unpaid Chinese income tax on the money, a US currency gain when the yuan has risen since you got it, and US penalties if the Chinese account was never reported S18 S52 S50.
  5. A foreign passport holder has no USD 50,000 quota. That quota is for holders of Chinese ID or a Chinese permanent-residence ID S14 S01.
