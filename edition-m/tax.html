Tax and penalties: the full breakdown

Every layer that takes money on the way from a Chinese yuan account to a US dollar account, in China and in the United States, and what each penalty is.

What the penalty really is

  • The conversion itself: a bank margin of 0.21% and fixed fees. No tax on it was found in the Chinese rules S30 S32 S04.
  • China: income tax on the money if it was never paid, at 3%–45% for salary-type income or 20% for interest, dividends, rent, asset sales and windfalls. The bank wants the tax voucher before it sells you dollars S18 S01.
  • United States: a currency gain if the yuan is worth more dollars now than when you got it. Yuan acquired at the 2024 average rate shows a gain of $699.46 per USD 10,000 converted today S52 S53.
  • United States: reporting penalties if the Chinese account was never reported, up to USD 16,536 per missed FBAR when not willful S58 S50.

US currency gain at each amount

Gain or loss in dollars from converting today, if the yuan was acquired at the IRS yearly average rate for that year. Formula: yuan ÷ 6.6851 (today's selling rate) − yuan ÷ that year's rate. This is the gain, not the tax; the tax is the gain times your own rate. A personal transaction with a gain of USD 200 or less is not recognised. See F22.
