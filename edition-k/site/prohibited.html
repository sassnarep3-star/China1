Prohibited routes — recognition only

These are listed because refusing them is part of the answer to "how do I get the money out". No operational instructions are published for any of them, by design: the point of this page is to make the cost legible, not the method.

Why this page exists. The complaint that started this project was that converting would cost "a considerable amount in taxes". The lawful conversion cost at $400,000 is $915.77 — 0.229%. The only way to reach a five- or six-figure penalty on a lawful balance is to use one of the routes below or to fail to report. The penalty is not a conversion fee; it is a fine.

What the regulation actually says

逃汇…处逃汇金额30%以下的罚款；情节严重的，处逃汇金额30%以上等值以下的罚款 (Art. 39); 私自买卖外汇、变相买卖外汇…处违法金额30%以下的罚款；情节严重的，处违法金额30%以上等值以下的罚款 (Art. 45); 违反规定携带外汇出入境…可以处违法金额20%以下的罚款 (Art. 42)

Verbatim from S08. Read the full text before relying on any summary, including this one.

The US side does not cancel the Chinese side

A route that hides the money from Chinese authorities creates a US problem rather than solving one: the account remains FBAR-reportable, the income remains taxable, and a false answer on a US filing is a separate violation. There is no arrangement in which unreported money becomes cheaper by moving it.

What to do instead

Every prohibited route in this list is a shortcut around documentation. The lawful answer to missing documentation is to obtain it — a tax clearance, a notarised inheritance entitlement, a SAFE approval, an employment record — or to choose a route whose documents you can actually produce.
