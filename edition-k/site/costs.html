4. Worked example: $10,000, step by step

  1. Target: $10,000.00 landed in your US account.
  2. CNY required at BOC's selling rate: $10,000.00 × 6.6851 = ¥66,851.00 (S02).
  3. FX spread cost: $10,000.00 × 0.210% = $20.99.
  4. Commission: 0.1% of ¥66,851.00 = ¥66.85, which is above the CNY 50 floor and below the CNY 260 cap, so it is charged in full: ¥66.85 = $10.00.
  5. Cable fee: CNY 150 = $22.44.
  6. US receiving: $15.00.
  7. Total: $20.99 + $10.00 + $22.44 + $15.00 = $68.42, or 0.684% of the amount.
  8. You receive $9,931.58 — before any correspondent-bank deduction, which is not published.

5. Worked example: $400,000, step by step

  1. CNY required: $400,000.00 × 6.6851 = ¥2,674,040.00.
  2. FX spread cost: $839.44.
  3. Commission: 0.1% of ¥2,674,040.00 would be ¥2,674.04, but the cap is CNY 260, so you pay ¥260.00 = $38.89.
  4. Cable $22.44 and US receiving $15.00.
  5. Total: $915.77 = 0.229%. Note the percentage is roughly a third of the $10,000 case, purely because four fixed fees are spread over 40 times the amount.
  6. You still have to satisfy the remittance entitlement for the whole ¥2,674,040.00. Costing is the easy half; documentation is the hard half. See Steps.

6. Why the bank's rate can look better than the official mid-rate

On 2026-11-10 the PBOC central parity was 6.7167 CNY per USD (S01) while BOC's own selling rate at 17:39 was 6.6851 (S02). The bank's ask was therefore below the official reference published that morning. That is a timing effect, not a discount, and not a cost either.

This edition deliberately does not compute the FX cost as "bank rate minus PBOC mid". Doing so would mix a 09:25 reference with a 17:39 live quote and would produce a number that is part market move and part spread. The spread is instead measured inside one snapshot, where both quotes are stamped 2026-11-10 17:39:58. The mid-rate comparison is disclosed here and logged as an open item on the flagged page.
