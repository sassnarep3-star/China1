Step-by-step procedures

The sequence you would actually follow, document by document. Nothing here asks you to sign anything untrue, and nothing here tells you to split a transaction to stay under a limit.

Before step one, in every route: establish the character of the money and keep the evidence in one folder — the contract or payroll record, the China tax records, the bank statements showing the money landing, and the identity documents. Banks decide on documents, not on explanations. The 17 routes below are ordered from the ordinary to the exceptional.

Bank of China wire: buy USD, send to your US account Lawful

Walk into (or log into) BOC, convert CNY to USD inside your FX account, then telegraphic-transfer it to your own US account. Up to USD 50,000/day outward from an FX savings account is ID-only; above that the bank must see genuine-transaction documents.

  1. Confirm which bank holds the CNY and whether the account is a personal RMB account or an FX account, because the paperwork differs.
  2. Assemble proof of the character of the money: salary slips and China tax records, a property sale contract and tax clearance, an inheritance notarisation, or the SAFE approval letter if you already have one.
  3. At the bank (or in the app), request 购汇 (FX purchase) of USD up to the amount needed, presenting your passport and, if above the annual total, the supporting documents listed in SAFE Huifa [2007] No.1 Art. 10/12.
  4. Request 境外汇款 (outward remittance) to your own US account, giving the SWIFT/BIC, your US account number and the exact beneficiary name matching your passport.
  5. Pay the commission (0.1%, min CNY 50, max CNY 260) plus the CNY 150 cable fee; keep the receipt and the 购汇/汇款 slips.
  6. Record the CNY amount, the rate applied and the USD actually credited, because the difference is what you will report if the FX gain exceeds USD 200.
  7. File the US side: FBAR if the Chinese accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any point in the year, Form 8938 if the thresholds in the table are met, and Form 1116 for any Chinese tax on income also taxed by the US.

Sources: S01 S02 S03 S05 S08 · cost basis: Priced end-to-end from BOC's own published board and tariff plus a Chase receiving charge. Intermediary bank fees are excluded because no first-party figure was found.

HSBC China wire (Premier / Advance account) Conditional

The same legal route as a BOC wire, run through HSBC China. Its published commission band is wider (min CNY 100, max CNY 500) and the cable fee is CNY 120. Premier status changes some fees and unlocks HSBC Global Transfers.

  1. Confirm your HSBC customer tier, because the waiver schedule is tier-specific.
  2. Prepare the same underlying-transaction evidence as for any bank wire.
  3. If your destination account is also HSBC, ask whether the transfer can be routed as an HSBC Global Transfer, which the tariff lists as free for Premier.
  4. Otherwise request an outward telegraphic transfer: 0.1% commission, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable.
  5. Keep the debit advice showing the rate used and the charges, and reconcile it against the USD credited in the US.
  6. Complete the US filings (FBAR, 8938, 1116) on the same basis as any other rail.

Sources: S04 S05 · cost basis: Commission and cable are priced from HSBC's own tariff. HSBC's FX spread is not published in that document, so the all-in figure for this rail is deliberately left blank.

HSBC Global Transfer between your own HSBC accounts Conditional

A same-name transfer from HSBC China to your HSBC account in another country. The service fee is waived for Premier, but the money still has to be lawfully remittable and still gets converted at HSBC's rate.

  1. Open or confirm the destination HSBC account and complete same-name verification.
  2. Confirm the transfer qualifies under the underlying SAFE entitlement for the amount.
  3. Initiate the Global Transfer in the HSBC app from the CNY account, selecting the destination account.
  4. Accept the quoted conversion rate and check the fee line shows zero before confirming.
  5. Reconcile the USD credited against the CNY debited, and file US reports as usual.

Sources: S04 S05 · cost basis: Service fee is zero for Premier by tariff; the FX spread is not verified for HSBC, so the all-in total is blank rather than guessed.

Wise CNY-to-USD transfer Conditional

A licensed payment provider route out of China. Eligibility is narrow and specific: not a mainland Chinese national, must have worked in China, must hold a China tax record showing tax paid within the last three years, may only send to an account you own, and is capped at CNY 300,000 per transfer and at net salary overall.

  1. Check the three eligibility gates on Wise's own page: nationality, China work history, and a tax record showing tax paid within the last three years.
  2. Create the Wise account and complete identity verification, which the guide says takes one to two working days.
  3. Add your US account as a recipient in your own name.
  4. Start a transfer of no more than CNY 300,000, upload the tax record, and read the quoted fee and rate before funding.
  5. Fund the transfer from your Chinese bank account or Alipay as instructed.
  6. Keep the Wise receipt and the tax record with your US return file supporting the FBAR/8938 figures.

Sources: S17 S05 · cost basis: Not priced. Wise states the fee differs by delivery method and is only shown when a transfer is set up, so any number here would be invented.

Alipay / WeChat cross-border remittance Conditional

Mobile-wallet remittance into an overseas account, usually delivered through a licensed partner. Wise's guide lists a CNY 50,000 per-transfer cap for sending from Alipay, and notes that WeChat and Alipay receipts into China are capped by recipient too.

  1. Confirm the specific wallet's outbound remittance function is enabled for your account and nationality.
  2. Verify identity and link the destination account in your own name.
  3. Attempt a first small transfer and record the quoted rate and fee.
  4. Keep the transaction record; do not split a large sum into many wallet transfers to stay under caps, which is the conduct the authenticity rule targets.
  5. File US reports as usual.

Sources: S17 S05 · cost basis: No published tariff located, so no cost is computed.

Overseas-person route: remit lawful RMB income earned in China Lawful

If you are an overseas individual with lawful current-account RMB income in China, SAFE Huifa [2007] No.1 Art. 13 lets you buy FX against your ID plus transaction evidence including tax proof. This is the clause that most directly fits a foreign national who earned money in China.

  1. Obtain the China individual income tax records covering the income you want to remit.
  2. Obtain the employment or service contract and the proof of payment into your Chinese account.
  3. Present the package to the bank under the above-total current-account procedure.
  4. Complete the FX purchase and outward remittance in the same visit so the audit trail is unbroken.
  5. Retain copies for the US return file.

Sources: S05 S10 S15 · cost basis: Same bank tariff as the wire rails; not separately computed.

Emigrant property transfer (移民财产转移) Lawful

The route built for exactly this problem: a person who emigrated from mainland China applies to SAFE to remit the lawful assets they owned before acquiring emigrant status. Since 2014 it no longer has to be released in stages, and the CNY 500,000 central filing threshold is gone.

  1. Confirm you meet the emigrant definition and assemble proof of settlement abroad and cancellation of Chinese household registration.
  2. Assemble property-rights or asset-ownership evidence for everything you intend to transfer.
  3. Obtain the tax clearance or tax-payment certificate from the competent tax authority.
  4. File the written application and the two prescribed forms with the SAFE branch at your former household registration location.
  5. On approval, take the approval letter and the capital-account approval document to the designated bank.
  6. Buy FX and remit to your own account abroad; the FX bank must remit directly and may not hand you cash.
  7. Keep the approval documents permanently: they are the explanation for a large inbound wire if the US bank ever asks.

Sources: S06 S07 S05 S10 · cost basis: Government approval is not a fee; the bank tariff applies at the remittance step. No separate government charge was published in the sources read.

Inheritance transfer (继承财产转移) Lawful

A foreign citizen who has lawfully inherited a Chinese estate applies at the decedent's former household-registration SAFE branch to liquidate and remit it. Since 2014, inheritances from different decedents can be combined into one application.

  1. Obtain the inheritance entitlement documents and, where required, notarisation.
  2. Obtain the decedent's property-rights documents and tax clearance.
  3. Apply to the SAFE branch at the decedent's former household registration with the prescribed forms.
  4. On approval, take the approval and capital-account approval documents to the bank.
  5. Liquidate the assets inside China as required, then purchase FX and remit to your own overseas account.
  6. File the US side: Form 3520 may apply if you are reporting a foreign bequest, and FBAR/8938 follow the account balances.

Sources: S06 S07 S14 · cost basis: Not priced: fees depend on the estate, the notary and the local SAFE branch, and no first-party fee schedule was located.

Sell Chinese property, then remit the proceeds Conditional

Convert the asset into CNY through a real sale, pay the transfer taxes, then use a bank or entitlement route to move the proceeds. This is how most large legitimate sums actually leave, and it is also where the 20% figure that people call a 'conversion penalty' really comes from.

  1. Sell through a registered transaction with a proper sale contract and title transfer.
  2. Settle the transfer taxes and obtain the tax payment certificate.
  3. Deposit the net proceeds into your Chinese bank account and keep the chain of documents.
  4. Choose the remittance basis: current-account documents, or the emigrant/inheritance approval route.
  5. Buy FX and remit to your own US account, keeping the rate and fee slips.
  6. Report on the US side: the sale is a capital transaction, so basis and gain must be computed in USD.

Sources: S10 S05 S15 · cost basis: Not priced: the tax depends on basis and holding period, and the transaction taxes were not verified to a first-party rate table in this edition.

Sell China-listed shares and remit proceeds Conditional

If you hold China-listed securities, selling them creates a CNY balance that then has to pass the same remittance gate. The securities side has its own account and registration rules that vary by whether you are a domestic or overseas investor.

  1. Confirm the account type and whether the position can be sold by you without a new registration.
  2. Sell and settle through the broker, keeping the contract notes.
  3. Obtain the tax position in writing from the competent tax authority if the gain is taxable.
  4. Take the brokerage and tax documents to the bank and request the remittance basis that fits.
  5. Buy FX and remit to your own account, then report the disposition in the US.

Sources: S10 S15 · cost basis: Not priced: broker commission, stamp duty and tax were not verified to first-party sources here.

Employee stock plan / equity award proceeds Conditional

SAFE Huifa [2007] No.1 Art. 18 addresses foreign-listed employee stock ownership and option plans: the FX business is handled through the employer or a domestic agent after SAFE approval, and sale proceeds can be repatriated to a dedicated account and then settled or paid into the individual's FX savings account.

  1. Ask the employer's equity or payroll team whether a SAFE registration covers your plan.
  2. Get the vesting, sale and withholding statements.
  3. Process the FX business through the employer or the designated domestic agent as Art. 18 requires.
  4. Repatriate the proceeds to the dedicated account, then settle or transfer to your FX savings account.
  5. Remit onward to the US and report the income and any gain on your US return.

Sources: S05 S10 · cost basis: Not priced.

Surrender a Chinese insurance policy and remit proceeds Conditional

Cancelling a cash-value policy releases a surrender value. SAFE's rules allow insurance proceeds and claims to be settled or deposited into an FX savings account in the cases they describe; the surrender itself may create a taxable gain or a loss.

  1. Obtain the policy terms, surrender value illustration and the insurer's payment certificate.
  2. Surrender and receive the proceeds into your Chinese account.
  3. Take the contract and payment certificate to the bank as evidence of the funds' character.
  4. Buy FX and remit, then report any gain or loss on the US return.

Sources: S05 · cost basis: Not priced: surrender value depends on the contract.

Dividends from a Chinese company you own Conditional

A company you own declares a dividend and remits it after tax. This is a business route with its own registrations, board resolutions and tax filing, not a personal workaround.

  1. Confirm the company has distributable profits per audited accounts.
  2. Pass the profit-distribution resolution required by the company's constitution.
  3. Complete the tax filing and withholding on the dividend.
  4. Present the resolution, accounts and tax filing to the bank for the remittance.
  5. Report the dividend on the US return with a foreign tax credit claim.

Sources: S10 S08 S15 · cost basis: Not priced.

Liquidate or reduce a foreign-invested enterprise Conditional

If the money entered China as registered foreign capital, it can leave on liquidation or capital reduction after tax and registration steps. SAFE's capital-account rules govern the process.

  1. Take the corporate decision to liquidate or reduce capital and complete creditor procedures.
  2. Settle taxes and obtain the tax clearance.
  3. Complete the FDI registration change or cancellation with the competent authority.
  4. Process the remittance through the bank under the capital-account rules.
  5. Report the return of capital and any gain in the US.

Sources: S08 S07 · cost basis: Not priced.

UnionPay card spending and ATM withdrawals abroad Conditional

Using a Chinese debit or credit card overseas converts at the card scheme's rate. It moves consumption, not capital, and the annual cash-withdrawal controls are separate.

  1. Check the issuer's overseas use and cash-withdrawal settings before travelling.
  2. Use the card for genuine purchases rather than as a transfer mechanism.
  3. Keep statements so any FX gain or loss can be computed.
  4. Do not use ATM withdrawals to accumulate cash for a large carry.

Sources: S13 S19 · cost basis: Not priced: scheme and issuer fees were not verified to a first-party tariff.

Carry physical cash out of China Conditional

Legal only in small amounts with the right permit. Under USD 5,000 equivalent needs no carrying certificate; USD 5,000 to 10,000 needs a bank-issued certificate; above USD 10,000 is in principle not permitted and requires an exceptional SAFE permit. On the US side, over USD 10,000 must be declared on FinCEN 105.

  1. If the amount is under the equivalent of USD 5,000, carry it and declare it if asked.
  2. If it is between USD 5,000 and 10,000, obtain the carrying certificate from the FX bank before departure.
  3. If it is above USD 10,000, do not carry it without an exceptional SAFE permit.
  4. On arrival in the US, file FinCEN 105 for anything over USD 10,000.
  5. Deposit the cash and keep the declaration receipt.

Sources: S09 S19 S08 · cost basis: Not priced: the FX purchase leg uses the bank's cash rate, which differs from the remittance rate; the delivery cost is your own travel.

Receive a gift from a Chinese relative Conditional

A relative in China gifts you money and remits it. The US does not tax the receipt, but Form 3520 Part IV is required once aggregate gifts from a nonresident individual exceed USD 100,000 in a year. The Chinese side still has to satisfy the remittance rules.

  1. Document the relationship and the donative intent in writing.
  2. Have the relative complete the lawful Chinese remittance with the correct purpose code.
  3. Track the aggregate received from that donor and related parties across the tax year.
  4. File Form 3520 Part IV by the due date if the aggregate exceeds USD 100,000.
  5. Keep the remittance slips and the gift letter with the return.

Sources: S14 S16 · cost basis: Same bank tariff as the wire rails.

What to do on the US side, once the money lands

  1. Record the CNY debited, the rate applied and the USD credited. That record is the basis for any FX gain computation.
  2. Check FBAR: did the aggregate of your Chinese accounts exceed $10,000 at any point in the calendar year? If yes, file FinCEN 114 by 15 April, with an automatic extension to 15 October (S11).
  3. Check Form 8938 against the thresholds for your filing status and residence (S12).
  4. If the money is a gift or bequest rather than your own funds, check Form 3520 Part IV above $100,000 aggregate (S14).
  5. If China taxed income that the US also taxes, claim the credit on Form 1116 rather than paying twice (S15).
  6. If you physically carried more than $10,000, file FinCEN 105 at the border (S19).
