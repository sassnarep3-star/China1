The conversion penalty, decomposed

"Penalty" is used for at least four different things in this problem. Only one of them is a cost you pay at the counter, and it is measurable to four decimal places. Separating them is what makes the rest of the decision tractable.

0.2099%1. Spread (measured)The half of the bank's bid-ask bracket you cross. This is the conversion cost.
CNY 4102. Fixed charges (published)CNY 260 commission + CNY 150 cable at the cap, plus USD 15 at the US bank.
8 years3. Policy cost (if the quota applies)USD 50,000 a year turns one transfer into eight, each with its own fixed charges and rate risk.
0%4. Conversion tax (none)No PRC income-tax category covers a currency conversion (S10 Art. 2).

1. What the bank's own board actually proves

BOC publishes both sides of its bracket. Crossing half of it is the entire conversion cost at the counter, because the tariff adds no separate conversion commission (S11 item 210107011). Both facts are on the same page as the rate, so this is arithmetic on a single official source.

The trap. On 2026-11-10 the official central parity was CNY 6.7167 per USD while BOC sold dollars at 6.6851. Anyone computing "penalty versus mid-market" from the parity would report a negative penalty of 0.2008%. The fixing is set once each morning and is not a rate a customer can obtain. This edition therefore never uses it as a benchmark.

5. What is not a conversion penalty

  • PRC income tax. Not one of the nine categories in S10 Art. 2 is a currency conversion. The tax you may owe is on the money's source, and it is owed whether or not you convert.
  • US income tax on principal. Moving your own taxed money is not income. The currency leg is section 988, with the USD 200 personal-transaction rule (S15 (e)).
  • The CFETS parity gap. Not obtainable, so not a cost. See F20.
  • A "remittance tax". The 1% US remittance-transfer excise tax discussed in prior editions applies to specified remittances sent from the United States, not to money arriving from China. It is not re-verified in this session and is not included in any figure here.
The penalties that are real. PRC administrative fines reach 30% of the amount for evasion or disguised dealing (S01 Arts. 39, 45); US FBAR maximums are USD 16,536 non-willful and USD 165,353 willful per report (S19); a property gain is taxed at 20% (S10 Art. 3(3)). Compared with these, a 0.2099% spread is noise - which is exactly why the paperwork, not the rate, is the thing to optimise.
