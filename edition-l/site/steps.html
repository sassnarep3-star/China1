Step by step: exactly how each route works

The order matters more than the tactics. Four of the five phases below produce no transfer at all if the previous one was skipped - that is the point of them, because an undocumented purchase is the one thing that turns a 0.2099% spread into a 30% fine.

Phase 0 - facts you must be able to state

  1. Who the bank says you are: domestic individual (PRC resident ID) or overseas individual (passport) - S02 Arts. 2, 37; F01.
  2. What the balance is: salary, service income, property proceeds, inheritance, dividend, gift, or accumulated savings. The route follows the item, not your preference.
  3. When and how it was taxed in China, with the voucher or withholding record to prove it - S04.
  4. Whether the account has been reported in the US: FBAR above USD 10,000 aggregate (S16) and Form 8938 at its own thresholds (S17).
  5. Where it is going: your own account (required for Wise, S21), a joint account, or someone else's (which changes the analysis).

Phase 1 - classify the money before you ask for a price

  1. Ask the branch to confirm the current-account item and the exact document list in writing.
  2. If the answer is 'salary', collect the employment contract, the income proof and the tax record - the 2026 SAFE answer describes exactly this file (S04).
  3. If the answer is 'property', stop: the property route needs a completed sale, PRC tax paid, and SAFE approval before any purchase (S02 Art. 22; F12).
  4. If the answer is 'inheritance', the file runs through the SAFE office at the deceased's household-registration place (S05 Art. 5).
  5. If the money cannot be tied to any item, no lawful purchase exists for it. That is the finding, not a step to work around.

Phase 2 - get comparable, executable quotes

  1. Ask for the amount you will actually receive, not the rate: BOC charges 0.1% capped at CNY 260 plus CNY 150 cable (S11); HSBC charges 0.1% capped at CNY 500 plus CNY 120 (S13); Bank of America charges USD 15 to receive (S14).
  2. Ask each bank to quote SHA and OUR separately, so the correspondent deduction is either visible or priced (S11 note 4; S13 item 2.2.4.1).
  3. Take all quotes on the same day: the same board moved about 0.1% within one session (F19).
  4. Write down the timestamp of each quote. A quote without a time cannot be compared.

Phase 3 - execute one wire, then check what arrived

  1. Instruct the transfer to your own account and keep the purchase slip and SWIFT reference.
  2. Reconcile the amount received against the quote: any shortfall is the intermediary deduction that no source publishes (F18).
  3. Record the total cost as a percentage of the amount: at USD 100,000 the known total is about 0.2862% (see the ladder).
  4. If the shortfall is material, reprice the next wire with OUR.

Phase 4 - reporting, in the same year

  1. FBAR (FinCEN 114) due 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October, filed with FinCEN not the IRS (S16).
  2. Form 8938 with the return if the threshold is met (S17).
  3. Form 3520 Part IV if a foreign person gave you more than USD 100,000 in aggregate in the year (S18).
  4. Keep the bank statements, the purchase slip and the tax voucher for at least five years (S16 record-keeping guidance).

If the balance is property or inheritance, the sequence is different

  1. Realise the asset first, lawfully. An emigrant transfer moves property you owned before emigrating; an inheritance transfer moves what you lawfully inherited (S05 Art. 2).
  2. Settle the PRC tax on the disposal. Property transfer is 20% of proceeds minus original value and reasonable expenses (S10 Arts. 3(3), 6(5)); the tax certificate is a required document (S05 Art. 8(6)).
  3. Apply to the right SAFE office. Emigrant: the office at your original household-registration place. Inheritance: the office where the deceased was registered (S05 Art. 5). Combining several deceased persons' estates is now allowed (S06 VI.3).
  4. Collect identity and status proof. For emigration this includes cancellation of PRC household registration and proof of settlement abroad (S05 Art. 8(4)) - the requirement that trips most people up (F10).
  5. Remit to your own overseas account. The bank must not pay cash inside China after the sale (S05 Art. 11).
  6. Remit once or in instalments within the approved amount. The old 50% / half / balance staging was cancelled in 2014 (S06 VI.2), and notarisation of property and agency documents is no longer required (S06 VI.4).

What to ask, in writing

  • "Please confirm my classification under PBOC Order [2006] No. 3 and the document list for my balance."
  • "Please quote the amount my US account will receive, for SHA and for OUR, at today's rate, with the time of the quote."
  • "Please state any intermediary or correspondent charge that may be deducted and whether it is included in the OUR price."
  • "If I repeat this transfer, will the same file be accepted or must I resubmit?"

Red flags that change the route immediately

  • Anyone offering to use a relative's or friend's quota for your money - that is the splitting prohibition in S02 Art. 7.
  • Anyone offering to route through virtual currency inside China - prohibited financial activity (S09 Section 1).
  • Anyone offering a matched transfer with an offshore counterparty - private/disguised dealing, fine band up to 30% with confiscation (S01 Art. 45).
  • A "full arrival" promise without a written price - BOC's is USD 15 or 25 depending on the correspondent relationship (S11 note 5), HSBC pre-collects CNY 200 (S13).
