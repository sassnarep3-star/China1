The conversion penalty, broken down

There is no PRC tax on converting CNY into USD. Zero. The cost you are feeling is not a tax on conversion — it is a combination of (a) the capital-control quota wall, (b) retail FX spreads, and (c) US information-reporting exposure on the account while it sits there. Those are three different problems with three different fixes.

Read this first. "Penalty" is used loosely in most material on this topic. Here it is split into six layers that behave completely differently: a statutory tax rate, a commercial spread, a US income-tax rule, a US information-reporting penalty, a PRC enforcement fine, and a relief path. Conflating them is what makes the problem look unsolvable.

A. PRC tax on the conversion itself

ItemValueBasis (verbatim rule + official source)
Individual income tax on exchanging CNY for USD 0% IIT Law Art. 2 lists the nine categories of taxable income. Foreign-exchange gain and currency conversion are not among them. Art. 3(3) sets the flat 20% rate for interest/dividends, property leasing, property transfer and incidental income only.
Source: S05
Individual income tax on savings deposit interest 0% (provisionally exempt) From 2008-10-09 the State Council provisionally exempted individual income tax on savings deposit interest, covering both RMB and foreign-currency savings. Confirmed by MOF briefing and by the STA's own annotation to Guo Shui Fa [1999] No.180.

Tax clearance certificate needed to remit Required for emigrant / inheritance transfers The 2004 No.16 Measures make a tax payment certificate or tax clearance certificate a mandatory attachment for both 移民转移 (Art. 8(6)) and 继承转移 (Art. 9(5)). This is a documentary precondition, not a tax on the conversion.
Source: S02
Exit tax on cancelling PRC household registration unverified IIT Law Art. 10(5) and Art. 13 require a taxpayer cancelling PRC household registration on emigrating to settle tax before cancellation. Whether this amounts to an exit tax, and at what rate, is not established by the sources retrieved. Left blank deliberately.

Synthesis

  1. The premise in the question — that converting CNY to USD triggers a large PRC tax — does not survive the primary sources. The PRC taxes nine categories of income and FX conversion is not one of them; savings interest has been exempt since 2008. A lawful conversion through a lawful channel therefore carries a 0% PRC conversion tax.
  2. What actually costs money is the quota wall. USD 50,000 per person per year means a USD 1,000,000 balance needs 20 person-years of quota. The two general-purpose capital-account exits — emigrant transfer and inheritance transfer — are the only lawful ways to move an entire balance, and emigrant transfer requires giving up PRC household registration.
  3. The US side is where a genuine large liability can appear, and it is a reporting liability, not a tax liability. FBAR exposure runs to USD 16,536 per report non-willfully and USD 165,353 or 50% of the balance willfully. A single missed year on a USD 500,000 account can exceed the entire tax the reader is worried about.
  4. Watch-list mechanics matter more than the fine for most people: three years without a facilitation quota converts a one-year problem into a three-year one.
  5. Every prohibited route converts a tax question into a criminal one, and the statutory range is 30%–100% of the amount — several times any plausible tax. That asymmetry is the single most important finding in this dataset.
