  • Two and only two categories of emigrant/inheritance property transfer: 移民转移 and 继承转移

S17 IRS — Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) Internal Revenue Service (US) primary 2026-11-07
  • A US person with a financial interest in, or signature authority over, a foreign financial account must file FinCEN Form 114 if the aggregate value of the foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any time during the calendar year
  • Whether the account produced taxable income has no effect on whether it is a foreign financial account for FBAR purposes
  • Due 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October; not filed with the federal income tax return
  • Records must generally be kept for five years from the FBAR due date
  • Civil penalty maximums in 31 USC are adjusted annually for inflation (31 CFR 1010.821)

S20 IRS Instructions for Form 8938 Instructions for Form 8938 (Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets) Internal Revenue Service (US) primary 2026-11-07
  • Living in the US, unmarried: reporting threshold is more than USD 50,000 at year end, or more than USD 75,000 at any time during the year
  • Living in the US, married filing jointly: more than USD 100,000 at year end, or more than USD 150,000 at any time
  • Living outside the US, unmarried: more than USD 200,000 at year end, or more than USD 300,000 at any time
  • Living outside the US, married filing jointly: more than USD 400,000 at year end, or more than USD 600,000 at any time
  • Presence-abroad test: bona fide residence abroad for an uninterrupted period including an entire tax year, or 330 full days abroad in any 12 consecutive months ending in the tax year
  • Failure-to-file penalty of USD 10,000; continuing failure adds USD 10,000 per 30-day period after a 90-day notice, capped at USD 50,000 additional
  • If no income tax return is required for the year, Form 8938 is not required even above the threshold
  • Foreign financial accounts held directly are 'specified foreign financial assets'; foreign real estate held directly is not
S21 IRS Instructions for Form 3520 Instructions for Form 3520 (Annual Return To Report Transactions With Foreign Trusts and Receipt of Certain Foreign Gifts) Internal Revenue Service (US) primary 2026-11-07
  • Part IV filing trigger: more than USD 100,000 received from a nonresident alien individual or foreign estate, aggregating related foreign persons, treated as gifts or bequests
  • Section 6039F penalty for failure to report a foreign gift: 5% of the amount of the foreign gifts per month the failure continues, not exceeding 25% in total
  • No section 6039F penalty if the failure was due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect
  • Foreign trust penalties under section 6677: initial penalty the greater of USD 10,000 or 35% of the gross value of property transferred / distributions received, or 5% of the gross value of trust assets treated as owned
  • Due date: 15th day of the 4th month after year end (15th day of the 6th month for taxpayers living and working abroad); extendable to the 15th day of the 10th month
  • The fact that a foreign country would penalise disclosure is not reasonable cause
S22 IRS — Gifts from foreign person Gifts from foreign person (International tax gap series) Internal Revenue Service (US) primary 2026-11-07
  • Report on Part IV of Form 3520 if a large gift or bequest is received from a foreign person
  • Threshold from a nonresident alien or foreign estate: aggregate exceeding USD 100,000 during the tax year; each gift over USD 5,000 separately identified
  • Threshold for purported gifts from foreign corporations/partnerships: USD 20,116 for 2025 and USD 20,573 for 2026, inflation-adjusted
  • A foreign gift or bequest received from a foreign person is excluded from gross income by the recipient
  • Late or incomplete Form 3520 may expose the taxpayer to penalties under IRC 6039F(c) absent reasonable cause
S23 IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures Streamlined filing compliance procedures Internal Revenue Service (US) primary 2026-11-07
  • Available to individual taxpayers (including estates of individuals) certifying that the failure to report foreign financial assets and pay tax was not willful
  • Two tracks: streamlined foreign offshore procedures (non-US residents) and streamlined domestic offshore procedures (US residents)
  • Non-willful conduct = negligence, inadvertence, mistake, or good-faith misunderstanding of the law
  • Ineligible if the IRS has begun a civil examination for any taxable year, or the taxpayer is under IRS Criminal Investigation
  • Returns under the procedures may still be selected for audit and may face additional civil penalties and criminal liability if the conduct was in fact willful
S24 26 USC §988 — Treatment of certain foreign currency transactions 26 U.S.C. §988 — Treatment of certain foreign currency transactions US Government Publishing Office (govinfo), United States Code primary 2026-11-07
  • §988(a)(1)(A): foreign currency gain or loss on a section 988 transaction is computed separately and treated as ordinary income or loss
  • §988(c)(1)(C)(i): any disposition of nonfunctional currency is a section 988 transaction
  • §988(e)(1): the section does not apply to a section 988 transaction entered into by an individual which is a personal transaction
  • §988(e)(2): on disposition of nonfunctional currency in a personal transaction, no gain is recognised by reason of exchange-rate change — but the exclusion does not apply if the gain would exceed USD 200
  • §988(e)(3): 'personal transaction' means any transaction entered into by an individual, excluding transactions whose allocable expenses meet section 162 or section 212 requirements

S26 US–PRC Income Tax Convention (1984, in force 1987) United States–The People's Republic of China Income Tax Convention Internal Revenue Service (US), treaty text PDF primary 2026-11-07
  • Signed at Beijing 30 April 1984; second protocol 10 May 1986; instruments of ratification exchanged 22 October 1986; entered into force 1 January 1987

S27 IRM 4.26.16 — FBAR examination procedures Internal Revenue Manual 4.26.16 — Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) Internal Revenue Service (US) primary 2026-11-07
  • Non-willful FBAR penalty authority is 31 USC 5321(a)(5)(A) and (B)(i)

  • In most non-willful cases examiners recommend one penalty per violation
  • Willful penalty maximum is governed by 31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)
