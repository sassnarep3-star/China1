What does it actually cost to get CNY out of China as a US citizen?

Short answer, from the primary sources: the PRC does not tax the conversion. The cost you are feeling is three separate things, and only one of them is a real tax — a quota wall, a retail FX spread, and US reporting exposure on the account. This pack documents each one against an official source, and leaves blanks where nothing official exists.

Six findings that decide the answer

1. The PRC does not tax the conversion.

Individual income tax applies to nine enumerated categories of income. Foreign-exchange gain and currency conversion are not among them. Savings-deposit interest has been provisionally exempt since 9 October 2008. A lawful conversion through a lawful channel therefore carries a 0% PRC conversion tax.

Source: S05 S06 S07

2. The wall is a quota, not a tax.

The binding constraint is USD 50,000 per person per year of FX purchase and settlement. Above that, a genuine current-account purpose with documents is required. A USD 1,000,000 balance needs 20 person-years of quota.

Source: S04 S11

3. Only two lawful routes can move a whole balance.

Emigrant transfer and inheritance transfer under PBoC 2004 No.16 are the only general-purpose capital-account exits. Emigrant transfer requires cancelling PRC household registration first, and a PRC tax clearance certificate.

Source: S02 S03

4. The genuine large liability on the US side is reporting, not tax.

FBAR exposure is USD 16,536 per report non-willfully and USD 165,353 or 50% of the balance willfully. One missed year on a USD 500,000 account can exceed the entire tax you are worried about.

Source: S17 S19 S27

5. Every prohibited route is 30%–100% of the amount.

Evasion and illegal FX arbitrage carry fines up to 30% of the amount, rising to 30%–100% where serious, plus criminal liability — several times any plausible tax. All ten of SAFE's published individual cases in March 2024 were underground-bank cases.

Source: S01 S15

6. Getting it wrong costs three years, not one.

Misdeclaring the purpose, or lending/borrowing a quota, puts you on the watch list: no facilitation quota for the listing year plus the following two years.

Source: S12 S16
