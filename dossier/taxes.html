Two jurisdictions, two different questions

Tax matrix

China asks "is this income, and has it been taxed?" The United States asks "is this income, and have you disclosed the account?" Neither asks "how much tax is due on the conversion?" — because neither taxes it.

The single most useful sentence in this dossier. China's Individual Income Tax Law lists nine chargeable categories of income CN-14 · State Taxation Administration / NPC. Moving your own already-taxed principal from a Chinese account to a US account is not one of the nine. The US treats a transfer between your own accounts as neither income nor gift, and does not recognise personal-currency exchange-rate gain below USD 200 US-04 · U.S. Code (Office of the Law Revision Counsel).

China side

Is there a Chinese tax on converting CNY into USD?
T01		No conversion taxNo provision of the Individual Income Tax Law imposes a charge on an exchange of currency. The charge, where it exists, attaches to income (income categories 1-9), not to the conversion act. The FX regulations impose filing and approval duties, not a levy.
Consequence: The conversion itself costs the bank spread, not tax.
CN-14 · State Taxation Administration / NPC CN-01 · People's Bank of China / SAFE		Verified
Is there a Chinese tax on remitting your own money abroad?
T02		NoRemittance is governed by the FX Regulations and SAFE rules (permits, quota, authenticity review), which carry administrative penalties for evasion — not a tax. Certain outward payments need a tax filing (备案) and the emigration route needs a tax clearance certificate, but neither creates a charge on the principal.
Consequence: The 'considerable tax' people experience on the way out is usually either the 20% tax on the underlying gain or a professional/agent fee, not a remittance tax.

Individual income tax residence
T04		183 days / domicileA person with a domicile in China, or who resides in China for a cumulative 183 days in a tax year, is a resident individual and is taxed on income from both inside and outside China. A non-resident is taxed only on China-source income.
Consequence: If the account holder is still a Chinese tax resident, their WORLDWIDE income is in scope in China — a separate and larger issue than moving a balance.

T05		20% flat第三条(三): 利息、股息、红利所得 applies a proportional rate of 20%. Withholding is done by the payer (e.g. the bank).
Consequence: A CNY deposit is already taxed at source. That tax is not avoidable by moving the money.

Consequence: If the balance came from selling a China property, the 20% was always owed on the GAIN — it is not created by the conversion and cannot be avoided by choosing another remittance channel.

Tax filing (备案) before an outward payment of USD 50,000 or more

Consequence: The bureau signs off that the assets are tax-clean before anything moves. This is where an untaxed gain surfaces.

United States side

Is receiving your own money into a US account taxable?
T11		NoA transfer between accounts you already own is not an accession to wealth and is not income under IRC §61; nor is it a gift (no donor), nor an inheritance (you are alive). Nothing in the US system taxes the arrival of your own funds.
Consequence: The premise that bringing the money home triggers a large US tax is, as a matter of income tax, incorrect.
US-04 · U.S. Code (Office of the Law Revision Counsel) US-13 · Internal Revenue Service		Verified
FX gain when CNY is converted to USD
T12		Not recognised unless gain > USD 200§988(e)(2): where an individual disposes of nonfunctional currency in a personal transaction, no gain is recognised by reason of exchange-rate changes between acquisition and disposition, unless the gain would otherwise exceed USD 200. Losses on personal transactions are not deductible.
Consequence: For a personal conversion, US tax on the currency move is normally zero. Character if it ever applies is ordinary, not capital.

Cash arriving in the US
T20		Report at > USD 10,00031 U.S.C. § 5316: file FinCEN Form 105 for more than USD 10,000 in currency or monetary instruments transported into or out of the US. § 5317 allows seizure and forfeiture of the unreported amount, and splitting among travellers is structuring.
Consequence: There is no cap on how much cash you may bring; there is an absolute duty to report. This is a reporting problem, not a tax problem.

Double taxation relief and the treaty
T21		Foreign tax credit / treaty reliefThe 1984 US-China income tax convention governs the allocation of taxing rights and the relief of double taxation; §901/§903 and the treaty allow a credit for Chinese tax on the same income.
Consequence: Where both countries tax the same income (typically the 20% on interest or a property gain), the US credit prevents true double taxation.

Information exchange between the two countries
T22		One-way in practice to dateChina is listed by Treasury as having reached a Model 1 FATCA agreement IN SUBSTANCE rather than a signed IGA; China also participates in the OECD common reporting standard. Either way, a Chinese account held by an identified US person is not opaque.
Consequence: Do not plan on non-discovery. Plan on correct filing.
