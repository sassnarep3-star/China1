F02HighA US citizen may still be banking on a PRC resident identity
Fixing the classification may be the unlock, and continuing to use the resident identity is misrepresentation to the bank.		Naturalised US citizens frequently keep an un-cancelled 户口 and a still-working 居民身份证. The National Immigration Administration confirms that PRC nationality is lost automatically on voluntary acquisition of another nationality while settled abroad. An account still classified as 境内个人 is therefore both a compliance problem and the reason the person is stuck behind the USD 50,000 quota.Take legal advice on the hukou/ID position before approaching the bank; do not present an ID you are no longer entitled to hold.CN-17 · 国家移民管理局 (nia.gov.cn)

F13MediumThe premise in the brief — 'no way to convert without paying a considerable amount of taxes' — does not match the law
Solving the wrong problem is expensive. If the real issue is an untaxed property gain, no remittance route avoids it.		Neither jurisdiction taxes the conversion of your own money. China taxes the INCOME (20% on interest, dividends and property gains) and restricts the TRANSFER (quota, approvals, penalties for evasion). The US taxes income and penalises non-reporting. The 'considerable amount' being felt is most likely (a) a 20% IIT bill on an underlying gain, (b) an agent's commission for an unlawful route, or (c) an unfiled-FBAR exposure that has been conflated with a transfer tax.Identify which of the three it actually is before choosing a route. The decision tree on the Strategy page is built for exactly this.CN-14 · State Taxation Administration / NPC
