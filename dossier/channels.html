A1
Detail, pros and cons — A1

Documents

Valid ID document only (within quota).

Cost

Bank bid/ask spread ≈ 0.42% (measured on BOC USD quotes of 2026-11-08); bank wire fees not verified.

Chinese tax treatment

None on the purchase itself. The purchase is recorded in the SAFE individual FX system.

US tax treatment

No US income from the conversion. Personal-transaction FX gain on disposing of CNY is not recognised unless it exceeds USD 200 (IRC §988(e)(2)).

Pros

  • Simplest and fastest route; usually done on a phone app in minutes.
  • No supporting documents, no tax filing, no SAFE approval.
  • SAFE has confirmed in 2026 that a current-account use above the quota is still possible with genuine purpose documents — the quota is a convenience tier, not a wall (see A2).

Cons

  • Hard ceiling of USD 50,000 per person per year for 境内个人.
  • At USD 50,000/yr a USD 500,000 balance needs 10 calendar years.
  • The purpose must be a genuine current-account purpose; the purchase and the remittance purposes are cross-checked (汇发[2020]14号 Art. 64).
  • Does not solve a capital-account objective (parking savings abroad) — see row D6.

Cost

Chinese tax treatment

A3
Detail, pros and cons — A3

Documents

Passport; evidence of the RMB income and that it was lawful current-account income (employment contract + payroll records + the individual income tax payment record); tax vouchers where applicable.

Cost

Same spread; bank documentary review; wire fees not verified.

Chinese tax treatment

No transfer tax. IIT on the income itself was already withheld (wages 3%-45%; interest/dividends 20%).

US tax treatment

None on the transfer. Foreign tax paid in China is creditable on the US return against the same income (treaty + FTC).

Pros

  • This is the strongest lawful route for a US citizen sitting on RMB salary/proceeds already taxed in China — and it is not subject to the USD 50,000 quota in the text of the rule.
  • Money goes bank-to-bank into the holder's own US account; clean audit trail on both sides.
  • Pairs with Art. 26 of the 2006 Measures for any residual capital-account piece.

Cons

  • Bank practice is the binding constraint, not the rule: branches vary in what they will accept as 'source of funds', and some will still push the case into the USD 50,000 quota.
  • Requires the money to BE current-account income. A property-sale gain or an investment gain is a capital-account item and does not fit here.
  • Turnaround measured in weeks for a large first-time transfer; expect a source-of-funds interview.

Documents

ID; documents above the daily line.

Cost

Wire fees only.

Chinese tax treatment

None on the remittance.

US tax treatment

None on principal.

Pros

  • A per-DAY line, not a per-year line — this is the fact most often missed: a resident can move USD 50,000 per day out of an FX account if the funds are already FX and the purpose stands up.

  • Repeated same-day-limit remittances without a genuine purpose is exactly the pattern that gets an account watch-listed.

Spread plus approval process; agent/legal cost not verified.

20% IIT on 财产转让所得 (sale price less original cost and reasonable expenses), withheld before the FX step.

B. Capital-account

RouteApplies toCap / limitStatus
Emigration asset transfer (移民转移) — the general capital-account exit
个人财产对外转移——移民转移		A natural person who has emigrated from mainland China to a foreign country (or HK/Macau) and is monetising lawful assets owned in China BEFORE acquiring emigrant statusThe whole declared balance, but staged: first release ≤ 50% of the declared total; after 1 year a further ≤ 50% of the remainder; after 2 years the balance. Declared total of CNY 200,000 or less may be released in one go after approval.Verified
B1
Detail, pros and cons — B1

Documents

Written application; 移民财产对外转移申请人情况表; 个人财产对外转移外汇业务申请表; ID plus the police-issued PRC hukou cancellation certificate AND a Chinese embassy/consulate-issued or authenticated certificate of foreign settlement; title documents to the assets; tax clearance or tax-paid vouchers from the competent tax authority where the assets sit.

Cost

Spread; translation/notarisation; possible agent cost (not verified). Approval: below CNY 500,000 handled by the provincial SAFE branch; CNY 500,000 and above goes to SAFE headquarters after provincial review.

Chinese tax treatment

No transfer tax. But a tax clearance / 完税凭证 is a mandatory document — the tax authority signs off before the money moves. Any untaxed underlying gain (for example 20% on a property transfer) surfaces here.

US tax treatment

No US tax on the transfer of principal. Any gain already realised inside China is analysed under US rules separately (capital gain, or excluded under §121 for a principal residence).

Pros

  • The only general-purpose, written-in-law channel for moving an entire personal balance out of China.
  • Two-year staging is a schedule, not a refusal — the full amount does leave.
  • Explicitly designed for exactly this fact pattern: a person who has already left China and still has assets behind.

Cons

  • Only available to someone who HAS emigrated and can produce a hukou cancellation certificate plus an authenticated foreign-settlement certificate. A US-born citizen who never held PRC hukou does not fit.
  • Assets must have been owned BEFORE emigrant status was acquired — assets accumulated afterwards fall outside the definition.
  • SAFE will not accept property that is legally restricted or whose lawful source cannot be shown.
  • Requires the whole amount to be declared once, up front; you cannot top it up later with a second application for the same emigration.
  • Cashed-out FX must be wired directly to the applicant's own account abroad — no cash withdrawal inside China.
  • Timeline of 2 years plus approval; expect legal/translation cost.

Inheritance asset transfer (继承转移)
个人财产对外转移——继承转移		A foreign citizen (or HK/Macau/Taiwan resident) who has lawfully inherited assets inside ChinaThe whole inherited estate, declared once; may be remitted in one or several instalments. Separate applications are required per deceased person.Verified
B2
Detail, pros and cons — B2

Documents

Written application; 个人财产对外转移外汇业务申请表; ID plus (for a foreign citizen) a Chinese embassy/consulate-issued or authenticated certificate of foreign settlement; proof of inheritance; the deceased's title documents plus tax clearance or tax-paid vouchers.

Cost

Spread; notarisation/translation; agent cost not verified.

Chinese tax treatment

China imposes no inheritance or estate tax. Underlying asset taxes (e.g. IIT on a property gain realised by the estate) must be cleared to obtain the 完税凭证.

US tax treatment

An inheritance is not US taxable income to the recipient, but a foreign inheritance above the threshold must be reported on Form 3520 Part IV; income the inherited assets later produce is taxable.

Pros

  • A clean, purpose-built channel for exactly the situation a US citizen most often inherits.
  • No Chinese inheritance tax to pay — the barrier is documentary, not fiscal.
  • Can be remitted in instalments, so timing is flexible.

Cons

  • Requires the deceased to have been a China-resident person with assets and a clean title chain.
  • Requires a Chinese consular certificate of the heir's foreign settlement — slow to obtain.
  • Heirs in different countries must apply separately for their own shares.
  • Reportable in the US on Form 3520 above the threshold.

Chinese tax treatment

None on the currency itself; the origin of the funds is still fully examinable.

  • Same-day / 15-day repeat-traveller rules cut the allowance to USD 500 / USD 1,000 — the anti-structuring rules are explicit.
  • Loss, theft and insurance risk.
