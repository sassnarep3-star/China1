Getting money out of China, with the smallest lawful penalty

A US citizen has CNY stuck in a mainland bank account and believes that converting it to USD costs a large amount in tax. This dossier tests that belief against the actual instruments — the PRC FX regulations, the Individual Income Tax Law, the US Internal Revenue Code and the IRS filing rules — and finds that the premise is the most expensive thing in the problem.

The five findings

1. There is no tax on converting your own money

Neither China nor the United States imposes a tax on an exchange of currency or on moving your own after-tax principal between your own accounts. China's Individual Income Tax Law taxes nine enumerated categories of income — wages, services, royalties, business income, interest/dividends, rent, property transfers, incidental gains. A transfer of principal is not one of them CN-14 · State Taxation Administration / NPC. On the US side a transfer between your own accounts is not income, and a personal currency conversion is not even recognised as a gain unless it exceeds USD 200 US-04 · U.S. Code (Office of the Law Revision Counsel).

2. The real conversion cost is about 0.42%

Bank of China quoted USD at 666.48 (bid) and 669.29 (ask) CNY per 100 on 2026-11-08 CN-11 · Bank of China. That spread — the only cost of a lawful conversion that is actually a percentage — is 0.42%, or about CNY 4,200 on a CNY 1,000,000 conversion. Wire fees are extra and are small on large amounts; where they could not be verified they are left blank.

3. The binding constraint is legal, not fiscal

China's FX framework splits current account (convertible) from capital account (gradual, approval-based) CN-01 · People's Bank of China / SAFE. Buying USD with the annual USD 50,000 quota is a current-account convenience; parking savings abroad is a capital-account act and is closed except through enumerated channels. This — not tax — is why the money is stuck.

4. One fact decides everything: how the bank classifies you

A US citizen is 境外个人 unless the bank still holds a PRC 居民身份证 for them CN-01 · People's Bank of China / SAFE. For 境外个人, Art. 59 of 汇发[2020]14号 allows a purchase against lawful China-source RMB income on ID plus source documents, with no annual cap in the rule text CN-03 · SAFE, published in the State Council Gazette (gov.cn). For 境内个人, the USD 50,000 annual quota governs. Same person, same money, two different countries' worth of difficulty.

5. The only number that is genuinely dangerous is 50%

The Chinese penalties are administrative, capped at 30% of the amount (30%–100% for serious cases) CN-08 · State Council (gov.cn). The genuinely catastrophic exposure is the US willful-FBAR penalty: the greater of USD 100,000 or 50% of the account balance, per year US-03 · U.S. Code (Office of the Law Revision Counsel). Non-reporting, not conversion, is where a life's savings actually goes. Handle the reporting before touching the transfer.

The decision fork

Before any route is chosen, one question has to be answered at the bank counter.

If the bank holds a PRC 居民身份证 → 境内个人

You are inside the USD 50,000 quota

  • Annual purchase cap: USD 50,000 per person per year CN-02
  • Above the cap: still possible, but only for a documented current-account purpose CN-05
  • Whole-balance exit: requires the capital-account route, or a change of status CN-06
  • Moving USD 50,000 per day out of an FX account is a separate, larger allowance CN-04
