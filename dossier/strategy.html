What to actually do

The right answer is not a single channel. It is a sequence: establish the facts, fix the US reporting, then move the money through the route that matches where it came from. Doing these out of order is what creates the losses.

  1. Establish what the bank thinks you are Ask, in writing, for the account's 账户主体类别 (境内个人 or 境外个人) and which identity document is on file. This is not a formality: it determines whether the USD 50,000 annual quota applies at all. See routes A1–A5 · flagged item F01.
  2. Fix the US filing position before the money moves Confirm the FBAR, Form 8938 and Schedule B history. If there are gaps, deal with them while the balance is still small enough to matter less — the willful penalty is 50% of the balance per year, and a large inbound transfer is what invites the question. flagged item F04.
  3. Identify where the money came from, tranche by tranche Salary already taxed in China → the current-account route. Property sale → SAFE approval plus 20% IIT on the gain. Inheritance → 继承转移. Unused inward remittance → return it. Investment gain → capital account. The route follows the source; it cannot precede it. flagged item F03.
  4. Match the route to the category, not to the rumour For a 境外个人 whose balance is lawful China-source income, routes A3 and A4 are the strongest in the entire dossier, because the rule text imposes no annual cap and requires only ID plus source documents. For a 境内个人, A1/A2 handle current-account needs and B1 is the only general capital-account exit.
  5. Convert once, at the best quote you can get, and wire directly Converting twice (CNY→USD→something→USD) doubles the 0.42% spread for no benefit. Avoid cash: it is the most expensive form and it triggers a customs reporting duty at the US end above USD 10,000. Cost breakdown.
  6. Report the arrival The receiving US account is not reportable as a foreign account, but the Chinese account remains reportable for every year it exists, including the year you empty it. File for that year. Tax matrix.
  7. Do not use group D Splitting across relatives' quotas, underground banks, stablecoins, fabricated invoices and undeclared cash are all penalised by name in instruments linked from this site. Each converts a 0.42% cost into a 30%–100% or 50%-of-balance loss. Group D.

Ranked by outcome for the fact pattern in the brief

A US citizen, CNY in a mainland bank account, no desire to break a rule. The ranking below is by expected net outcome, not by effort.

Best available, if the facts fit

境外个人 current-account purchase and remit — A3 / A4

If the bank holds a US passport and the balance is lawful China-source income that has already borne Chinese tax, this is the route the regulation actually provides. No annual cap appears in the text, and the documentary burden is source-of-funds evidence, which a US citizen should be able to produce anyway for their own US filings.

Estimated cost: ~0.42% spread plus fixed wire charges. Estimated tax: none on the transfer. Main risk: branch-level discretion — flagged F16.

Best available for a whole balance

Emigration asset transfer — B1

If the person has genuinely emigrated from the PRC and can produce a hukou cancellation certificate plus an authenticated foreign-settlement certificate, this is the only written-in-law route that moves an entire personal balance. It is staged over up to two years, and a declared total of CNY 200,000 or less can be released at once.

Estimated cost: spread plus notarisation/translation. Estimated tax: none on the transfer, but a tax clearance is mandatory. Main risk: the owning-before-emigration test — flagged F17.

Right answer for a dollar need

Consume it rather than repatriate it — C3 / E3

If the underlying need is "pay US tuition, US medical bills, US insurance, US travel", a mainland-issued card settles those abroad directly, consumes no quota, requires no approval and creates no taxable event. For a household with recurring USD expenses this is often worth more than any transfer scheme.

Estimated cost: card-network rate plus any issuing-bank FX fee (not verified). Estimated tax: none.

Slow but lawful

Stage it through the annual quota — A1 / E4

USD 50,000 per person per year, ID only, over the phone. Perfectly lawful for genuine current-account uses and the least administrative effort of any route. But USD 500,000 takes a decade, and each year's declared purpose has to be honest.

Estimated cost: ~0.42% per conversion. Main risk: a decade of currency exposure dwarfing the spread saved.

Never

Group D — the routes people are offered

Splitting across relatives' quotas is prohibited by name and gets the person watch-listed. Underground banks and mirror trades carry a fine of up to 30% of the amount, or 30%–100% for serious cases. Stablecoin bridges are classified as illegal financial activity. Fabricated invoices are 逃汇 with the same penalty band. Undeclared cash is forfeited in full at the US border.

Estimated cost: 30% to 100% of the amount, or 50% of the balance in the US willful case. This is the only place a "considerable amount" genuinely appears.

Not a solution

Renouncing citizenship, or moving it through crypto

Renunciation creates a mark-to-market exit tax under §877A, which is a far bigger event than the one being avoided. A crypto bridge is illegal financial activity on the China side and creates two taxable dispositions plus a reportable offshore exchange account on the US side.
