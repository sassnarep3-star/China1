Penalty breakdown

What the conversion actually costs

The brief asked for the conversion penalty to be broken down. Here it is, line by line, split into the cost you cannot avoid, the Chinese tax that is real but unrelated to converting, and the penalty exposure you only meet by skipping a lawful route.

0.42%
Measured bank spread
The real, unavoidable 'conversion penalty' on a lawful CNY→USD purchase, derived from Bank of China's own USD quotes of 2026-11-08.
≈ 0%
US tax on the FX movement
A personal conversion of your own currency is not recognised as US income unless the rate gain exceeds USD 200 (IRC §988(e)(2)).
0%
Chinese tax on remitting your own principal
The IIT Law taxes nine enumerated income categories; a transfer of already-taxed principal is not one of them.
20%
Chinese IIT that does apply — but to the gain, not the transfer
Property-transfer gains and interest/dividends are taxed at a flat 20%. If the balance came from a property sale, this tax was always owed.
50%
The number that is actually dangerous
US willful-FBAR exposure is the greater of USD 100,000 or 50% of the account balance — per year. Non-reporting, not conversion, is where a 'considerable amount' is genuinely at risk.

The arithmetic behind the 0.42%. Bank of China's own board on 2026-11-08 quoted USD at a 现汇买入价 of 666.48 and a 现汇卖出价 of 669.29 CNY per 100 USD CN-11 · Bank of China. Dividing the 2.81 CNY gap by the price you pay (669.29) gives 0.4199%. That is the round-trip cost of a single conversion at one of China's four largest banks, and it is the largest percentage cost of a lawful conversion anywhere in this dossier.

What this decomposition shows

The lawful route is cheap

Add the verified percentage items and a lawful conversion costs roughly 0.4%–0.5% of principal, plus fixed wire charges that are small relative to a large balance. On CNY 1,000,000 that is about CNY 4,200 of spread. There is no 20% conversion tax in either jurisdiction.

The 20% is real — but it belongs to the gain

If the CNY came from selling a property, 20% of the gain was always owed under the Individual Income Tax Law, and no remittance route avoids it. If it came from salary, the tax was already withheld. If it is deposit interest, 20% is withheld at source. This is why identifying the source of funds (flagged item F03) matters more than choosing a channel.

The penalties are where the money goes

A 30%-of-the-amount Chinese fine for evasion, or a 50%-of-the-balance US willful-FBAR penalty, dwarfs a 0.42% spread by two orders of magnitude. Skipping a lawful route to save four tenths of a percent is the worst trade in the entire problem.

Six lines are deliberately blank

Wire commission, correspondent charges, the receiving bank's inbound fee, notarisation cost, the deposit rate and the USD rate could not be confirmed from a tier-A source in this session. Rather than put a plausible-looking number in the table, they are empty and published on the Flagged for review page.
