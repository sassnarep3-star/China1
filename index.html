Getting money out of China, priced.

There is no tax on converting your own money in either country. What stands between a Chinese bank balance and a US account is China's capital-account rule, a published bank tariff under 1%, and a US reporting duty whose penalty can reach half the balance. This site prices every lawful route at every USD 10,000 step to USD 400,000 and links every number to an official document.

The answer in five rows

QuestionAnswerSource
Is conversion taxed?No. China taxes nine categories of income and an exchange is not one; on the US side the personal-transaction rule leaves the currency gain untaxed unless it exceeds USD 200.CN-04 ↗ US-01 ↗
What stops the money?Capital-account convertibility, not tax. Current-account business is convertible; capital-account business follows a process.CN-01 ↗
What does a lawful conversion cost?0.2104% of the amount in FX margin, plus a commission capped at CNY 260 and a CNY 150 cable charge per remittance.CN-06 ↗ CN-07 ↗
What decides the route?How the RMB arose. Salary or services: documented purchase with no annual cap in the rule text. Inheritance or emigration: the 2004 personal-property transfer rules. Neither: USD 50,000 a year.CN-02 ↗ CN-03 ↗
What is the real risk?A willful FBAR penalty is the greater of USD 165,353 or 50% of the balance, per violation. At USD 400,000 that is USD 200,000.US-03 ↗
