Methodology and limitations

The verification standard applied, what counts as an official source, how derived figures are handled, and where this research stops short.

The standard

  • Rule 1. A numeric limit, rate, threshold or fine appears in a table only where it was read in a source listed in data/sources.json, cited by article number.
  • Rule 2. Where a value could not be confirmed against an official source, the cell is blank and the item appears on the Flagged for Review page with an explanation and a route to resolution.
  • Rule 3. No value anywhere on the site is inferred, estimated, interpolated or carried over from a secondary source.
  • Rule 4. Derived figures are labelled as computed and show their inputs. The fine-as-a-percentage column is populated only where both official figures are in the same currency, so no exchange rate has been assumed.
  • Rule 5. Official means a government, regulator, central bank, tax authority or judicial body publishing its own instrument, or a national legislature's gazette. Bank, law firm, accounting firm, press and aggregator sources are not accepted, and where one had to be used because the primary text could not be located, the entry is marked not official and flagged.
  • Rule 6. build/linkcheck.py runs before the site is published and fails the build on any unresolved citation, broken internal link or unflagged blank.

What counts as an official source

A government department, regulator, central bank, tax authority, national legislature gazette or judicial body publishing its own instrument on its own domain. For this research that means safe.gov.cn, gov.cn, pbc.gov.cn, chinatax.gov.cn and its provincial subdomains, moj.gov.cn, provincial government portals, irs.gov, home.treasury.gov, ecfr.gov and congress.gov.

Law firm notes, accounting firm alerts, tax-press summaries, bank marketing pages, aggregator sites and forum posts are not accepted, however accurate they appear. Where a search returned only secondary sources for a figure, the figure was left blank and flagged rather than cited to them.

One exception, recorded

The regulator's own text of Huifa [2017] No.29 on overseas card cash withdrawal could not be located on safe.gov.cn during this session. The figures for that route are taken from Bank of China's verbatim republication of the SAFE notice and of the SAFE spokesperson's answers. Bank of China is a state-owned commercial bank, not the regulator, so the entry is marked not official and recorded as F08.

How derived figures are handled

The only arithmetic performed on official data anywhere on this site is the fine-as-a-percentage column on the penalty page. It is computed as the published fine divided by the published amount moved, labelled as computed, and populated only where both official figures are expressed in the same currency. Where they are not, the cell is left blank rather than converted at an assumed exchange rate. No exchange rate is quoted anywhere on the site because no instrument in the source register fixes one; both Fashi [2019] No.1 Art.11 and Individual Income Tax Law Art.16 specify which rate to use without specifying the rate.

Limitations of this research

  • The instruments reviewed are the ones cited. Several SAFE instruments are published as consolidated texts marked as amended, and the full amendment history was not assembled in every case; see F16.
  • Branch-level practice varies. Three separate conflicts between official sources are recorded as F02, F09 and F17, and each changes how the same transaction is processed at different counters.
  • No fact pattern was supplied beyond 'a US citizen with renminbi in a Chinese bank account'. Whether the balance is current-account income or capital determines which routes are open at all, and that is not known here.
  • Chinese tax rates on some corporate transactions, notably the characterisation of capital reduction proceeds, remain contested in practice and could not be resolved from an official instrument; see F03.
  • US penalty amounts are inflation-adjusted annually. The figures shown are those in the eCFR current to 5 November 2026 for penalties assessed on or after 17 January 2025, and will move at the next adjustment.
  • Two channels commonly recommended for this exact problem, market procurement trade and cross-border e-commerce export, are omitted rather than described from secondary sources; see F14.

How the site is built and checked

build/build.py reads data/*.json and writes every page into docs/, which is the GitHub Pages source directory. Output is deterministic and no page is hand-edited. build/linkcheck.py then runs as a gate before publication and fails on any citation to a source id that does not exist, any internal link to a page that was not generated, any orphan page, any route category with no page, and any flag referenced but not defined. Both scripts use only the Python standard library.
