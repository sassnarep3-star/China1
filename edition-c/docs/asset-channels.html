Asset monetisation channels

3 channels in this category. Each is stated with its legal basis, its limit where one is fixed by an official instrument, its tax cost, the documents it requires, and its pros and cons.

Channels that convert a Chinese asset into cash first, then remit the after-tax proceeds: property, equity, inherited estate, and the emigrant property transfer.

Emigration property transfer R05

移民财产转移

verified Low if genuinely eligible; severe if documents are falsified Only for a former PRC citizen who has cancelled hukou
FieldValue
Legal basisInterim Measures for the Transfer of Individual Property Abroad, PBOC Announcement [2004] No.16; Detailed Rules Art.20
How it worksA natural person who moves from mainland China to a foreign country, or settles in Hong Kong or Macao, may realise the lawful property owned in China before acquiring emigrant status and remit it. The application goes to the SAFE branch at the emigrant's former place of household registration.
Stated limitOne application only, covering the full amount, remitted in stages: the first tranche may not exceed half; after one year from the first remittance up to half of the remainder; after two years the balance. Where the total is RMB 200,000 equivalent or less it may be remitted at once after approval. S06 S07 S02 S13
Chinese taxA tax certificate or tax-paid voucher from the competent tax authority where the property or income arose is a mandatory document. Individual income tax is due on any gain before transfer, and Art.13 of the Individual Income Tax Law requires tax clearance before Chinese household registration is cancelled on emigration.
US taxReturn of capital is not income. Any gain realised on the underlying asset is US taxable in the year of realisation, with a foreign tax credit for the Chinese tax.
DocumentationWritten application explaining the reason and the source and realisation of the property; the signed emigrant property transfer applicant form; the individual property transfer application form; public-security proof of cancellation of Chinese household registration; a settlement-abroad certificate issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate; property title documents; the tax certificate.

Pros

  • Pro. The only route that moves an entire lifetime balance of Chinese assets out in one approved process
  • Pro. No cap on the total amount applied for
  • Pro. Covers deposits, real property and other lawfully owned property, not just income

Cons

  • Con. Requires cancellation of Chinese household registration, so it is closed to a US citizen who never held a PRC hukou
  • Con. Two-year staged remittance is slow and locks the plan in; the amount cannot be increased later
  • Con. Above RMB 500,000 equivalent the local branch must refer the file to SAFE headquarters
  • Con. The bank must remit straight to the applicant's own account in the country of residence and domestic FX cash withdrawal is prohibited
  • Con. SAFE will not accept applications over property under judicial or supervisory restriction, property whose lawful source cannot be shown, or property involved in unresolved criminal or civil proceedings

Sources: S06 S07 S02 S13

Inheritance property transfer R06

继承财产转移

verified Low Only where there is an inheritance
FieldValue
Legal basisInterim Measures, PBOC Announcement [2004] No.16 Arts.2, 9; Detailed Rules Art.20
How it worksA foreign citizen, or a Hong Kong or Macao resident, who lawfully inherits an estate situated in China may realise it and remit the proceeds. The application goes to the SAFE branch at the decedent's lifetime place of household registration.
Stated limitAll property inherited from the same decedent must be applied for in a single application, but may be remitted in one or several tranches. Property inherited from different decedents is applied for and remitted separately. S06 S07 S02
Chinese taxThe decedent's property title documents plus a tax certificate or tax-paid voucher from the competent tax authority where the property is located are mandatory.
US taxInherited property generally takes a stepped-up basis; the remittance of principal is not income. Any gain on a later sale is US taxable.
DocumentationWritten application stating the reason, the relationship to the decedent and the source and realisation of the property; the application form; identity documents, and for a foreign citizen a settlement-abroad certificate issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate; proof of entitlement to the inherited property; the decedent's property title documents and the tax certificate. Documents not notarised must be notarised.

Pros

  • Pro. Expressly open to foreign citizens, so unlike emigration transfer it is available to a US citizen by birth
  • Pro. Single application but flexible remittance timing
  • Pro. No stated cap on the inherited amount

Cons

  • Con. Requires an actual inheritance from a decedent with a Chinese estate
  • Con. Notarisation and embassy authentication of foreign documents is slow and expensive
  • Con. The same RMB 500,000 equivalent referral threshold to SAFE headquarters applies
  • Con. The bank must remit directly to the heir's own account in the country of residence

Sources: S06 S07 S02

Sale of domestic commercial property by an overseas individual R07

境外个人转让境内商品房所得资金购汇汇出

verified Low to medium, driven by the conflict between the 2006 rule and later guidance Medium; conditional on owning Chinese property
FieldValue
Legal basisMeasures Art.22; Huifa [2006] No.47 Art.5; Detailed Rules Art.21; SAFE 2025 operational guidelines item 8
How it worksAn overseas individual who lawfully owns domestic commercial property may sell it and remit the after-tax proceeds. Huifa [2006] No.47 Art.5 required the local SAFE branch to confirm the FX purchase application, the transfer contract and the tax-paid certificate before conversion. The Henan branch's 2018 guidance states that such proceeds may now be processed directly at the bank, with no SAFE application and no time limit. The 2025 operational guidelines issued with Huifa [2025] No.43 set out the current documentary checklist.
Stated limitThe amount remitted may not exceed the transfer price less the taxes and fees of that transfer. Where the property is co-owned, the applicant may not remit more than their own share of the after-tax balance. S01 S17 S18 S19 S13
Chinese taxIndividual income tax on property transfer income at a proportional rate of 20%, computed as transfer revenue less original value and reasonable expenses. Art.15 of the Individual Income Tax Law requires the registration authority to verify the individual income tax paid certificate before registering the transfer.
US taxGain on the sale is US taxable as a capital gain in the year of sale, with a foreign tax credit for the Chinese individual income tax. The conversion of the proceeds is not itself taxable.
DocumentationPassport or home-return permit; the transfer contract registered and filed with the real property authority and the registration certificate; the tax-paid certificate for the transfer of property rights; the FX purchase application; a notarised power of attorney and the agent's identity document where an agent acts.

Pros

  • Pro. Turns an illiquid Chinese asset into remittable cash with an established legal path
  • Pro. The 2018 Henan guidance removes the SAFE pre-approval step for overseas individuals
  • Pro. No time limit is stated on when the proceeds must be remitted

Cons

  • Con. Requires actually owning and then selling Chinese real property, with market risk and a long transaction timeline
  • Con. Only the after-tax balance is remittable, so the 20% gain tax plus transaction taxes reduce the amount materially
  • Con. Co-ownership with a domestic individual blocks that person's share, because there is no policy support for domestic individuals buying overseas real property
  • Con. The 2006 rule and the 2018 guidance are not identical on whether SAFE approval is needed, so the position should be confirmed with the handling bank before contract

Sources: S01 S17 S18 S19 S13
