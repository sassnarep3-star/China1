Dividend from a foreign-invested enterprise to a foreign corporate shareholder R15

外商投资企业向境外法人股东分配利润

verified Low High where a compliant operating entity exists or can be created
FieldValue
Legal basisRegulation on Foreign Exchange Administration Art.21 and Art.22; Huifa [2015] No.13; EIT Law Art.4 and Art.27(5); EIT Implementation Regulations Art.91; US-China treaty Art.9(2)
How it worksA Chinese entity with distributable profits declares a dividend to its foreign shareholder. The entity completes direct-investment FX registration, files for tax on the outbound payment where a single payment exceeds USD 50,000 equivalent, and the bank then processes the conversion and remittance.
Stated limitLimited by distributable profits after making good prior-year losses and funding the statutory reserve. No FX quota applies. Huifa [2015] No.13 provides that only after direct-investment FX registration is complete may the entity carry out FX conversion and remittance, including remittance of profits and dividends. S03 S20 S14 S30 S15
Chinese taxCorporate income tax of 25% on the entity's profit first. Then withholding income tax on the dividend: the statutory rate for a non-resident enterprise under Art.3(3) of the EIT Law is 20%, reduced to 10% by Art.91 of the Implementation Regulations. Treaty Art.9(2) also caps the dividend tax at 10%, so the treaty gives no further reduction. VAT does not apply to a dividend distribution.
US taxA US corporate shareholder generally excludes 100% of the foreign-source portion of a dividend from a 10%-owned foreign corporation under IRC s.245A, but may have a GILTI or Subpart F inclusion instead. A US individual shareholder includes the dividend in income, generally at qualified dividend rates, with a foreign tax credit for the 10% Chinese withholding.
DocumentationAudited financial statements; board or shareholder resolution on profit distribution; evidence that prior-year losses are made good and the statutory reserve is funded; tax payment certificates; the outbound payment tax filing number and verification code where the single payment exceeds USD 50,000 equivalent; direct-investment FX registration.

Pros

  • Pro. The standard, well-trodden route for recurring extraction with no volume ceiling
  • Pro. Registration is handled at the bank rather than by SAFE approval since June 2015
  • Pro. Treaty and domestic withholding rates are identical at 10%, so the cost is predictable and does not depend on treaty relief

Cons

  • Con. Requires a Chinese entity with real distributable profits; a bare bank balance is not distributable
  • Con. Double layer of tax for an individual: 25% corporate tax then 10% withholding, so about 32.5% of pre-tax profit is lost before it reaches the shareholder
  • Con. Setting up and maintaining a compliant entity costs audit, accounting and registered-capital time
  • Con. Dividends are only distributable out of after-tax profits after losses and the statutory reserve

Dividend from a foreign-invested enterprise to a foreign individual shareholder R16

外商投资企业向境外个人股东分配利润

verified Low High mechanically; expensive fiscally
FieldValue
Legal basisIndividual Income Tax Law Art.2(6) and Art.3(3); Regulation Art.21 and Art.22; Huifa [2015] No.13
How it worksAs R15, but the shareholder is a natural person. The distributing entity withholds individual income tax on the dividend and then processes the outbound payment and conversion.
Stated limitLimited by distributable profits. No FX quota applies. S13 S03 S20
Chinese taxCorporate income tax of 25% at the entity level, then individual income tax on interest, dividends and bonuses at a proportional rate of 20% on the full amount of each receipt, per Art.3(3) and Art.6(6) of the Individual Income Tax Law.
US taxThe dividend is US taxable to the individual in the year received, with a foreign tax credit for the Chinese tax, subject to the s.904 limitation. The conversion and remittance of the after-tax dividend is not a further taxable event.
DocumentationAs R15, plus the withholding declaration for the individual shareholder.

Pros

  • Pro. Reaches the individual directly with one entity and one distribution decision
  • Pro. No FX quota and no SAFE approval once direct-investment registration is in place

Cons

  • Con. Combined burden of 25% corporate tax then 20% dividend tax leaves 60% of pre-tax profit
  • Con. A widely cited exemption for dividends paid to foreign individuals from foreign-invested enterprises rests on 1994-era circulars whose current validity was not confirmed from an official source in this session, so no relief is assumed here
  • Con. Requires genuine distributable profits and a compliant entity

Return of registered capital by capital reduction R17

外商投资企业减资汇出

verified Medium, driven by tax characterisation uncertainty Medium; slow and fact-specific, with unresolved tax characterisation
FieldValue
Legal basisRegulation Art.22; Huifa [2015] No.13; Huifa [2013] No.21
How it worksThe entity reduces its registered capital, returns the reduced amount to the foreign investor, and remits it after completing the change in direct-investment FX registration and paying tax on any gain.
Stated limitBounded by the amount of registered capital actually contributed and by Company Law creditor-protection procedures. No FX quota applies. S03 S20 F03
Chinese taxA non-resident enterprise's gain on a capital reduction is taxable; the specific characterisation and computation of the reduction proceeds was not confirmed from an official source in this session and is therefore left unstated here.
US taxTreatment depends on whether the distribution is a return of basis or a dividend equivalent. Not quantified here.
DocumentationShareholder resolution on the capital reduction; newspaper or creditor notification and the statutory waiting period; amended articles and business licence change; tax clearance; change of direct-investment FX registration; board resolution and audit where required by the bank.
Verification noteThe FX mechanics and the stated limit are sourced. The tax characterisation of capital-reduction proceeds is NOT confirmed and is left unstated in the China tax field; see flag F03. F03

Pros

  • Pro. Returns contributed capital rather than profits, so it does not depend on the entity being profitable
  • Pro. No FX quota and no SAFE approval once registration is updated

Cons

  • Con. Company Law creditor notification and objection procedures make this slow
  • Con. Reduces the entity's capital base and may breach minimum requirements or contractual commitments
  • Con. The tax characterisation of reduction proceeds is genuinely contested in practice and was not resolvable from official sources in this session

Liquidation of a foreign-invested enterprise R18

外商投资企业清算汇出

verified Low, but slow and irreversible High where the entity is to be wound up anyway
FieldValue
Legal basisRegulation Art.22 paragraph 3
How it worksArt.22 paragraph 3 of the Regulation provides that where a foreign-invested enterprise is lawfully terminated, the RMB belonging to the foreign investor may, after liquidation and tax payment in accordance with State regulations, be purchased into foreign exchange and remitted through a financial institution operating settlement and sale of foreign exchange.
Stated limitBounded by net liquidation proceeds after creditors, taxes and liquidation expenses. No FX quota applies. S03
Chinese taxLiquidation income is taxable at the entity level; tax payment is an express statutory precondition to remittance. The computation was not separately verified in this session.
US taxLiquidation distributions are analysed under the US liquidation rules; not quantified here.
DocumentationTermination and liquidation approvals; liquidation committee reports; creditor settlement; tax clearance certificates; deregistration of direct-investment FX registration; bank authenticity review.

Pros

  • Pro. Express statutory authority in a State Council regulation, so the entitlement is strong
  • Pro. Moves the entire residual value, not just profits or a quota slice
  • Pro. Clean exit: the entity and its compliance burden end

Cons

  • Con. Destroys the operating platform; it is a one-way exit
  • Con. Liquidation is lengthy, requires settling all creditors and employees and obtaining tax clearance
  • Con. Only the residual after all liabilities and taxes is remittable
  • Con. Any unresolved tax exposure surfaces during clearance and can block the whole remittance

Sale of equity in a Chinese entity to a foreign buyer R19

转让境内企业股权

verified Low to medium High where a buyer exists
FieldValue
Legal basisIndividual Income Tax Law Art.2(8), Art.3(3), Art.6(5) and Art.15; EIT Law Art.19(2) and Art.27(5) with Implementation Regulations Art.91; US-China treaty Art.12(5)
How it worksThe shareholder sells its interest in the Chinese entity. Consideration may be payable offshore by the buyer, or onshore and then remitted. Art.15 of the Individual Income Tax Law requires the market entity registration authority to verify the individual income tax paid certificate before registering the equity change.
Stated limitNo FX quota. The remittance of the proceeds follows the capital-account rules applicable to the seller. S13 S14 S30
Chinese taxIndividual seller: 20% on property transfer income, being transfer revenue less original value and reasonable expenses. Non-resident enterprise seller: taxable income is the full revenue less the net value of the property, at the 10% reduced rate under Art.91 of the Implementation Regulations. Treaty Art.12(5) permits China to tax a gain on a participation of 25% or more in a Chinese resident company.
US taxThe gain is US taxable in the year of sale with a foreign tax credit. Treaty Art.12 leaves the gain also taxable in the US as the state of residence, and the Protocol's saving clause preserves US taxation of its citizens in any event.
DocumentationEquity transfer agreement; valuation evidence supporting the price; tax payment certificate before registration change; consideration payment evidence; direct-investment FX registration change.

Pros

  • Pro. Realises the whole value of the holding in one transaction
  • Pro. Where a foreign buyer pays offshore, the consideration never enters China and no outbound remittance is needed at all
  • Pro. Only the gain is taxed, not the proceeds, where the cost base is documented

Cons

  • Con. Requires a willing buyer and an acceptable price
  • Con. A below-market price invites adjustment; Art.8 of the Individual Income Tax Law permits adjustment of arrangements without reasonable commercial purpose
  • Con. Registration cannot complete until the tax paid certificate is produced, so tax is unavoidable and must be funded first
  • Con. Undocumented cost base means 20% on gross proceeds in practice

Cross-border service fee paid by the Chinese entity to a US related party R20

境内企业向境外关联方支付服务费

verified Medium, driven by transfer pricing High where genuine services exist
FieldValue
Legal basisRegulation Arts.5, 12 and 14; STA and SAFE Announcement 2021 No.19 and 2013 No.40; EIT Implementation Regulations Art.7(5) and Art.49; Individual Income Tax Law Art.8
How it worksThe Chinese entity pays a genuine fee for services actually supplied from outside China. The payment is a current-account item, which the State does not restrict. Where a single payment exceeds USD 50,000 equivalent the payer completes the outbound payment tax filing and the bank processes the remittance against the filing number and verification code.
Stated limitNo FX quota. Bounded by the arm's-length value of real services and by deductibility rules. S03 S15 S14 S13
Chinese taxWithholding income tax where the recipient is a non-resident without a Chinese establishment: 10% on the gross amount under Art.91 of the Implementation Regulations. VAT on imported services; the specific rate and any cross-border zero-rate or exemption depends on the service type and on whether it is consumed entirely outside China. Management fees paid between enterprises are expressly not deductible under Art.49 of the Implementation Regulations.
US taxThe fee is income to the US recipient and is US taxable. Where the recipient is a corporation, the arm's-length standard under IRC s.482 applies on both sides.
DocumentationService contract with a genuine scope and deliverables; evidence of performance; invoices; withholding tax payment certificate; outbound payment tax filing number and verification code above USD 50,000 equivalent; transfer-pricing documentation.

Pros

  • Pro. Current-account, so Art.5 of the Regulation says the State does not restrict it
  • Pro. Recurring and scalable, and it does not require distributable profits
  • Pro. The 2021 announcement lets one filing cover multiple payments under the same contract, and exempts reinvestment of legitimate direct-investment income in China
  • Pro. Deductible at the Chinese entity, reducing 25% corporate tax on the same amount

Cons

  • Con. The services must be real and documented; a bare management fee is not deductible between enterprises
  • Con. Art.8 of the Individual Income Tax Law and the transfer-pricing rules allow adjustment where related-party dealings are not at arm's length
  • Con. 10% withholding plus VAT means the gross-up must be modelled before pricing
  • Con. Tax authorities scrutinise service fees to related parties closely, and a disallowed deduction converts the payment into a deemed distribution

Royalty payment by the Chinese entity to a US related party R21

境内企业向境外支付特许权使用费

verified Medium, driven by transfer pricing High where real intangibles exist
FieldValue
Legal basisUS-China treaty Art.11(2) and Protocol para.6; EIT Implementation Regulations Art.7(5), Art.20 and Art.91; STA and SAFE Announcement 2021 No.19
How it worksThe Chinese entity pays for the right to use a copyright, trademark, patent, design, model, secret formula or process, know-how or similar right. Royalties are sourced where the payer is located, so a Chinese payer creates Chinese-source income for the US recipient.
Stated limitNo FX quota. Bounded by arm's-length pricing and by the existence of a real intangible. S30 S14 S15
Chinese taxWithholding income tax at the reduced 10% rate under Art.91 of the Implementation Regulations. Treaty Art.11(2) caps it at 10% of the gross royalty, so no treaty reduction below the domestic rate. For leasing of industrial, commercial or scientific equipment, Protocol para.6 taxes only 70% of the gross amount, an effective cap of 7%. VAT applies to royalties; rent and royalties paid between operating units of the same enterprise are not deductible under Art.49.
US taxRoyalty income to the US recipient, US taxable, with a foreign tax credit for the Chinese withholding subject to the s.904 limitation and the separate passive-category basket.
DocumentationLicence agreement; evidence of the intangible and of its ownership; withholding tax payment certificate; outbound payment tax filing above USD 50,000 equivalent; transfer-pricing support.

Pros

  • Pro. Predictable 10% ceiling under both domestic law and the treaty
  • Pro. Equipment leasing gets an effective 7% ceiling under Protocol para.6
  • Pro. Scales with revenue rather than with profit, so it works even in a loss-making year
  • Pro. Deductible at the Chinese entity against 25% corporate tax

Cons

  • Con. Requires a genuinely owned, valuable intangible; a retro-fitted licence is the classic transfer-pricing adjustment
  • Con. Related-party royalties between operating units of the same enterprise are not deductible
  • Con. VAT and surcharges apply on top of the withholding tax
  • Con. The 10% treaty rate is no better than domestic law, so there is no treaty planning benefit

Outbound intercompany lending from the Chinese entity R24

境内企业向境外关联方放款

unverified Medium to high, driven by unresolved regulatory parameters Not established from official sources in this session
FieldValue
Legal basisMeasures Art.21; Detailed Rules Art.23; Regulation Arts.17 and 20
How it worksA Chinese entity lends to a related offshore borrower and receives principal and interest abroad, or the loan is repaid offshore. Art.23 of the Detailed Rules states that liberalisation of domestic individuals providing loans abroad, borrowing foreign debt, providing external guarantees and directly participating in overseas commodity futures and derivatives is to proceed according to the progress of capital-account convertibility, with specific measures to be formulated separately.
Stated limit— unverifiedF04
Chinese taxInterest received is taxable. Interest paid by the Chinese entity to a non-resident is subject to 10% withholding under Art.91 of the Implementation Regulations and is sourced where the payer is located. Interest paid between non-bank operating units of the same enterprise is not deductible under Art.49.
US taxInterest received by the US lender is US taxable; a foreign tax credit is available for Chinese withholding.
DocumentationLoan agreement; registration with SAFE where required; the applicable cross-border financing quota; outbound payment tax filing above USD 50,000 equivalent for interest.
FlagF04

Pros

  • Pro. Interest is deductible in China and creates a recurring outbound current-account payment
  • Pro. Principal repayment is a return of capital rather than income

Cons

  • Con. Capital-account item, so registration and quota conditions apply and the enabling measures were still to be formulated as drafted
  • Con. Outbound lending by a Chinese entity is subject to macro-prudential cross-border financing limits whose current parameters were not verified from an official source in this session
  • Con. Thin capitalisation and arm's-length interest rate rules allow adjustment
  • Con. A loan that is never repaid invites recharacterisation as a distribution
