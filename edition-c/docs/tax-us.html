US-side tax and reporting

Worldwide income, the two mutually exclusive reliefs, and the four reporting regimes with their adjusted penalty amounts.

The US position is a reporting position first and a tax position second. A US citizen is already taxed on worldwide income wherever the funds sit, so converting and remitting after-tax money between the taxpayer's own accounts is not a second realisation. The exposure is in the forms. S24 S29

Relief: exclusion or credit, never both on the same income

  • The foreign earned income exclusion reaches USD 130,000 for 2025 and USD 132,900 for 2026 per qualifying person, with a housing limitation of USD 39,000 and USD 39,870 respectively. It requires a foreign tax home plus bona fide residence for an uninterrupted period including an entire tax year, or physical presence for at least 330 full days in any twelve consecutive months. It does not cover pensions, annuities or social security, and does not reduce self-employment tax. S23 S24
  • The foreign tax credit is limited to US tax liability multiplied by foreign-source taxable income over total taxable income, computed separately for each limitation category, with a one-year carryback and a ten-year carryover. S28
  • No credit or deduction is allowed for foreign tax paid on income excluded under either exclusion, so the two reliefs cannot be stacked. S28
  • Where all foreign-source gross income is passive category income on qualified payee statements and creditable foreign tax does not exceed USD 300, or USD 600 jointly, the credit may be claimed without Form 1116. S28
