What it actually costs to get CNY out of China

Thirty routes, priced where a published price exists and blank where it does not. The short version: there is no conversion tax in either country. What looks like a penalty is a bank spread of about 0.21%, plus a capped commission and a flat cable fee. The genuinely large numbers on this page are Chinese tax on the income itself and US penalties for not reporting the account.

The cost shape, at a glance

The FX spread scales with the amount; the bank fees do not. That single fact drives every row of the cost ladder: at USD 10,000 the fixed and capped fees are about 0.32% of the transfer, and at USD 400,000 they fall to about 0.015%. The FX cost, by contrast, is $841.79 at USD 400,000 and $21.04 at USD 10,000 - always the same 0.21%.

Six route families

Current account - income you can document (6)

The only family of routes without a hard amount ceiling, but each requires an evidence chain from the CNY to specific lawful income. Start here if any part of the balance is documented earnings.

Capital account - property, inheritance and business exit (5)

The lawful home for pre-emigration savings and inherited assets. All of these are approval-gated, and none of them is a way to relabel income you cannot document.

Business, dividends and investment (5)

Company-level and investment routes. Real costs here are Chinese taxes, not bank fees.

Cash, cards, wallets and retail channels (6)

Small, fast and capped. Useful for genuine consumption; incapable of moving a six-figure balance.

Keep in place, or avoid converting at all (4)

Sometimes the right answer is not to convert. These rows exist so the comparison is honest about the zero-cost option and about the routes that only look clever.

Prohibited - do not use (4)

Listed because they are what people are actually offered. They carry the largest expected cost of any row on this site, and no execution steps are published here.

Findings

J1 Converting CNY to USD is not a taxable event in either jurisdiction

China taxes nine enumerated categories of income; currency exchange is not one of them (IIT Law Art. 2). The US taxes your income, not the act of changing currency, and for an individual's personal transaction §988(e)(2) disregards exchange-rate gain unless it exceeds USD 200. So the 'tax on converting' in the brief does not exist as a separate charge.

CN-IIT ↗US-988 ↗

J2 What people experience as a conversion penalty is a bank spread, and it is small

On the BOC board read for this edition, one USD costs CNY 6.6903 to buy and fetches CNY 6.6622 to sell. The gap is 0.42% of the bid, so a round trip costs that much and a single conversion costs about 0.21% - half the spread, measured against the bank's own mid. That is the entire 'penalty' at the currency layer. Note that comparing the ask to the PBOC central parity gives a negative number on this snapshot, because the two are from different days; see the explanation on the penalty page.

BOC-BOARD ↗CFETS-FIX ↗

J3 The real barrier is convertibility, not tax

Current-account items are convertible in principle; capital-account items follow a 'convertibility process' that means approval (Measures Art. 3). A balance with no documented current-account source has no default lawful route, which is why it feels stuck.

CN-PBOC3 ↗

J4 A US passport changes which quota applies - and usually helps

The USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota is framed around 境内个人 (Chinese citizens with a resident ID). For 境外个人 buying against documented lawful current-account income, Art. 59 of the 2020 Guide states no annual cap. The binding constraint becomes evidence, not size.

CN-GUIDE2020 ↗

J5 The largest number on this site is a US penalty, not a Chinese fee

A willful FBAR violation carries a civil maximum of the greater of USD 165,353 or 50% of the account balance at the time of the violation. At USD 400,000 that is USD 200,000 - roughly 46 times the entire bank cost of moving the money.

US-5321 ↗US-1010821 ↗

J6 Chinese tax on the money can dwarf every fee, and one exemption just ended

Interest, dividends, asset transfers and incidental income are taxed at a flat 20% (IIT Law Art. 3(3)). The long-standing exemption for foreign individuals' FIE dividends was repealed with effect from 2026-09-01, so any guide still calling those dividends tax-free is out of date.

CN-IIT ↗CN-DIV2026 ↗

J7 Six of the thirty catalogued routes are prohibited, and one has a fresh legal basis

Dealer intermediation, quota splitting, crypto, and false documents are joined by two more structural traps. The crypto basis changed on 2026-02-06: 银发〔2026〕42号 replaced the 2021 notice entirely.

CN-FXREG ↗CN-VA2026 ↗CN-GUIDE2020 ↗

J8 Staging rules you may find online for property transfers were cancelled in 2014

The 2004 Measures required emigrant transfers in three stages over two years. 汇发〔2014〕2号 Section VI cancelled the staging, the national-level filing above RMB 500,000, and the notarisation duty. The 2026 SAFE Q&A still cites the 2004 Measures without restating the amendment, which is how the stale version keeps circulating.

CN-PROPERTY2004 ↗CN-CAP2014 ↗CN-PROPERTYQA ↗

J9 No method in this catalogue can be given an exact all-in price today

Two components are published and computed here: the FX spread and the BOC sender tariff. Receiving-bank fees, intermediary charges, Chinese tax on the underlying income, and notarisation or travel costs are all unknown for this account. Those cells are blank on purpose and counted on the verification page.

BOC-TARIFF ↗BOC-BOARD ↗

J10 The cheapest lawful route is the one that matches how the money was earned

If the balance is documented salary or service income, R01 is lawful, uncapped and priced. If it is pre-emigration savings, R08 needs an approval but no quota. If it is inherited, R09 does. The choice is decided by evidence you already have, not by provider shopping.

CN-PBOC3 ↗CN-GUIDE2020 ↗CN-PROPERTY2004 ↗
