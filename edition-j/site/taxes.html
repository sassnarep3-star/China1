1 · The FX spread is not a tax — it is the bank’s price

At the 2026-11-10 07:32:39 snapshot (S07), BOC posts buy 666.22 / sell 669.03 per $100. Midpoint 667.625 is the reference. Your one-way cost over midpoint is (669.03−667.625)/667.625 = 0.2104% (S07). Add BOC handling 0.1% capped at ¥260 + ¥150 cable (S06) and the known cost at $400k is just under $1k — not a “40% tax”.

2 · Chinese IIT is on the earning, not the wire

IIT Law Art.2 (S09) taxes salary, services, dividends, asset transfers, etc. — conversion is not listed. Art.3 sets rates: comprehensive income 3–45% progressive; business 5–35%; flat 20% for interest/dividends, leasing, asset transfer, incidental. If your balance is salary already net-taxed via payroll, the IIT is already paid and the bank wants that voucher (S02 Art.10/13). If it is untaxed savings or an undeclared gain, that IIT is the “penalty” that must be settled before SAFE/bank will let it through (S03 §VI, S05 checklist).

3 · US worldwide income & FX gain — separate from the wire fee

US citizens report worldwide income (IRS S15). Already-taxed principal moved to the US is not new income, but you must characterize interest, dividends, gains, gifts and currency gains separately. §988 (S14) taxes personal FX gains >$200 per transaction as ordinary income; personal losses are disallowed and personal transactions ≤$200 are excluded (S14 §988(e)(2)). Basis is the USD value when you received the CNY (S13, S16 practice unit). The foreign tax credit (S15, Form 1116) reduces US tax by qualifying Chinese income taxes — the lawful relief — but cannot be used on excluded foreign-earned income and is limited by category.

4 · Reporting & penalty exposure — not fees, but real

FBAR (S10): aggregate foreign accounts >$10k at any point ⇒ FinCEN 114; willful violation up to $100k or 50% of balance. Form 8938 (S11): specified foreign assets >thresholds (e.g. $200k on last day for single expat). Form 3520: gifts from foreign persons >$100k aggregated (S12). Penalties are per-form, per-year and increase with willfulness — they dwarf wire fees, so timely filing is the cheapest “tax” move.

Least-tax checklist:
  1. Document the underlying income and keep Chinese tax receipts → preserves FTC eligibility (S15).
  2. Use the route whose tax paperwork you can already meet — don’t create a fake asset sale to chase a lower FX fee (P03 is fraud, S01 Art.7).
  3. Keep §988 gain ≤$200 per wire by timing (S14) or be ready to report it (S13).
  4. File FBAR/8938/3520 on time (S10–S12) — penalties are the biggest avoidable “penalty”.

5 · What is not a China→US tax

  • No general USD 50k gift-tax threshold (S12: foreign-person gift reporting >$100k; 2026 entity purported-gift threshold $20,573 — not a blanket tax).
  • No universal USD 5k cash exemption — SAFE caps overseas card cash at ¥100k/yr, ¥10k/day (customs S19) and Customs declaration is separate.
  • The new 1% US remittance excise is on US→abroad wires funded by cash/money orders/cashier’s checks (IRS S22), not China→US.
  • The PBOC 2021 crypto notice (S18) is repealed by 2026 No.42 (S21) — crypto cash-out is still banned illegal financial activity (S21).
