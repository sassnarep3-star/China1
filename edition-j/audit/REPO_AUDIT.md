## A03 — "Conversion tax" premise does not match the law (irregularity, confirmed)

- The brief's premise ("no way to convert without paying a considerable amount of
  taxes") does not match either jurisdiction. China IIT Art. 2 lists nine income
  categories; currency exchange is not one (S05). U.S. §988 taxes currency *gains*,
  not principal movement (S10). The blocker is capital-account inconvertibility
  (Measures Art. 3, S01). Recorded as an irregularity, not silently rewritten.

## A04 — USD 50,000 conflation (confirmed across editions)

- USD 50,000/**year** is the *purchase* facilitation quota for 境内个人 (2020 Arts. 54–55).
- USD 50,000/**day** is the *remittance* threshold for FX already held (2020 Art. 64).
- Overseas-person lawful-income purchase (2020 Art. 59) has no annual cap in the rule
  text. A U.S. citizen with a U.S. passport is 境外个人 (Measures Art. 37). Sources: S01, S02.

## A05 — §988 $200 exclusion misquoted in older material (corrected)

- §988(e)(2): the personal-transaction exclusion fails when gain *exceeds* USD200.
  It is not a universal USD200 deduction. Edition J states the conditional rule (S10).

## A06 — Staged emigrant remittance (2004 vs 2014)

- PBOC 2004 No. 16 Art. 6 staged releases are superseded: SAFE Huifa [2014] No. 2 §VI(2)

## A07 — Gift thresholds (corrected)

- No general USD50,000 "gift tax" threshold. Form 3520: >USD100,000 from a nonresident
  alien/foreign estate; USD20,573 (2026) for foreign corp/partnership purported gifts,
  with aggregation. Source: S12.

## A08 — 1% remittance tax direction (corrected)

- The new 1% tax covers specified physical-instrument-funded remittances sent *from*
  the U.S., not China-to-U.S. transfers. Source: S16 (inherited — F12).
