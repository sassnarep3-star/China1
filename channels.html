Every route, including the ones that don't work

Listed as completely as the rules allow. Routes that are legally unavailable are kept in the table rather than deleted, because "this does not exist" is often the most useful finding — it stops you paying someone to attempt it.

Profile assumed US citizen (therefore 境外个人 under PBoC Order [2006] No.3 Art.37), holding RMB in a mainland bank account, resident in the US. If you hold a Chinese Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID, Art.59 changes the picture materially — see China rules.

How to read the status column

Available

A bank will process it today against documents most people can actually produce.

Conditional / Limited

Lawful, but gated on a fact about you (a cancelled hukou, a specific property, a genuine trade) or capped at a trivial amount.

Not available / Not a route

Either no operative rule exists, or the thing is impossible for structural reasons — e.g. the money would have to leave the capital account first, which is the original problem.

Detail on the four that actually matter

C01 · Documented current-account remittance

The workhorse. Art.59 lets an overseas individual convert "legitimate current-account RMB income obtained in China" on presentation of passport + proof of the transaction amount + tax documents. Art.65 lets you remit it out on ID. No cap.

Why people fail: they show a bank balance instead of a transaction. A bank needs a source, not a sum.

Friction: a single payment over USD 50,000 also needs a 税务备案 filed with the tax bureau first — a separate building from the bank.

S03 →

C02 · Emigrant property transfer

The only route designed to move a whole pre-emigration lifetime abroad. Uncapped, but: requires a cancelled Chinese hukou, covers only property owned before you emigrated, requires the assets to be liquidated, and pays out 50% / half the remainder at 12 months / balance at 24 months.

The real cost isn't tax — it's that you take the exchange rate at three dates you don't control.

S04 →

C04 · Home-sale proceeds

A three-document checklist: application, sale contract, title-transfer tax clearance — reviewed by the SAFE branch where the property sits. Usually the fastest uncapped route, because Chinese IIT on the sale is withheld at the transaction.

The trap: your original purchase had to comply with the overseas-individual rules (in practice, having worked or studied in China for over a year). If it didn't, SAFE can refuse the remittance entirely.

S05 →

C20 · Do nothing

The benchmark every other option should beat. Zero cost, zero risk, keeps optionality — but the RMB exposure and the annual US reporting continue, and paying out over 24 months at unknown future rates may well be worse.

Note: if you have real China expenses (family, property, travel), spending the RMB in China — C09 — is genuinely free, and is the correct answer for that slice.

The uncomfortable finding If the balance cannot be tied to any permitted category — it is just an unexplained accumulation — then no lawful channel reaches it. Every route that appears to reach it is on the Do not do page, where the penalty is up to 100% of the amount.
Not advice. Bank discretion is real and codified: Art.68 of the 2020 Guidelines lets a bank refuse any transaction whose authenticity it doubts, and requires it to explain why. Nothing here guarantees a particular bank will accept a particular file. Confirm with the branch and, for anything material, with a Chinese-qualified lawyer and a US CPA.
