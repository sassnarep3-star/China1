# Source notes and adjudications

## 1. The USD 50,000 figure is not a universal exit cap

SAFE’s 2020 current-account guidelines distinguish the domestic-individual convenience purchase quota from an overseas individual’s documented lawful mainland current-account RMB income. The 2026 Tianjin FAQ repeats the Article 59 documentation rule. A US passport is relevant to classification, but habitual residence, permanent-residence status and the bank’s review still matter. Do not tell every US citizen that USD 50,000 is either a hard ceiling or an automatic entitlement.

Evidence: S02, S03, S04, S05.

## 2. Property transfers are narrow capital routes

Emigrant transfer requires the relevant pre-emigration property/history; inheritance transfer requires a qualifying inheritance. The 2026 Tianjin FAQs identify the competent former-hukou office and require direct remittance to the applicant’s own overseas account. The 2024 Beijing guide gives local documents and a 20-working-day complete-application period. The 2014 English circular allows lump sum or batches within the approval. The old staged 50%/25%/25% schedule is not published here as confirmed current law.

Evidence: S06–S10. Limitation: the full 2024 capital-guideline section 6.4 was not isolated.

## 3. House-sale proceeds are not generic savings

The Shanghai government-hosted BOC FAQ describes a nonresident seller’s documented sale/registration/tax path. It does not convert unrelated savings into house-sale proceeds. The STA filing threshold above USD 50,000 is procedural, not a 50,000-dollar tax band.

Evidence: S11, S12.

## 4. Deposit interest correction

The inherited root cost code says a CNY deposit coupon is taxed at a flat 20% and nets 80%. The Shanghai tax bureau’s full-text-effective republication of Caishui [2008] No.132 temporarily exempts savings-deposit interest accrued from 2008-10-09. The statutory 20% category remains relevant to other interest/dividend items, but the blanket deposit sentence is overbroad. US taxation is separate.

Evidence: S28, S29; audit A02.

## 5. Crypto authority correction

PBOC’s 2026 No.42 notice expressly repeals the 2021 No.237 notice. Earlier editions citing only No.237 are outdated as authority even where the practical prohibition remains. No CNY–USDT–USD workaround is recommended.

Evidence: S38; audit A03.

## 6. Commercial prices are not official regulatory rates

BOC and SC publish their own tariffs. They are provider-primary evidence, not government rates. BOC’s personal schedule is effective 2025-11-11; SC’s retrieved tariff is Version 2 dated 2026-07-26. Receiving-bank charges, FX execution and tier eligibility remain unknown. SC OUR does not guarantee every beneficiary-side deduction.

Evidence: S14–S19.

## 7. Closed-loop investment is not US repatriation

WMC southbound uses paired RMB/CIPS accounts and returns principal/proceeds to the mainland remittance account. QDII is an approved institutional product/custody channel. Stock Connect’s operator presentation describes a closed-loop trading link. None is a general route to deposit USD in a US account.

Evidence: S26, S27, S39, S40.

## 8. US reporting is not a conversion tax

US citizens generally report worldwide income. FBAR, Form 8938 and Form 3520 have different triggers. FTC relief is limited and cannot be stacked on excluded income. The treaty saving clause preserves US citizen taxation. Publication 525’s personal-FX USD 200 tip is official-search-excerpt evidence and is not used to compute this person’s gain. Form 720’s 1% remittance excise concerns certain physical-instrument-funded transfers after 2025, not all money leaving China.

Evidence: S30–S37.
