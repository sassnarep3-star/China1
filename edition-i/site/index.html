Getting money out of China: what it actually costs, and what is actually taxed

For a U.S. citizen holding CNY in a Chinese bank account, the conversion itself is not taxed in either country. The friction is (1) FX margin and wire fees, (2) Chinese income tax on the money if it was never taxed, and (3) U.S. filing duties (FBAR/8938/§988). The fastest lawful route depends on one question: how was the money earned and what documents prove it?

Bottom line: if the balance is documented (after-tax salary, service income, sale proceeds), use the matching documented route in the methods table — the paperwork is the product. If it is undocumented savings, no channel makes it tax-free; the quota route (USD 50,000/year) is the only simple one. Never split transfers across people or use crypto/underground banking: those are prohibited and carry criminal risk (S01 Art. 7, S08, S18).
