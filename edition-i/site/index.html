Edition I · 32 routes · 1,280 generated cost rows

Move CNY to USD lawfully, then compare the price.

A U.S. citizen with RMB in a Chinese bank is not stuck. The binding question is what the RMB is — documented current income, pre-emigration capital, an inheritance, a property sale or a business distribution — because that decides which rule applies. Only after that does the wire fee matter.

Bottom line. No exact personal total can be certified from the facts supplied, and none is invented here. What can be certified is the shape of the cost: a lawful bank route costs about 0.23%-0.53% of principal in published sender-side charges. The tax depends on the source event. Evasion, illegal FX purchase and private FX trading risk fines of 30%-100% of the amount; illegal cash carriage risks up to 20%.

The decision in six lines

What the evidence supportsSource
The conversion itself is not a tax event
China taxes the source event (wages, property gain, dividend). The U.S. taxes you on worldwide income. A transfer fee is not a tax, and a tax is not a transfer fee. Treating the two as one 'penalty' is the core error in most online guides.		S15 ↗S20 ↗
Overseas individuals have no published USD 50,000 purchase cap
The 50,000 figure is an annual facilitation quota for individual settlement and for domestic individuals' purchases. SAFE policy answers state that an overseas individual's purchase has no annual cap, but requires identity, source-of-funds and tax evidence.		S02 ↗S03 ↗S05 ↗
The published bank cost is roughly 0.31% plus about USD 22
On the 2026-11-10 board, the one-way FX margin is 0.2104%, commission is 0.1% (min CNY 50, max CNY 260) and the cable charge is CNY 150. At USD 10,000 that is about 0.53% all-in on the sender side; at USD 400,000 about 0.23%.		S08 ↗S09 ↗
The expensive part is the source event, not the wire
Property gains carry 20% IIT, FIE dividends now carry 20% (the old exemption was repealed from 2026-09-01), and undeclared income carries U.S. tax plus reporting penalties. No bank fee comes close.		S15 ↗S16 ↗S22 ↗
Illegal routes risk 30%-100% of the amount
Evasion, illegal FX purchase and private FX trading carry fines up to 30% of the amount, rising to 30%-100% when serious, plus confiscation. That is the only 'penalty' that can exceed the tax.		S27 ↗
Reporting duties survive the transfer
FBAR above USD 10,000 aggregate, Form 8938 above the applicable threshold, Form 3520 above USD 100,000 of foreign gifts or bequests. None of these depends on the money being taxable.		S21 ↗S22 ↗S29 ↗

Start here: what is the RMB?

Salary or fees earned in China

Use the current-account route. An overseas individual needs identity, source documentation and tax evidence; there is no published annual purchase cap. P01 →

Money you owned before emigrating

Use the emigrant property transfer: SAFE approval at the original household-registration location, then a bank purchase and remittance to your own account. Staging was cancelled in 2014. C01 →

An inheritance

Use the inheritance transfer with succession documents and the deceased's tax clearance. C02 →

Property you are selling

An overseas individual needs SAFE approval for the proceeds, and China taxes the gain at 20% (or 1% deemed). C03 →

A business distribution

Genuine FIE profits are distributable, but foreign individuals now face 20% withholding. C05 →

Nothing fits

Then hold and document first. Converting without a lawful basis is where the 30%-100% penalties live. See what not to do →

What this edition will not do

  • It will not state your tax, because your basis, residency, source and compliance history are unknown.
  • It will not invent a receiving-bank or correspondent fee.
  • It will not give execution steps for crypto, underground banks, quota splitting or false documents.
  • It will not re-certify the other editions line by line. It crawls them mechanically and flags what it finds.
