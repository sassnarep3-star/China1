1. Reframe the problem

The PRC Individual Income Tax Law lists nine taxable income categories; converting one's own principal is not one of them. US §988(e) excludes a personal FX gain up to USD 200. So the cost being described is almost always one of three different things: (a) tax on the income that created the balance, (b) a bank requirement to produce tax vouchers before selling FX, or (c) the bid/ask spread and wire tariff. Those are not a conversion tax.

2. The legal fork that decides everything

个人外汇管理办法 Art. 3: current-account individual FX is administered on the convertibility principle; capital-account individual FX is administered "according to the process of convertibility". Parking savings abroad is a capital-account act. The USD 50,000 figure is an annual purchase convenience quota for 境内个人, while USD 50,000/day is the remittance threshold for FX already held. Those two are routinely conflated.
