Executive summary

Question

Money is stuck in a Chinese bank account as CNY. What is the best way to convert and move it to USD for a U.S. citizen, with the least tax penalty? What does it cost at each size from $10,000 to $400,000?

Answer in five points

  1. There is no conversion tax in either country. China's IIT Law taxes nine income categories — currency exchange is not one (S09 Art. 2). The U.S. exempts personal-transaction FX gains of $200 or less (26 U.S.C. §988(e)(2), S14) and taxes larger gains as ordinary income. Fees are not taxes; the conversion cost is FX margin + bank fees.
  2. The real tax question is the source of the money. If it is salary or service income already taxed in China, the documented-income route (M01/M03) moves it with tax vouchers and adds no new Chinese tax; claim the U.S. foreign tax credit (Form 1116, S15) so it is not taxed twice. If it was never taxed, no channel legalizes it — the tax on the underlying income (3–45% comprehensive, 20% flat on some categories, S09) is the cost to make it movable.
  3. Least-friction routes by source: after-tax salary/income → documented income wire (no per-year USD ceiling stated for foreign individuals, S01/S02 Art. 13); routine savings of a mainland-ID holder → the $50,000/year quota (S02 Art. 2); sold property → property route with tax voucher (S05); pre-emigration assets or inheritance → SAFE-approved transfer (S03/S04); real business receipts → trade settlement account (no quota, S02 Art. 9).
  4. Published cost of the wire mechanics: at the 2026-11-10 07:32:39 BOC board (S07), moving $400,000 as one wire costs $903.07 in verified components (0.2258%: FX margin 0.2104% + capped ¥260 handling + ¥150 cable); as eight $50,000 quota wires it costs $1,332.05 (0.3330%). Receiving-bank fees, intermediaries and taxes are unverified and shown blank (F03–F05).
  5. Avoid every shortcut. Splitting transfers across relatives, underground banking, false trade papers and crypto cash-out are prohibited (S01 Art. 7, S08, S21 — the 2021 crypto notice S18 was expressly repealed by Yin Fa [2026] No. 42 and the ban continues under it) with criminal exposure. The penalties that actually get charged in practice are filing penalties: FBAR up to $10,000 (non-willful) or 50% of balances (willful), Form 8938 up to $10,000 + $10,000/30 days capped at $60,000 (S12).
