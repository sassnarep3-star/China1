Every method found — with pros and cons

Lawful routes are conditional entitlements: the rule text allows them when the facts fit. Prohibited routes are listed with risk information only and no execution steps. Every row links the official source it was verified against.

Classification matters: 境内个人 (mainland-ID holder) and 境外个人 (everyone else) follow different rules (S01 Art. 37). A U.S. citizen is a 境外个人 unless they also hold a PRC resident ID. The bank makes this call at account level — confirm it first (F01).

Pros and cons in detail

M01 — Documented current-account income wire (foreign individual)

Documents: Passport/ID; employment or service contract; income evidence; Chinese tax payment voucher (税务凭证); remittance application; receiving-account details.

Pros
  • ✅ No published per-year USD ceiling in the rule text (unlike the domestic-individual quota).
  • ✅ Tax already paid on the income means the conversion itself adds no Chinese tax (S09 Art. 2 — conversion is not a taxable category).
  • ✅ One documented route can move the whole balance in one or several wires.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Requires a genuine documented income trail with tax vouchers; undocumented savings do not qualify.
  • ⚠️ Bank has authenticity-review discretion; processing may take longer for large amounts.
  • ⚠️ Wire fees and FX margin apply at every transfer (see ladder L1).

M02 — Annual USD 50,000 convenience quota (domestic individual)

Documents: Valid ID. (Above-quota amounts need transaction evidence — see M03.)

Pros
  • ✅ Simplest lawful channel: ID only, no income documents within the quota.
  • ✅ Cumulative quota across banks is tracked through the SAFE personal FX system (S02 Art. 32–36).
Cons
  • ⚠️ Hard ceiling of USD 50,000 per calendar year per person — USD 400,000 needs 8 years (or more persons, which is prohibited structuring if organized to evade limits — see P01).
  • ⚠️ Uses of purchased FX cannot include overseas property, securities or insurance (S08).
  • ⚠️ Each wire carries its own fee and cable charge (ladder L2 shows chunked costs).

M03 — Above-quota documented current-account purchase and remittance (domestic individual)

Documents: ID plus the Art. 10 evidence for the income type (e.g. employment contract + income proof + tax records), and transaction-amount proof for the purchase.

Pros
  • ✅ Removes the USD 50,000 ceiling for genuine documented current-account income.
  • ✅ Same bank-wire mechanics and published tariff as the quota route.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Document assembly is heavier; banks scrutinize large amounts.
  • ⚠️ Chinese income tax compliance on the underlying income is expected; unpaid-tax situations are not cured by paperwork.

M04 — Mainland property sale proceeds repatriation

Documents: Passport; commodity-house transfer contract and registration certificate; tax-payment voucher from the local tax authority; Tax Filing Form for External Payments when the transaction exceeds USD 50,000; notarized power of attorney if an agent acts.

Pros
  • ✅ Explicitly lawful channel with a published step list.
  • ✅ S03 Section VI removed mandatory staged remittance and document notarization requirements.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Chinese tax on the sale must be settled first and evidenced (S05 steps 3–4).
  • ⚠️ Tax amount depends on facts (holding period, cost basis, local practice) — left blank (flag F05).
  • ⚠️ Bank and SAFE review of paperwork; timelines vary.

M05 — Emigration property transfer (移民财产转移)

Documents: Identity + emigration-status documents (e.g. household-registration cancellation proof per S04); property-rights evidence; lawful-source evidence; SAFE approval document; bank remittance papers.

Pros
  • ✅ Designed for exactly this use: converting pre-emigration assets and sending them abroad.
  • ✅ 2014 reforms made it faster (no forced batches, no HQ filing, no notarization).
Cons
  • ⚠️ Requires formal SAFE approval; property must be lawful, undisputed and free of judicial restriction (S04).
  • ⚠️ 2004 text on staged remittance (Art. 6) conflicts with S03 Section VI — implementation varies by office (flag F09).
  • ⚠️ Approval and document work are slow; professional help is often needed (cost unverifiable — blank).

M06 — Inheritance property transfer (继承财产转移)

Documents: Inheritance legal documents or notarized instruments (S02 Art. 10(3)); identity; property evidence; SAFE approval; bank papers.

Pros
  • ✅ Lawful capital route for heirs with a genuine estate.

  • ⚠️ SAFE approval required; estate must be lawful and undisputed.
  • ⚠️ Estate tax and income-tax treatment in China and the U.S. depends on facts (blank; flag F05/F01).
  • ⚠️ Cross-border estate administration is document-heavy.

M07 — Personal foreign-trade settlement account (个人对外贸易经营者)

Documents: Business registration / trade-operator filing; trade contracts; customs declarations or, for agency exports, agency contracts + logistics documents (S02 Art. 9(2) as amended by Huifa [2016] No. 13); invoices.

Pros
  • ✅ No annual quota ceiling for genuine trade flows.
  • ✅ Suited to actual exporters/importers with trade documents.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Only for real trade — using it to relabel savings is false-documenting (P03).
  • ⚠️ Institutional-account bank pricing is not the personal e-banking tariff; modeled costs are flagged (F13).
  • ⚠️ Requires ongoing compliance (registration, declarations, verification).

M08 — Family support, gifts and other documented inbound transfers

Documents: Notarized gift agreement or kinship proof; donor's income evidence (bank deposit certificate, personal income tax voucher); IDs of both parties.

Pros
  • ✅ Lawful for genuine family flows.
  • ✅ Can supplement the annual quota with documented above-quota settlement.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Genuine family relationship and donor income must be evidenced.
  • ⚠️ Gifts must comply with national rules (S02 Art. 10(1)).
  • ⚠️ Not a device to move the user's own savings — that is structuring (P01).

M09 — Wise from-CNY transfer (licensed-provider route)

Documents: Wise verification (1–2 working days first time); China tax records within the last 3 years; Chinese bank account or Alipay; destination account the sender owns.

Pros
  • ✅ Digital onboarding; no counter visit.
  • ✅ Overall limit tied to documented after-tax salary — can exceed the bank quota route for eligible earners.
  • ✅ Funds arrive in an account you own abroad.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Eligibility excludes mainland-Chinese senders and anyone without recent China tax records.
  • ⚠️ No fixed published fee table — exact fees appear only at transfer setup (S17); all fee cells blank (F07).
  • ⚠️ Single-transfer caps force multiple transfers at larger amounts.

M11 — Physical cash carrying with declaration and permits

Documents: Customs declaration form; bank or SAFE issued 携带证 (FX Carrying Permit) when applicable; ID.

Pros
  • ✅ No wire or correspondent fees.
  • ✅ Simple for small amounts within the permit-free band.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Strict hard caps; over-limit cash without permits is returned or confiscated with fines (S19).
  • ⚠️ Insurance, security and travel costs are unpriced here (blank).

  • ⚠️ Cash still needs lawful-source answers on both sides of the border.

M12 — Company profit/dividend remittance from a foreign-invested enterprise

Documents: Board/partner profit-distribution resolution; tax-filing form (tax authority); company audit basis for distributable profit; shareholder identity.

Pros
  • ✅ Explicitly facilitated channel for genuine distributions (2014 reform).
  • ✅ Above USD 50,000 the document list is short and fixed.
Cons
  • ⚠️ Requires a real profitable company with declared dividends; it cannot relabel personal savings.

M14 — Unused-RMB re-conversion on original exchange slip (foreign individual)
