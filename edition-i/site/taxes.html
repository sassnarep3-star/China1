Tax and reporting, separated

Two systems apply at once, and neither is a conversion tax. China taxes the event that created the money. The United States taxes you on worldwide income and on currency movements. Reporting duties exist even when nothing is taxable.

No personal tax figure is shown. Without residency, basis, acquisition dates, source documents and compliance history, any number would be fabricated. What is shown is the rule and the rate, with the official source.

The order of operations

  1. Tax attaches to the event, not to the wire.
  2. China taxes first at the source event: wages 3-45%, asset gains 20%, dividends 20%.
  3. The U.S. then taxes the person on worldwide income; the foreign tax credit reduces double taxation but is limited.
  4. Section 988 can add ordinary income purely from RMB appreciation, even when the principal was already taxed.
  5. Reporting penalties (FBAR, 8938, 3520) are fixed-dollar exposures that have nothing to do with the amount converted.

Three mistakes this page is designed to prevent

"The conversion is taxed at X%"

There is no such rate. China taxes wages, gains and dividends; the U.S. taxes income and section 988 currency gain. A wire fee is a fee.

"I already paid tax in China, so the U.S. is settled"

The foreign tax credit is real but limited. It is claimed on Form 1116 and can leave residual U.S. tax, especially across baskets.

"If I move it, reporting ends"

FBAR and 8938 look at the year's maximum balances. Moving money after 31 December does not undo a reporting requirement for that year.
